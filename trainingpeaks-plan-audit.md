## 2. Periodisation Logic Extracted

### 2.1 Macro-Level Periodisation

All plans follow a consistent periodisation philosophy:

**16-week plans (Gravel, Road Race, Sportive):**
- **Structure:** 4 mesocycles of 3 build weeks + 1 recovery week (3:1 ratio)
- **Progressive overload:** Each mesocycle peaks higher than the previous one
- **Taper:** Final week (week 16) drops dramatically to ~5 hours — classic 1-week taper before a target event
- **Volume range:** Builds from ~7-8 hrs in week 1 up to ~10-11 hrs at peak, then tapers to ~5 hrs
- **Pattern:** Wk1 ~8h → Wk2 ~9h → Wk3 ~9.5h → Wk4 (recovery) ~7h → repeat with higher ceiling → Wk16 taper ~5h

**12-week plans (Base, Build):**
- **Structure:** 3 mesocycles of 3 build + 1 recovery (3:1 ratio)
- **Progressive overload:** Same escalating pattern
- **No taper:** These are phase plans (base or build), not event-specific — they end at peak volume, ready to feed into a race-specific block
- **Volume range:** Builds from ~8 hrs to ~11 hrs

**6-week plans (VO2 Max Booster, FTP Booster):**
- **Structure:** Linear build over 5 weeks, 1 recovery/absorption week
- **No formal mesocycle breaks** — too short for 3:1 structure
- **Volume range:** Starts ~7 hrs, builds to ~10.5 hrs, drops to ~7 hrs for recovery

### 5.2 Key Architectural Decisions Needed

1. **S&C integration:** Should S&C sessions replace bike sessions or be additional? Given the "no heavy compound lifts" rule for this demographic, S&C should focus on: single-leg strength (lunges, step-ups, single-leg deadlifts), core stability, hip mobility, and injury prevention. Recommend 2x per week, integrated into the plan rather than bolted on.

2. **How volume tiers scale:** The primary lever for scaling from 6 to 12 hrs/week should be:
   - **Long ride duration** (the Saturday ride scales most — from 2:00 at 6 hrs to 4:00+ at 12 hrs)
   - **Weekday ride duration** (scale from 0:45-1:00 at 6 hrs to 1:30-2:00 at 12 hrs)
   - **Number of sessions should stay at 5 bike + 2 S&C** across all tiers — changing the number of sessions breaks people's schedules

3. **How experience levels differentiate:**
   - **Beginner:** Simpler intervals (fewer types), longer warm-ups, more Z2, no complex cadence drills, 2:1 build-to-recovery ratio instead of 3:1
   - **Intermediate:** Current structure (3:1, polarised, moderate workout variety)
   - **Advanced:** More workout variety, race-specific sessions (crit simulations, climbing intervals), potentially 3:1:1 pattern (3 build, 1 recovery, 1 test week)

4. **Pricing on TrainingPeaks:** The plans should NOT be listed as FREE on TrainingPeaks if they're part of the $297-397 Roadman Method course. Options:
   - Gate behind course enrollment (distribute via TrainingPeaks coach account, not public marketplace)
   - Price individually on TrainingPeaks marketplace as premium plans ($49-79 each)
   - Keep 1-2 free plans as lead magnets, gate the rest

5. **Plan descriptions:** Every plan needs a compelling description for SEO and conversion. This is low-hanging fruit.

### 5.3 Periodisation Template for 12-Week Roadman Method Plans

Based on the existing logic, the standard 12-week Roadman Method plan should follow:

```
Week 1-3:   Base/Build Mesocycle 1 (3:1 → recovery week 4 is absorbed into next block)
Week 4:     Recovery / Adaptation Week
Week 5-7:   Build Mesocycle 2 (progressive overload)
Week 8:     Recovery / Adaptation Week
Week 9-11:  Race-Specific / Peak Mesocycle (highest volume + specificity)
Week 12:    Taper Week (50% volume reduction)
```

**Gran Fondo variant:** Emphasise long ride progression, climbing endurance, pacing strategy
**Racing variant:** Emphasise threshold power, VO2 max, race-specific intervals (crits, attacks)
**General Fitness variant:** Balanced approach, body composition focus, sustainable intensity

### 5.4 S&C Session Template (Roadman Method Appropriate)

Since heavy compound lifts (deadlifts, squats, barbell rows) are excluded for injury risk in the 35-55 demographic, the S&C component should include:

**Recommended 2x/week S&C sessions (30-40 min each):**

Session A — Lower Body + Core:
- Single-leg Romanian deadlifts (bodyweight or light DB)
- Walking lunges
- Step-ups (moderate height)
- Glute bridges / hip thrusts
- Side-lying clam shells
- Plank variations (front, side)
- Dead bugs

Session B — Upper Body + Mobility:
- Push-ups (modified if needed)
- Band pull-aparts
- Shoulder stability (Y-T-W raises)
- Thoracic spine rotation
- Hip flexor stretches
- Foam rolling protocol
- Single-leg balance work

These should be scheduled on Tuesday and Thursday (pre-ride or evening) to complement the cycling sessions.
