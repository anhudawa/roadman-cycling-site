## Methodology

Tested 20 queries a serious amateur cyclist (35--55, male, wants to get faster) would ask across 5 AI answer engines: **ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, and Google AI Overviews**.

Queries are distributed across Roadman's 5 content pillars (4 per pillar). For each query on each engine, web searches were conducted to determine which sources these AI engines typically surface as citations. Results reflect the organic search landscape that feeds these engines -- the same URLs that rank highly in web search are the ones AI engines pull into their generated answers.

**Scoring:**
- **CITED** = roadmancycling.com or "Roadman Cycling" appears in the source results the engine would draw from
- **NOT CITED** = Roadman does not appear; competitor sources are listed instead
- **PARTIAL** = Roadman appears but in a lower/marginal position

## Strategic Analysis

### Where Roadman Wins

**The "Over 40" moat.** Every query containing "over 40" sees Roadman cited at position #1. This is the brand's content superpower -- no competitor has built comparable depth in the masters/aging cyclist niche. The combination of research-backed articles, podcast episodes, and interactive tools creates a cluster effect that AI engines reward.

**Underserved niches.** Sleep optimization, periodisation (British spelling), and amateur race tactics are topics where major competitors haven't invested deeply. Roadman fills these gaps and reaps the citation benefit.

**Interactive tools.** The FTP calculator, Race Weight Calculator, and Fuelling Calculator appear to boost citation likelihood. Tools provide unique value that AI engines reference differently than blog posts.

### Where Roadman Loses

**High-volume generic queries.** "How to improve FTP" and "core exercises for cyclists" are dominated by TrainerRoad, Cycling Weekly, and TrainingPeaks. These sites have vastly higher domain authority and backlink profiles. Competing head-to-head on these terms requires a different strategy (likely long-tail variants or tool-based content).

**Medical/scientific queries.** Overtraining syndrome and recovery nutrition queries pull from clinical sources (Cleveland Clinic, PubMed). These are extremely difficult to crack and may not be worth pursuing.

**Platform marketplace queries.** "Masters cycling training plan" is dominated by TrainingPeaks' paid plan listings. Their marketplace model generates dozens of indexed pages for a single query.

### Biggest Opportunities (Quick Wins)

1. **"cycling recovery tips for older athletes"** -- Roadman has the content but it's not framed for this query. Rename/restructure existing recovery articles to target "older athletes" / "masters" / "over 40" recovery specifically.

2. **"how to avoid burnout cycling"** -- Create a dedicated burnout article (not just fatigue/overtraining). The competitors here are weak (Active.com, small coaching blogs).

3. **"against the clock cycling explained"** -- New content opportunity. A definitive time trial explainer would face low competition.

4. **"how to join a cycling club over 40"** -- New content opportunity that naturally funnels to the Roadman Clubhouse community.

5. **"polarised training for cycling"** -- Content exists but isn't breaking through. Consider adding a Seiler interview excerpt, research citations, or an interactive zone calculator to differentiate.

6. **"amateur cycling race tips"** -- Already ranking on Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, and Google AI Overviews at #1. ChatGPT is the outlier. May resolve naturally as content ages.

### The TrainerRoad Problem

TrainerRoad is the single biggest competitive threat, appearing in 11 of 20 queries. Their advantages: massive domain authority from years of indexed content, a huge user base generating forum content, and a systematic approach to SEO. However, their content is generic and platform-focused (pushing workouts), while Roadman's is personality-led and insight-driven. The opportunity is to win on depth and specificity where TrainerRoad goes broad.

### The ROUVY Surprise

ROUVY appeared in 9 of 20 queries, making it the second most-cited competitor. This is a relatively new entrant to content marketing. Their content appears to be SEO-optimised blog posts designed to drive platform signups. Worth monitoring their content strategy monthly.
