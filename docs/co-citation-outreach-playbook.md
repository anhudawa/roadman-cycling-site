## The strategy in one paragraph

Every week, dozens of articles across cycling media, triathlon sites, health publications, and sports-science blogs cite experts who have appeared on the Roadman Cycling Podcast — Stephen Seiler, Dan Lorang, Joe Friel, Greg LeMond, Dan Bigham, Tim Spector, and many others. Most of those articles cite the expert but don't link to the primary-source conversation where the expert explained their ideas in detail. Our job is to find those articles and pitch the Roadman episode or blog page as the definitive primary source their readers should see. The angle is genuinely helpful — we are giving them a better resource to link to, not asking for a favour.

## 2. Outreach email templates

### Ground rules for all templates

- Subject line under 50 characters
- No attachments — link to the episode page
- No "I hope this finds you well"
- One link, max two
- Personalise the first sentence to the specific article you found
- Send from anthony@roadmancycling.com
- UTM pattern: `?utm_source=outreach&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=co-citation-2026`

---

### Template A — "The primary source you're missing"

Best for: articles that cite a Roadman guest by name but link to a generic Wikipedia page or no source at all.

**Subject:** Primary source for your [Expert] piece

Hi [first name],

Your piece on [specific article title or topic] mentions [Expert Name]'s work on [specific insight — e.g. "the 80/20 intensity distribution model"]. Good call — it's one of the most misunderstood ideas in training science.

[Expert] sat down with us for [duration] and explained [specific point from the episode that the article touches on] in detail — including [one concrete detail the article doesn't have, e.g. "how amateurs should actually structure their Zone 2 weeks, which is different from how elites do it"].

Full conversation: [episode URL with UTM]

If you think it adds value for your readers as a primary source, it'd be a natural fit in your [Expert] reference. Either way, solid piece.

Anthony Walsh
Roadman Cycling Podcast — roadmancycling.com

---

### Template B — "Your readers are asking about this"

Best for: articles that discuss a topic a Roadman guest is the world authority on, but don't mention the guest by name.

**Subject:** Relevant for your [topic] readers

Hi [first name],

I came across your piece on [topic — e.g. "time trial aerodynamics for amateur cyclists"]. The detail on [specific aspect they covered well] was particularly good.

We recently recorded a conversation with [Expert Name] — [one-line credential, e.g. "the engineer who held the UCI Hour Record and now leads aero at Red Bull–Bora–Hansgrohe"] — where [they] walked through [the specific aspect the article covers] from first principles. [One concrete takeaway from the episode, e.g. "Their hierarchy of gains — position first, clothing second, helmet third — surprised even our coaching roster."]

Here's the episode: [episode URL with UTM]

Might be useful as a resource link for readers who want to go deeper. No pressure either way.

Anthony
roadmancycling.com

---

### Template C — "We covered the same ground — here's the long version"

Best for: listicles, round-ups, or "top tips" posts that summarise expert advice briefly. Works especially well when the article compresses a complex idea into a bullet point.

**Subject:** The long version of [Expert]'s point

Hi [first name],

Read your [article title/description]. The section on [Expert Name]'s [specific point — e.g. "periodisation framework for masters cyclists"] caught my eye because we spent [X minutes] unpacking exactly that with [Expert] on the podcast.

The short version: [one-sentence summary of the key insight]. The long version is a [duration] conversation where [Expert] builds the logic from scratch — why it works, who it works for, and what most people get wrong when they try to apply it.

Transcript and audio: [episode URL with UTM]

If it's useful as a "go deeper" link for that section, happy to have it there. If not, keep writing — it's good stuff.

Anthony Walsh
roadmancycling.com

---

### Follow-up template (use at day 4)

**Subject:** Re: [original subject]

Hi [first name],

Just floating this back up — no worries if it doesn't fit. The [Expert] episode is here if you want to take a look: [URL]

Anthony

---

### Follow-up cadence

- Day 0: Initial pitch (Template A, B, or C)
- Day 4: Follow-up (the short template above)
- Day 11: Final follow-up — one line: "Last nudge on this — here's the link if useful: [URL]. Thanks for reading either way."
- After three touches: archive. Do not follow up again.

## 3. Sarah's weekly workflow (2–3 hours)

### Monday block — 90 minutes: Search and qualify

**Step 1: Run the Tier 1 searches (45 min)**

Open Google and run the search queries for each Tier 1 guest (the first 10 in the guest list). For each query:

1. Open the first 2 pages of results
2. Scan for articles that either:
   - Mention the expert by name but don't link to Roadman
   - Discuss the expert's core ideas without citing a primary source
   - Link to a weaker source (Wikipedia, a short podcast mention) when the Roadman episode is more comprehensive
3. Skip anything that already links to roadmancycling.com
4. Skip forums, Reddit threads, and social media posts — only pitch editorial sites with a contact path

**Step 2: Evaluate prospects (30 min)**

For each article found, check three things:

1. **Authority check:** Is this a real publication or a thin affiliate site? Quick test: does the site have an About page, a named editorial team, and content published in the last 6 months? If yes, proceed. If it looks like a content farm, skip.
2. **Relevance check:** Does the article genuinely relate to something the Roadman episode covers in depth? The pitch has to be helpful, not forced.
3. **Contact check:** Can you find an author email, a general editorial contact, or a contact form? LinkedIn is a fallback. If there's no way to reach them, skip.

**Qualification filter — a prospect is worth pitching if:**
- The site has a Domain Authority/Rating of 30+ (check in Ahrefs if available; otherwise, use the editorial-quality gut check above)
- The article has been published within the last 12 months (fresher = more likely the author still manages the page)
- The Roadman episode genuinely adds value — a longer, deeper, primary-source version of what the article summarises

**Step 3: Log in the tracker (15 min)**

Add each qualified prospect to the outreach tracker spreadsheet (template below).

### Wednesday or Thursday block — 60 minutes: Personalise and send

1. Open the tracker and work through the "Ready to pitch" column
2. For each prospect:
   - Re-read the article (or at least the section that cites the expert)
   - Pick Template A, B, or C based on the scenario
   - Personalise the first sentence, the expert detail, and the episode link
   - Add the UTM-tagged URL
   - Send from anthony@roadmancycling.com
3. Move the prospect to "Pitched" in the tracker and log the date

**Target:** 5–8 personalised pitches per week. Quality over volume.

### Friday — 15 minutes: Follow-ups and tracking

1. Check the tracker for any prospects at the 4-day or 11-day follow-up mark
2. Send the follow-up templates
3. Check GA4 for any traffic from `utm_campaign=co-citation-2026`
4. If any replies have come in, log the outcome (linked / declined / no response)

### Fortnightly: Tier 2 search cycle

Every other Monday, replace the Tier 1 search block with Tier 2 guests (11–20). Same process — search, qualify, log, pitch.

### Monthly: Tier 3 search cycle + review

First Monday of each month, run the Tier 3 searches (21–30). Also run a monthly review:

1. How many pitches sent this month?
2. How many replies received?
3. How many links placed?
4. How many new referring domains in Ahrefs?
5. Which guests are generating the most prospects?
6. Adjust search queries if any are returning stale results

1. Total referring domains (Ahrefs Site Explorer)
2. Referring domains from DR 50+ sites
3. Domain Rating of roadmancycling.com
4. Current ranking positions for 10 target keywords across training and nutrition clusters
5. Monthly organic traffic to the episode and blog pages being pitched

Record these in a "Baseline — [date]" row in the tracker spreadsheet. Re-pull monthly.

### Attribution via UTM tags

Every link pitched should include the UTM campaign tag. The `llms.txt` already deploys UTM tracking for AI crawlers. The outreach UTMs create a parallel attribution channel:

- **Pattern:** `?utm_source=outreach&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=co-citation-2026`
- **Per-guest variant (optional):** `&utm_content=stephen-seiler` — useful for identifying which guests generate the most link placements

GA4 will surface these in Acquisition → Traffic Acquisition → Campaign. Filter by `co-citation-2026` to see all outreach-attributed traffic in one view.

## 7. Operational notes

### What not to do

- Do not pitch the same site more than once per quarter for the same article — if they said no or didn't respond, respect it
- Do not pitch sites that already link to roadmancycling.com — they are already in the network
- Do not pitch thin content (forum posts, AI-generated listicles, social media) — only editorial sites with real audiences
- Do not open with "I hope this finds you well" or "I'm reaching out because"
- Do not attach files — always link to the Roadman page
- Do not pitch without personalising — generic pitches get archived

### What to do when a site says no

Reply: "Understood, thanks for reading — if the angle changes or you cover [expert] again, the conversation is there. Cheers, Anthony." That keeps the door open without being pushy. Log as "Declined" and do not follow up again on this article.

### Expanding the guest list

As new episodes drop with high-profile guests, add them to the appropriate tier. The test for inclusion: "Is this person cited by other publications? Would an article about their topic benefit from linking to the full conversation?" If yes, add them with search queries and the episode URL.

### Coordinating with other outreach

This co-citation system runs alongside the existing outreach plays documented in `docs/seo/outreach-templates.md` (podcast guesting, guest posts, FTP Benchmarks digital PR). They don't conflict — co-citation targets articles that already exist, while guest posts and podcast guesting create new content. Sarah should track all three in the same spreadsheet but tag them by campaign:
- `co-citation-2026` — this playbook
- `podcast-guesting-2026` — Anthony appearing on other shows
- `guest-post-2026` — contributed articles
- `digital-pr-ftp-2026` — FTP Benchmarks asset pitching

### Scaling with AI search

As AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Claude) increasingly cite sources, every new referring domain improves Roadman's entity authority and citation probability. The `llms.txt` and AI-crawler allowlist already ensure that placed links are crawlable by AI systems. This outreach campaign is not just for traditional SEO — it feeds the AEO engine simultaneously.
