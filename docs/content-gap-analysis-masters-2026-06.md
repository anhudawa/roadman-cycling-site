# Content Gap Analysis — Masters Cycling (June 2026)

Companion to the June 2026 internal-linking sweep. The linking pass closed
every orphaned and weakly-linked blog post (16 → 0 weakly-linked, 0
orphans). This document covers the other half of the brief: where the
content itself is missing.

## Method

Gaps were identified against the five Roadman content pillars — **Coaching,
Nutrition, Strength & Conditioning, Recovery, Le Métier** — and filtered to
the audience that converts best for us: the serious masters amateur (the
"Not Done Yet" rider, 40–60, training hard, refusing to accept decline).

Every proposed topic was checked against the existing catalogue so we don't
duplicate what's already published. The five June-10 cluster hubs
(`/masters/vo2max`, `/training/zone-2`, `/training/reverse-periodisation`,
`/training/indoor`, `/nutrition/masters`) already own the core masters
training and nutrition ground, so these gaps deliberately sit in the
adjacent space those hubs *don't* cover: racing, sleep, fit, heat
tolerance, alcohol, group craft, detraining, and mobility.

The differentiator on every one of these is the same as the rest of the
site — a named expert from the podcast archive saying something specific,
not generic health content.

## The Gaps (8 briefs)

### 1. Racing at 50+: How to Actually Be Competitive in Masters Racing
- **Pillar:** Coaching / Le Métier
- **Target keyword:** `masters cycling racing` (secondary: `racing after 50`, `masters crit racing`, `how to race masters cycling`)
- **Why it's a gap:** We have masters *training* covered deeply, and we have racing-adjacent pieces (`road-racing-decline-cycling-future-amateur`, `masters-racing-doping-cycling-amateur-cheating`, the Cory Williams criterium piece), but **nothing on how to race well as an older rider** — the tactics, category realities, and the experience-over-watts edge. The masters-racing podcast material is host-led, which is a clean fit for a Walsh-voice how-to.
- **Angle:** The masters racer's advantage isn't power, it's race-craft — positioning, patience, reading the move, spending matches once not ten times. What changes after 50 (recovery between rounds, needing to peak fewer times) and how to train to race rather than just ride. Honest on category/sandbagging.
- **Suggested experts:** Cory Williams (criterium tactics), Anthony Walsh (former elite, masters perspective).
- **Internal links:** `/masters/vo2max`, `cory-williams-sprint-power-vs-winning-power`, `masters-cycling-training-plan-over-40`, `cycling-taper-race-preparation-system`.
- **Word count:** ~2,200

### 2. Sleep and the Masters Cyclist: Why Recovery Gets Harder After 40
- **Pillar:** Recovery
- **Target keyword:** `sleep masters cyclist` (secondary: `sleep older athletes recovery`, `deep sleep growth hormone cycling`, `sleep cycling performance over 40`)
- **Why it's a gap:** We have general sleep pieces (`cycling-sleep-optimisation`, `cycling-sleep-performance-guide`) but neither addresses the **age-specific** problem: deep (slow-wave) sleep — where most growth-hormone release and physical repair happen — declines sharply from the 40s, which is a direct mechanism behind the longer masters recovery window.
- **Angle:** The reason a 50-year-old's hard session takes 72 hours to absorb instead of 48 is partly sleep architecture, not just muscle. What changes (slow-wave decline, fragmentation, earlier circadian shift), what's controllable, and a masters sleep protocol that protects the deep stages. Pairs with the training-load and recovery-window work.
- **Suggested experts:** Dr Andy Galpin (recovery-as-adaptation), James Nestor (breathing/sleep), Joe Friel (Fast After 50 recovery).
- **Internal links:** `/masters/vo2max`, `training-load-management-cyclists-40s-50s`, `post-ride-recovery-window-cyclists-over-40`, `masters-recovery-audit-seven-things-to-check`.
- **Word count:** ~2,000

### 3. Bike Fit After 40: Why Your Position Has to Change as You Age
- **Pillar:** Strength & Conditioning
- **Target keyword:** `bike fit masters cyclist` (secondary: `bike fit older riders`, `bike fit flexibility age`, `cycling position after 40`)
- **Why it's a gap:** General fit content exists (`bike-fit-guide-cyclists`, `numb-hands-cycling-5-fixes-bike-fit`, `cycling-knee-pain-causes-fixes`), but none covers the **ageing-rider fit problem** Dr Andy Pruitt is explicit about on the podcast: flexibility drops, feet flatten, and the aggressive position that worked at 35 starts causing back, neck and hand pain at 50 — so fit is "not one-and-done," it needs a yearly look.
- **Angle:** Your fit should move as your body does. The ~45° torso sweet spot, raising the front end without losing power, why a once-a-year refit beats chasing aero you can no longer hold, and the comfort-equals-speed argument for masters riders. Direct tie to the existing `dr-andy-pruitt` expert page.
- **Suggested experts:** Dr Andy Pruitt (Body Geometry / Boulder Center), Phil Burt (former British Cycling physio).
- **Internal links:** `/experts/dr-andy-pruitt/bike-fit`, `numb-hands-cycling-5-fixes-bike-fit`, `cycling-knee-pain-causes-fixes`, `cycling-mobility-routine`.
- **Word count:** ~1,800

### 4. Heat Tolerance and the Ageing Cyclist: Why Masters Riders Overheat
- **Pillar:** Recovery / Coaching
- **Target keyword:** `heat training masters cyclist` (secondary: `older cyclist overheating`, `thermoregulation age cycling`, `heat adaptation over 50`)
- **Why it's a gap:** We own heat *adaptation* protocols generally (`cycling-heat-training-guide`, `heat-training-cyclists-30-watts-ftp-protocol`, `cycling-heat-training-protocol-at-home`) and the indoor heat-management piece — but nothing on the **age dimension**: sweat response, skin blood flow and thirst sensitivity all blunt with age, so masters riders run hotter for the same effort and dehydrate before they feel it.
- **Angle:** Why the same gran fondo that cooked you at 50 didn't at 35, the physiology of declining thermoregulation, and a masters-adjusted heat-adaptation and hydration plan (lower starting dose, longer ramp, proactive drinking). Links into the indoor hub's heat-management work.
- **Suggested experts:** Professor Stephen Cheung (thermoregulation), Dr Allen Lim (hydration / Skratch).
- **Internal links:** `/training/indoor`, `indoor-cycling-heat-management-trainingpeaks-virtual`, `cycling-heat-training-guide`, `electrolytes-sweat-rate-cycling`.
- **Word count:** ~1,900

### 5. Alcohol and the Masters Cyclist: What One Glass Really Costs You After 40
- **Pillar:** Nutrition / Recovery
- **Target keyword:** `alcohol cycling recovery` (secondary: `alcohol masters cyclist`, `does alcohol affect cycling performance`, `wine recovery athletes over 40`)
- **Why it's a gap:** High-curiosity, high-search, and genuinely under-served — we have no piece on it, and the masters audience over-indexes on the "few glasses of wine a week" demographic. The honest, evidence-led version (not preachy) is very on-brand.
- **Angle:** What alcohol actually does to overnight recovery — suppressed deep sleep, blunted protein synthesis (which matters more under anabolic resistance), disrupted glycogen replenishment and hydration — and why the cost climbs after 40. Practical, not puritanical: where it genuinely sets you back and where a glass is fine. Ties directly to the sleep and masters-nutrition work.
- **Suggested experts:** Dr Andy Galpin, Tim Spector (metabolic health), Alex Larson (dietitian).
- **Internal links:** `/nutrition/masters`, `masters-metabolism-anabolic-resistance-nutrition`, `cycling-sleep-performance-guide`, `post-ride-recovery-nutrition-cyclists`.
- **Word count:** ~1,600

### 6. Back to the Bunch After 40: Group-Ride and Chaingang Craft for Returning Riders
- **Pillar:** Le Métier
- **Target keyword:** `group ride etiquette cycling` (secondary: `chaingang etiquette`, `how to ride in a group cycling`, `returning to club cycling`)
- **Why it's a gap:** Le Métier is our least-served pillar on the blog, and this is the piece the comeback-rider persona (returning after a decade off) actually needs. We have skills pieces but nothing on the **unwritten rules of the group** — the craft, not the fitness.
- **Angle:** The stuff nobody writes down: holding a wheel, through-and-off in a chaingang, half-wheeling and why it's rude, signalling, when to pull and when to sit, the social code of the club run. Framed for the rider coming back to it after years away and not wanting to be "that guy."
- **Suggested experts:** Anthony Walsh (Le Métier voice), plus any pro guest on bunch craft (e.g. George Hincapie, Andre Greipel on positioning).
- **Internal links:** `comeback-cyclist-12-week-return-plan`, `cycling-climbing-tips-stop-getting-dropped`, `how-to-descend-faster-cycling`.
- **Word count:** ~1,700

### 7. Detraining After 40: How Fast You Lose Fitness on a Break — and How to Come Back
- **Pillar:** Coaching
- **Target keyword:** `detraining cycling` (secondary: `how fast do you lose fitness cycling`, `detraining masters cyclist`, `losing fitness off the bike over 40`)
- **Why it's a gap:** `comeback-cyclist-12-week-return-plan` covers the *return*, but nothing covers the **rate of loss** — which is the high-search question ("how fast do I lose fitness?") and is age-dependent: VO2max and the top end go first and faster after 40, exactly the system our VO2max hub is built around.
- **Angle:** The timeline of detraining (what goes in week 1 vs week 4 vs 3 months), why the top end fades first and fastest for masters riders, the minimum-effective dose to hold fitness through an unavoidable break (illness, work, injury), and how to come back without injuring yourself. Anchors on Dr David Lipman's "floor over ceiling" point.
- **Suggested experts:** Dr David Lipman (masters performance), Joe Friel, Dr Andy Galpin.
- **Internal links:** `/masters/vo2max`, `vo2max-decline-reversibility-masters-cyclists`, `comeback-cyclist-12-week-return-plan`, `training-load-management-cyclists-40s-50s`.
- **Word count:** ~1,800

### 8. The 10-Minute Mobility Routine That Protects a Masters Rider's Position
- **Pillar:** Strength & Conditioning
- **Target keyword:** `mobility masters cyclist` (secondary: `flexibility cycling over 40`, `hip mobility cyclists`, `cycling stretching routine masters`)
- **Why it's a gap:** `cycling-mobility-routine` exists but is general. The masters version is the missing one and it's the practical other half of brief #3 (Bike Fit After 40): the fit can only be as aggressive as the body is mobile, so mobility is what *earns back* the position age takes away.
- **Angle:** Hips, T-spine, hamstrings and ankles — the four restrictions that quietly pull a masters rider out of their fit and into back and neck pain. A specific, repeatable 10-minute routine (not a generic stretch list), when to do it, and how it lets an older rider hold a position that's otherwise slipping away. Explicit pairing with the bike-fit piece.
- **Suggested experts:** Phil Burt, Dr Andy Pruitt, a named S&C coach (e.g. Derek Teel / Dialed Health).
- **Internal links:** `cycling-mobility-routine`, `core-strength-cyclists-beyond-planks`, `cycling-knee-pain-causes-fixes`.
- **Word count:** ~1,500
