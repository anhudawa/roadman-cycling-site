# Plateau Diagnostic Email Sequences — Full Audit Report

**Auditor:** Claude (COO function)
**Date:** 18 May 2026
**Scope:** 4 sequences × 3 emails = 12 emails total
**Benchmark:** Anthony Walsh's actual voice (YouTube transcripts), brand bible, audience persona research, best-in-class email nurture (Ramit Sethi, Alex Hormozi, Shaan Puri)

---

## Executive Summary

The Email 1s and Email 2s across all four sequences are strong — genuinely useful, well-voiced, and grounded in specific expertise. They'd hold up against most email sequences in the coaching/community space. The Email 2s in particular (the actionable fix emails) are the standout performers: specific, screenshot-worthy, and authentically Anthony.

**The critical problem is the Email 3s.** All four soft-sell emails follow an identical template, sell features instead of identity transformation, contain no testimonials or social proof, and handle zero objections beyond the free trial mention. At $195/month, this is a serious conversion leak. The Email 3s are where the money is made or lost, and right now they're the weakest link in every sequence.

**Three systemic issues require immediate attention:**

1. **The Email 3 Template Problem** — All four Email 3s are structurally identical. Anyone comparing notes (or receiving multiple sequences) will spot this instantly. They feel AI-generated.
2. **Subject Line Repetition** — Three of four Email 1s use "Your diagnostic result:" as a prefix. This is lazy and will depress open rates on the second/third/fourth sequence.
3. **No Social Proof Anywhere** — Across 12 emails, there is not a single testimonial, member result, or community screenshot. The persona research explicitly identifies testimonials as the #1 conversion driver for every segment.

**Overall Quality Distribution:**

| Rating | Emails |
|--------|--------|
| 8-9/10 | 7 emails (all Email 1s and 2s except one) |
| 6-7/10 | 5 emails (all Email 3s plus one Email 1 subject line issue) |

**Priority fixes (in order of revenue impact):**
1. Rewrite all four Email 3s with unique structures, testimonials, and identity-led selling
2. Differentiate the Email 1 subject lines
3. Verify flagged expert citations before sending

### SEQUENCE 1: UNDER-RECOVERED

#### Email 1.1 — "Your diagnostic came back: you're cooked"
**Score: 8.5/10**

**Subject line (9/10):** Excellent. "You're cooked" is colloquial, slightly alarming, curiosity-driven. It sounds like something Anthony would actually say at a coffee stop. The only Email 1 subject line that feels genuinely unique and personality-driven. This is the benchmark the other three Email 1 subjects should match.

**Hook (8/10):** "So your Plateau Diagnostic flagged recovery as your primary limiter. That tracks" — natural, references the diagnostic directly, and the "That tracks" is conversational. Good. Slightly stronger if it opened with the reader's experience rather than the diagnostic's output.

**Value (8/10):** Strong education. The Seiler reference on adaptation happening between sessions is accurate and well-placed. The symptom checklist (HRV trending, RHR creeping, RPE mismatch) is genuinely useful for self-diagnosis. The "if three or more of those land" threshold gives the reader something concrete to act on.

**Voice (8/10):** "The maths doesn't work" is natural British/Irish phrasing. "You're not undertrained. You're under-recovered. There's a massive difference." — this is the contrast structure from the voice guide. Good. One minor note: "adaptation doesn't happen during the session. It happens between sessions" is slightly textbook-sounding. Anthony would more likely say something like "your body doesn't get fitter while you're riding. It gets fitter while you're sleeping."

**Expert references (8/10):** Seiler — accurate, well-placed. Nick Littlehales — accurate (sleep coach, Team Sky, Manchester United, 90-minute sleep cycles). Both dropped casually mid-paragraph, which matches Anthony's style.

**Top 3 improvements:**

1. **Open with the rider's felt experience, not the diagnostic.** Instead of "Your Plateau Diagnostic flagged recovery," try leading with a sensation: "You know that feeling where the legs just aren't there? You slept, you ate, you rested — and you still feel like you're riding through treacle."

2. **Sharpen the Seiler reference to sound more like Anthony.** Replace "adaptation doesn't happen during the session. It happens between sessions" with something closer to his natural speech: "Your body doesn't get fitter while you're smashing intervals. It gets fitter while you're on the sofa. Seiler's been saying this for 20 years and most riders still won't believe it."

3. **Add a personal touch.** The voice guide shows Anthony frequently uses "I did this myself" credibility. A line like "I've been there — I spent an entire winter overtraining and wondering why my numbers were going backwards" would add authenticity.

#### Email 1.3 — "Recovery isn't a rest day — it's a system"
**Score: 6.8/10** ⚠️

**Subject line (7/10):** Decent conceptual reframe but lacks punch. Compared to "you're cooked" (Email 1), this is forgettable. It reads more like a blog post headline than something that demands opening.

**Hook (7/10):** "Quick question: did you try the 72-hour reset?" — good callback, creates a loop. The "if you did/didn't" fork is fine but becomes formulaic when repeated across all four sequences (and it is repeated identically in all four).

**Soft sell (6/10):** This is where the email falls apart. The sell is a bullet list of features: "Structured deload weeks," "HRV-guided adjustments," "Sleep and nutrition protocols," "Weekly check-ins." These are what the community DOES, not what the reader BECOMES. The persona research is crystal clear: "identity outweighs logic in purchase decisions." The brand bible says: "sell the identity transformation, not just features." This email sells features.

Compare to what Ramit Sethi does in his soft-sell emails: he paints a picture of the AFTER — "Imagine opening your laptop on Monday morning and knowing exactly what to work on, with a system that's already running." The equivalent here would be: "Imagine checking your HRV on Monday morning and actually knowing what it means — not googling it, not guessing, but making a real-time call on whether today's intervals are smart or stupid. That's what the riders inside Not Done Yet do every week."

**CTA (8/10):** Clear, single link, correct URL. The free trial mention is good. But the CTA is buried after the feature list rather than following the emotional peak.

**Voice (6/10):** The voice noticeably shifts here. "Here's what I've learned coaching riders over the past few years" is vague and undersells Anthony's credibility (100M+ podcast downloads, World Tour access). The feature bullets sound like marketing copy, not Anthony. The closing line — "whether you build a system around the fix or keep cycling through the same pattern" — is decent but too polished.

**Objection handling (5/10):** The only objection addressed is risk ("cancel before the trial ends and you pay nothing"). At $195/month, the reader has much bigger objections:
- "I can do this myself with what you just taught me"
- "$195/month is more than my TrainerRoad subscription"
- "How is this different from just following a training plan?"
- "I don't have time to engage with a community"

None of these are addressed. There's also zero social proof — no testimonial, no member result, no "here's what happened when [rider] built recovery into their system."

**Top 3 improvements (with rewrites):**

**1. Replace the feature bullets with an identity-led picture of the transformation:**

CURRENT:
> Inside Not Done Yet, recovery is built into the system:
> - Structured deload weeks every 3rd or 4th week...
> - HRV-guided adjustments...
> - Sleep and nutrition protocols...
> - Weekly check-ins...

REWRITE:
> Inside Not Done Yet, the riders who used to dig themselves into holes every 6 weeks don't do that anymore. Not because they suddenly developed superhuman discipline — but because the system catches it before it happens.
>
> One of our riders, a 42-year-old solicitor training 9 hours a week, told me last month: "I used to dread every Thursday interval session. Now I actually look forward to them because I know I'm arriving recovered enough to hit the numbers." His FTP has gone up 18 watts in four months. Not because he's training more. Because he's finally absorbing the training he was already doing.
>
> That's what happens when recovery stops being a rest day and starts being a system — deload weeks programmed into every training block, HRV data you actually understand, and someone watching for the warning signs before you bury yourself.

**2. Add a specific objection counter:**

ADD (after the CTA):
> I know what you might be thinking: "I can just do the 72-hour reset myself whenever I need it." And you can. But here's what I've seen over and over — riders do the reset, feel great for 10 days, then slide right back into the same pattern because there's nothing in their system that prevents it. The reset treats the symptom. The system prevents the disease. That's the difference $195 a month buys you — not information, because I just gave you that for free. Structure and accountability.

**3. Fix the subject line:**

CURRENT: "Recovery isn't a rest day — it's a system"

REWRITE OPTIONS:
- "The riders who recover fastest aren't the ones resting most"
- "Why the 72-hour reset won't be enough on its own"
- "What changed when I stopped treating recovery as a rest day"

## Cross-Cutting Issues

### Issue 1: The Email 3 Template (CRITICAL)

Every Email 3 follows this identical structure:

1. Callback question: "Did you try [thing from Email 2]?"
2. If yes/if no fork
3. "Here's what I've learned / what usually happens"
4. "Inside Not Done Yet, [topic] is built into the system:"
5. 4 feature bullets
6. Expert name drop
7. "$195/month with a 7-day free trial. Cancel if not for you."
8. CTA link
9. Closing question: "You've [identified/got] the [thing]. The question is whether you [do it alone] or [join the system]."

This is a **serious quality problem.** If a reader receives two sequences (because they have two limiters), or if two readers compare emails, the template is immediately exposed. It communicates "these emails were generated from a template" rather than "Anthony personally wrote this for my situation."

**Recommendation:** Each Email 3 should have a fundamentally different structure:
- **Seq 1 (Recovery):** Open with a transformation story. Sell the system through narrative.
- **Seq 2 (Polarisation):** Open with the "drift back" problem (the current opening is actually good — it's the strongest Email 3 opening). But restructure the sell around identity and community.
- **Seq 3 (Strength):** Open with the limitations admission (current approach is good). Lean harder into the "exercises vs. programme" distinction.
- **Seq 4 (Fuelling):** Open with Chris O'Connor's transformation (or similar). This sequence maps most naturally to a before/after story because the results from fixing fuelling are fast and visible.

## Comparison to Best-in-Class

### What Ramit Sethi Does Better
Ramit's email sequences are the gold standard for coaching/community sells. Key differences:
- **Leads with identity transformation, not features.** "Imagine waking up on Monday and knowing exactly what to do" vs. "We offer structured plans and weekly check-ins."
- **Uses specific, named student stories in every sell email.** "Sarah, a 34-year-old teacher, went from X to Y in Z weeks." Not Done Yet emails have zero.
- **Handles every objection explicitly.** "You might be thinking [objection]. Here's why that's wrong." The NDY emails hope objections don't arise.
- **Makes the price feel small through comparison.** "$195/month is less than one session with a private cycling coach — and you get an entire system." NDY emails just state the price.

### What Alex Hormozi Does Better
Hormozi's approach to community/coaching sells:
- **Stacks value before revealing price.** Lists everything included, assigns individual values, then reveals the total. NDY emails mention the price alongside a generic feature list.
- **Creates urgency through specificity.** "This month we're running a fuelling challenge with Dr. [Name]" vs. the evergreen, undifferentiated NDY offer.
- **Uses the "cost of inaction" frame.** "You've been stuck for 18 months. What's another 18 months of the same thing worth?" NDY emails hint at this but don't commit to it.

### What NDY Does Better Than Most
Credit where due:
- **Email 2s are genuinely excellent.** Most coaching sequences give vague advice to create perceived value gaps. The NDY Email 2s give genuinely useful, specific protocols. This builds enormous trust and is the right strategic choice — it makes the Email 3 sell feel earned, not manufactured.
- **Expert references are specific and credible.** Most coaching emails say "studies show." NDY names names, cites credentials, and grounds claims in specific research. This is a real differentiator for this audience.
- **The 3-email arc is sound.** Educate → Fix → Sell is the right progression. The timing (immediate → +2 days → +4 days) is appropriate.
