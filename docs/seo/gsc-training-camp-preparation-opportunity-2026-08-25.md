## Problems in the previous version

The previous guide presented coach observations as universal outcomes. It
prescribed a 10–15% weekly ramp and one exact taper, claimed day-one TSS was the
single biggest predictor of success, promised fixed CTL gains and an equivalent
number of training weeks, prescribed one camp-wide carbohydrate intake, used
fixed heart-rate and power-to-RPE warning thresholds, and guaranteed a
7–10-day supercompensation timetable. Those claims were not supported as
written and overlapped the adaptation and first-timer owners.

## Decision

1. Lead with the exact preparation query and a four-week planning promise.
2. Start from the operator's hardest route and the rider's recent comparable
   training rather than a universal FTP, volume or CTL threshold.
3. Use successive-day rehearsals to test fuelling, contact points, gearing and
   recovery without recreating the entire camp.
4. Apply taper evidence with an explicit limit: competition studies inform the
   decision but do not prove one exact recreational-camp formula.
5. Ground load, nutrition, heat and sleep statements in named consensus or
   systematic-review sources.
6. Route camp-week structure, post-camp recovery and commercial availability to
   their declared owners.
7. Replace guaranteed outcomes with stop, adjust and ask-the-operator decisions.

## Measurement

At the 7-day and 28-day checkpoints, compare clicks, impressions, CTR and
average position for the URL. Monitor preparation, four-week plan, taper,
checklist and fuelling queries; assisted clicks to `/training-camps` and
`/coaching`; and whether expectations and adaptation queries remain on their
separate owners.
