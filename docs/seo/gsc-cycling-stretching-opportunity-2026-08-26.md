## Trust defects found

The incumbent claimed that cycling posture becomes permanent, fascia tightens,
stretching reverses most related issues, restricted flexibility directly costs
power, one hip-flexor stretch fixes the most common source of cyclists' back
pain, thoracic extension improves breathing capacity, and one precise weekly
dose is mandatory. It also used upper- and lower-cross syndrome as if they were
established diagnoses for the reader.

The page had no current review date, named evidence check, cited claim panel,
research links, symptom boundary or distinction between flexibility, mobility,
warm-up, recovery and pain treatment. Its absolute pre-ride warning also
ignored the dose-dependent evidence: performance effects are larger with long
static holds, while shorter holds followed by task-specific movement have much
smaller effects.

## Changes prepared

- Retitled the stable owner `Stretching for Cyclists: Evidence-Based 10-Minute
  Routine`, matching the demonstrated generic and routine intents.
- Reframed stretching around the well-supported outcome—range of motion—and
  removed unsupported power, posture, recovery and pain-cure promises.
- Added a seven-movement, approximately 10-minute routine with controlled
  intensity, timing and symptom boundaries.
- Separated progressive pre-ride warm-up, optional post-ride flexibility work
  and dedicated flexibility sessions.
- Added six source-bounded claims, six FAQs, named editorial review and a
  six-step HowTo protocol.
- Added systematic-review evidence on flexibility dose, acute performance,
  post-exercise recovery, injury incidence, full-range resistance training,
  cycling low-back pain and bike fitting.
- Added urgent neurological escalation based on NICE referral guidance.
- Preserved distinct mobility, warm-up, strength, back-pain and bike-fit pages
  through explicit internal links instead of creating redirects.
- Pinned the owner in bounded LLM discovery, added it to the recurring IndexNow
  set and added AI benchmark prompt 242.
