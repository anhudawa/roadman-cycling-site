## Decision

Keep `/blog/magnesium-cyclists-recovery-performance-guide` as the broad magnesium-for-cyclists owner. Permanently redirect `/blog/cycling-magnesium-performance-recovery-guide` to it.

The pages were published one day apart and answer the same intake, performance, recovery, sleep, cramp, testing, form and dose intent. The chosen owner has 58 of the combined 94 clicks, the earlier publication date, the stronger existing internal-link graph and the more natural `magnesium` + `cyclists` query pattern. The retired page has a slightly better average position, so its history and links should strengthen the single owner rather than compete with it.

The GSC interface rounds totals above one thousand in this view. Impressions below are therefore recorded as displayed and combined approximately rather than presented with false precision.

## Editorial changes

- Rebuilt the owner around a 60-second food, risk, testing and supplement decision.
- Replaced the unsupported 400–600 mg endurance-athlete target with EFSA's 350 mg/day male and 300 mg/day female adult adequate intakes, clearly separated from supplement dosing.
- Removed claims that most cyclists are deficient, soil depletion makes adequate food intake unlikely or training hours prove a supplement need.
- Replaced “serum is useless; RBC is best” with the NIH and biomarker-review position that magnesium status is difficult to assess and no single method is satisfactory.
- Removed fixed RBC “optimal” ranges and symptom-based self-diagnosis.
- Bounded performance claims to athlete reviews, a 20 km cycling trial and the small 2025 cycle-ergometer crossover trial.
- Replaced a universal bedtime glycinate sleep protocol with the uncertain review evidence and small subjective effect in the 2025 bisglycinate trial.
- Clarified that no randomised trials show prevention of exercise-associated cramps.
- Replaced the universal glycinate ranking and fixed oxide absorption percentage with the narrower comparative citrate-versus-oxide evidence.
- Added EFSA and NIH supplemental upper-limit context, kidney and gastrointestinal safety, medication interactions and anti-doping batch testing.
- Added a direct answer to whether magnesium supplements need to be cycled on and off.
- Strengthened the cycling-nutrition hub and supplement guide so they route magnesium intent to one reviewed owner.
- Routed product interest to `/app?source=magnesium-guide`, inside the existing single `app-waitlist` Beehiiv audience.
- Added AI benchmark prompt 361 and pinned the owner in LLM discovery documents.
