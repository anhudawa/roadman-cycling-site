Together the two pages produced **58 clicks**, **6,080 web impressions**, about
**1.0% combined CTR** and **1,041 Google AI-feature impressions**. The reviewed

The visible queries confirm direct cannibalisation. Both pages rank for variants
of:

- `cycling testosterone`;
- `testosterone cycling`;
- `does cycling increase testosterone`;
- `does cycling boost testosterone`; and
- `is cycling bad for testosterone`.

The broader `/blog/masters-cycling-hormones-performance-guide` has a different
multi-hormone intent and remains separate. It recorded 15 clicks, 1,230 web
impressions and 321 AI-feature impressions in the same window.

## Ownership decision

Keep `/blog/cycling-testosterone-and-training-over-40-guide` as the canonical
owner. Its URL and existing search demand match the broad cycling/testosterone
question, including training, testing, masters and TRT intent.

Permanently redirect the guest-led free-testosterone page. Its distinctive Dr
Mark Gordon podcast context remains in the owner, but its unsupported athlete
percentile target, total-testosterone dismissal, cumulative-crash claims,
supplement suggestions and categorical anti-doping advice do not remain live.

## Trust and answer upgrades

The owner now:

- answers “does cycling increase or lower testosterone?” before discussing age;
- separates acute exercise responses from resting testosterone;
- distinguishes endurance adaptation, low energy availability and clinical
  hypogonadism;
- follows Endocrine Society repeat-testing and free-testosterone guidance;
- rejects a 50th–75th percentile athlete target;
- bounds head-trauma claims to significant injury and clinical assessment;
- links the 2026 WADA Prohibited List and WADA male-hypogonadism TUE guidance;
- explains that a TUE can be possible but is not automatic from a prescription,
  ageing or low free testosterone; and
- routes launch interest through the single app audience with
  `source=testosterone-guide`.
