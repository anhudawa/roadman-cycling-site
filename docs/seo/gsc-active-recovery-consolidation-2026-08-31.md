# Active-recovery search consolidation — 31 August 2026

## Baseline

The 90-day app strength-and-recovery Search Console baseline recorded **61 clicks**, **11,570 impressions**, **0.5% CTR** and **7.2 average position** for the recovery-only cluster.

`/blog/cycling-active-recovery-rides-guide` was the leading active-recovery URL with **11 clicks** and **2,124 impressions**. It therefore remains the canonical broad search owner.

## Decision

Three later pages repeated the same broad recovery-ride intent and are retired with permanent redirects:

- `/blog/cycling-active-recovery-explained`
- `/blog/active-recovery-rides-evidence-cyclists`
- `/blog/cycling-recovery-rides-how-to-do-them-properly-guide`

All three now consolidate on `/blog/cycling-active-recovery-rides-guide`. Live internal links, topic membership, answer links and the generation backlog point to the owner only.

## Trust correction

The owner no longer describes lactate as waste, promises faster next-day adaptation, or presents fixed power, heart-rate, duration and HRV thresholds as universal facts. It distinguishes an immediate cool-down from a later recovery ride and cites primary research for each important boundary.

The support answer `/answers/active-or-passive-recovery` remains distinct because it owns a narrow comparison question. It now agrees with the guide: neither active nor passive recovery is universally better.

## Product path

The owner links to `/tools/training-readiness`, `/tools/recovery-screen` and the single app waiting list at `/app`. The tools remain transparent decision aids rather than diagnostic or prescriptive claims.

## Measurement

- **7 September 2026**: verify the owner is indexed, retired URLs report the permanent redirect, and Google-selected canonical matches the owner.
- **28 September 2026**: compare clicks, impressions, CTR, average position and query-to-page concentration against this baseline.
- Watch for the retired URLs continuing to receive impressions, a new competing owner, or loss of non-brand active-recovery queries.
