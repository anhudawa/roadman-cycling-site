## Ownership decision

Keep the existing article URL as the single owner for RED-S in cyclists. It
answers signs, female and male presentation, energy-availability thresholds,
diagnosis, blood tests, recovery and return to training.

Keep `/tools/energy-availability` as the formula and estimation utility, not a
medical screener. The article owns clinical education; the tool shows the
arithmetic and routes users back to the owner.

## Trust corrections

The former article and tool treated 30 kcal/kg FFM/day as a clinical danger line,
45 as universally optimal, diagnosed risk from uncertain free-living inputs,
claimed systems shut down below one threshold, equated stable weight with hidden
RED-S, prescribed calorie increases and diet phases, and provided a red/yellow/
green result that could be confused with the IOC CAT2.

The reviewed owner and estimator now:

- distinguish energy availability, low energy availability and RED-S;
- present 30 and 45 as research references rather than universal cut-offs;
- explain error in food, exercise-expenditure and body-composition inputs;
- include current debate and limitations in the evidence;
- use the IOC CAT2 three-step physician-led pathway;
- treat signs, blood tests and performance changes as non-specific;
- preserve separate male, female, bone-health and differential-diagnosis paths;
- make recovery and sport participation individual clinical decisions;
- remove the tool's RED-S screen, clinical traffic lights and report lead magnet;
- use direct daily exercise expenditure instead of an invented hours/intensity
  calorie model; and
- route both article and tool into the single app waiting-list audience with
  `source=reds-guide` and `source=energy-availability-estimate` attribution.
