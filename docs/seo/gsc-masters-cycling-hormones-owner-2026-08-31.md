## Ownership decision

Keep the existing URL as the broad hormone-navigation owner for masters cyclists.
It answers whether hormones can explain slower recovery and routes each distinct
question to its specialist owner:

- testosterone and TRT;
- cortisol and stress;
- thyroid function;
- perimenopause and menopause;
- REDs and low energy availability; and
- symptom-led blood testing.

The page does not target the dedicated `does cycling increase testosterone`
query as its primary job.

## Trust corrections

The previous page prescribed an annual panel, treated free testosterone as more
important than total testosterone, promoted the testosterone:cortisol ratio as a
recovery marker, described interval and Zone 2 work as hormone medicine, inferred
growth-hormone status from sleep and IGF-1, claimed a high athlete prevalence of
subclinical hypothyroidism and gave fixed supplement, body-fat, menopause and TRT
advice.

The reviewed owner now:

- separates normal exercise responses from endocrine diagnosis;
- rejects a universal annual hormone panel;
- bounds the testosterone:cortisol ratio to limited group-level research;
- explains why random growth-hormone testing is not diagnostic;
- uses NICE thyroid guidance instead of an athlete-optimal range;
- gives menopause training principles without one mandatory protocol;
- treats REDs as multifactorial in women and men;
- links the 2026 WADA list where hormone drugs are discussed; and
- routes app interest through `source=masters-hormones-guide` into the single
  waiting-list audience.
