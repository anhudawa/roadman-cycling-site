## Content risk in the previous version

The article accurately drew several details from the Roadman transcript but
converted them into universal claims: saddle height as the single highest-impact
change for most amateurs, most amateurs riding high, one-millimetre changes as a
mandatory rule, and a 7mm case as evidence of a repeatable one-minute gain. It
also repeated first-person crank-power cases as though they established general
effects.

This risks weakening both Roadman's source integrity and the distinct broad
bike-fit owner. The transcript is valuable primary evidence for what Fitzgerald
said; it is not a population study.

## Decision

1. Keep the URL as the Daryl Fitzgerald entity and interview-context owner.
2. Verify the 7mm, one-minute, three-watt, 1–2mm and shorter-crank statements
   against the episode transcript.
3. Label high-saddle frequency and power cases as practitioner observation or
   individual anecdote rather than universal evidence.
4. Pair the interview with current saddle-height, static/dynamic-angle and
   cycling-overuse reviews.
5. Provide a measured, reversible home test while ceding the complete bike-fit
   intent to `/blog/bike-fit-guide-cyclists` and the extractable setup answer to
   `/answers/how-to-set-saddle-height`.
6. State explicitly that Fitzgerald did not review Roadman's evidence analysis.
