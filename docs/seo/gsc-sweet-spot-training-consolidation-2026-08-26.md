# Sweet spot training search-owner decision — 26 August 2026

## Decision

Keep `/blog/sweet-spot-training-cycling-guide` as the single owner for **sweet spot training**, **sweet spot cycling**, **cycling sweet spot training**, definition, 88–94% FTP, session progression, weekly use and masters questions.

- Rebuild the owner around the coaching convention, evidence limits and response-led application.
- Permanently redirect four overlapping blog guides and two answer pages to the owner.
- Canonicalise `/glossary/sweet-spot` to the owner with a permanent route-level redirect.
- Keep `/tools/sweet-spot` as a distinct calculation job, but remove universal weekly-dose, recovery-time and benefit claims.
- Keep focused comparison pages for sweet spot vs Zone 2, threshold and polarised training.
- Keep `/topics/sweet-spot-training` as a short learning-path hub, not another definition article.

## Problems corrected

The former cluster stated or implied that:

- 88–94% FTP is a distinct physiological zone rather than a coaching convention;
- sweet spot provides roughly 90% of threshold's adaptation at a fraction of the fatigue;
- it is proven to be the best method for every time-crunched cyclist;
- two or three sessions per week is a universal optimum or ceiling;
- fixed 24-, 36-, 48- or 72-hour recovery windows can be prescribed from the label or the rider's age;
- a four-to-eight-week block guarantees an FTP gain;
- age over 40 automatically requires a particular recovery gap; and
- FTP percentages precisely locate individual lactate thresholds.

The replacement makes the 88–94% definition explicit, separates external power from physiological measurement, explains FTP-method uncertainty, gives repeatable sample sessions without presenting them as minimum doses, and routes comparison questions to focused pages.

## Evidence record

| Question                                           | Evidence used                                                                                                                | Boundary applied                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What is 88–94% FTP?                                | TrainingPeaks official power-training guidance                                                                               | A common coaching convention spanning upper tempo and lower threshold, not a separate measured zone |
| Is FTP an interchangeable physiological threshold? | Mackey and Horner 2021 FTP20 scoping review, PMID 34304689                                                                   | FTP20 can be reliable, but large limits of agreement prevent interchangeability with other markers  |
| Is sweet spot universally superior?                | Filipe et al. 2025 trained-cyclist meta-analysis, PMID 39788807; Molmen et al. 2025 IPD network meta-analysis, PMID 39888556 | Several distributions can improve performance; athlete level and whole-programme context matter     |
| Does polarised always beat threshold-heavy work?   | Rosenblat et al. 2019 meta-analysis, PMID 29863593                                                                           | The pooled result favoured polarised, but only four studies met inclusion and study quality was low |
| Is one block or weekly frequency established?      | Almquist et al. 2023 trained-cyclist periodisation review, PMID 36640771                                                     | Seven eligible studies did not establish one superior 8–12-week model                               |
| Do masters always recover more slowly?             | Borges et al. 2016 review, PMID 25880787; Wiewelhove et al. 2022, PMID 35050974                                              | Evidence is limited and mixed; age alone does not validate a fixed recovery interval                |

## Measurement cohort

Do not change the owner title, canonical target or intent boundary before the first stable comparison unless a factual or safety correction is required.

- **5 September 2026:** verify all redirects, canonical, schema, sitemap, hub/tool handoffs, LLM discovery and internal links; record directional movement only.
- **26 September 2026:** compare the same 28-day page and query cohort with the preceding 28 days, allowing for Search Console reporting lag.
- Primary measures: owner clicks, impressions, CTR and average position; exact-query metrics for the three broad queries; decline in impressions on the retired URLs as Google processes the redirects.
- Secondary measures: discovery of `what percent of ftp is sweet spot`, `how often should I do sweet spot`, `sweet spot intervals cycling` and masters variants; assisted visits to the calculator, comparison pages and coaching journey.
- Guardrail: calculator intent should remain on `/tools/sweet-spot`, and comparison intent should remain on the focused comparison owners rather than migrating to the broad guide.

## Rollback boundary

Keep the canonical owner and permanent redirects. If CTR does not improve after a stable comparison window, test the owner's title and description. Do not restore duplicate broad pages, the unsupported 90%-benefit formula, fixed recovery windows or guaranteed block results.
