# Cycling-and-cortisol search owner — GSC refresh (31 August 2026)

## Decision

Keep `/blog/cycling-cortisol-stress-performance-guide` as the single long-form
owner for whether cycling raises, lowers, increases or spikes cortisol; how
intensity, duration, heat, sleep and life stress affect the answer; and what a
cyclist should do with that information.

The HRV guide remains the owner for measurement and HRV-guided training. The
autonomic-recovery guide remains the owner for parasympathetic reactivation,
breathing and cold-water questions. Both now support this page without allowing
HRV to become a cortisol proxy.

## What changed

- Reframed the title and first answer around the exact raise-versus-lower intent.
- Distinguished a normal acute exercise response from long-term regulation and
  clinical hypercortisolism.
- Added the HIIT meta-analysis, the conflicting low-intensity studies, direct
  cycling heat evidence and the physical-activity regulation meta-analysis.
- Replaced “one identical stress budget” with a useful but explicitly bounded
  multidimensional load-and-recovery model.
- Removed HRV as a real-time cortisol monitor, fixed 5–7-day rules, universal
  clearance times and symptom-based cortisol diagnosis.
- Replaced the claimed 20–30% sleep effect with the 2024 meta-analysis and its
  mixed sample/protocol findings.
- Removed the after-40 clearance guarantee and added the over-40 hormone review.
- Added the athlete salivary-cortisol and overtraining biomarker limitations,
  medical boundaries, app attribution, priority recrawl, LLM discovery and AI
  benchmark prompt 370.
