## Problems in the previous version

The old guide presented one rider example as a universal 5,500–7,000-calorie
target, claimed a fixed 1,000-calorie deficit impaired performance by day three,
called a meal within 30–60 minutes the single most important habit, prescribed
one breakfast and fluid rate, claimed Mediterranean sweat rises by 30–50%,
required 150% fluid replacement and one morning-weight threshold, guaranteed
specific effects from one versus three glasses of wine, and imposed an exact
four-to-six-week gut-training progression. These statements exceeded or
misrepresented the evidence and mixed nutrition with preparation and recovery
ownership.

## Decision

1. Target the exact `cycling training camp nutrition` intent with a daily route
   fuel plan rather than a fear-based calorie promise.
2. Build the plan from route duration, intensity, conditions, tested tolerance,
   food access and the recovery window.
3. Present 30–60g/hour and up to 90g/hour as published carbohydrate ranges with
   training and tolerance requirements, not one mandatory target.
4. Explain that rapid refuelling matters most when recovery time is short and
   remove the universal 30-minute deadline.
5. Replace one bottle rate with a comparable-ride sweat estimate, IOC
   overdrinking guardrail and individual adjustment.
6. Ground gut training, hydration and alcohol/sleep statements in named
   systematic-review or consensus sources.
7. Add explicit clinical escalation for gastrointestinal, metabolic, allergy,
   disordered-eating and low-energy-availability concerns.
