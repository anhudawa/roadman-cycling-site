## Ownership decision

Keep the existing URL as the single owner for cycling and thyroid function. It
answers four related intents:

- whether cycling helps or harms thyroid health;
- which symptoms warrant assessment;
- how TSH, FT4, FT3 and antibodies are selected and interpreted; and
- how thyroid disease differs from low energy availability and overtraining.

The wider hormone hub remains the navigation owner. The blood-testing, REDs,
persistent-fatigue and overtraining pages retain their narrower pathways.

## Trust corrections

The former article required TSH, FT3, FT4 and antibodies for every endurance
athlete; prescribed an athlete TSH target of 0.5–2.5; put FT4 in the upper half
and FT3 in the upper third; treated low T3 as a cycling adaptation; promoted
reverse T3; claimed a fixed two-to-four-week overtraining distinction; required
fasted pre-9am draws; recommended annual panels; gave prescriptive levothyroxine
timing; and dismissed standard GP testing.

The reviewed owner now:

- follows NICE's TSH-led adult testing pathway;
- separates primary and central hypothyroidism;
- explains why FT3, antibodies and reverse T3 are not generic readiness tests;
- rejects a cyclist-specific optimal thyroid range;
- distinguishes transient exercise effects, low energy availability, REDs and
  thyroid-gland disease;
- treats overtraining as a diagnosis of exclusion with no gold-standard marker;
- uses NHS medication guidance without changing prescribed treatment;
- routes persistent fatigue to the appropriate specialist owners; and
- sends app interest through `source=thyroid-guide` into the single waiting-list
  audience.
