# Bedtime protein knowledge package — 31 August 2026

## Release decision

The podcast knowledge audit scored the Dr Michael Ormsbee episode 79/100 and
identified three missing trust fields: chapters, reviewed citations and a guest
bio. It was the strongest unfinished episode adjacent to Roadman's upcoming
strength-and-conditioning and recovery product territory.

The existing cluster also repeated claims that were broader than the evidence:
that surplus bedtime protein could not increase body fat, that 30–40g was a
universal prescription, and that skipping the feed jeopardised next-day cycling
performance. Those claims weakened the site's value as a citable knowledge
layer, so this phase corrects the complete owner set rather than polishing only
the episode template.

This intent split preserves both useful article URLs without leaving two pages
that make the same promise.

## Evidence boundary

Reviewed sources establish that protein consumed before sleep can be digested
and absorbed and can increase overnight myofibrillar and mitochondrial protein
synthesis after evening exercise. Most studies used 20–45g about 30 minutes
before sleep and were conducted in young men.

The boundary is equally important:

- a systematic review warned that unequal total daily protein complicates the
  longer-term timing evidence;
- a protein-timing meta-analysis identified total protein as the stronger
  predictor;
- the direct professional-cycling trial found no added recovery or performance
  benefit from 40g of pre-sleep casein in riders already consuming more than
  2.5g/kg/day; and
- an Ormsbee-group trial found no next-morning strength or muscle-damage benefit
  after evening resistance exercise.

The release therefore presents pre-sleep protein as an optional way to close a
daily shortfall, not a fat-loss shortcut, guaranteed performance gain or
compulsory 40g ritual.
