## Search ownership

## Problems in the previous version

The old guide required one hard-easy-hard schedule and fixed three-to-five-hour
days, guaranteed two to three weeks of adaptation in one camp, prescribed one
calorie range and a 30–40% under-fuelling statistic, presented an immediate
30-minute recovery window as mandatory, prescribed eight hours of sleep,
compression, leg elevation and “flushing metabolic waste”, imposed fixed
heart-rate and FTP recovery caps, and forecast a 10–14-day supercompensation
window plus five-to-seven-day immune suppression. It also carried stale EUR995
commercial copy and duplicated packing and readiness intent.

## Decision

1. Target the distinct `cycling training camp week plan`, structure and recovery
   intent rather than another broad “what to expect” promise.
2. Treat two or three priority rides plus lower-load or optional days as a
   practical starting framework, not a universal formula.
3. Introduce a daily symptoms, sleep, soreness, motivation and effort-response
   check grounded in IOC consensus and systematic-review evidence.
4. Replace fixed easy-day percentages with a purpose test: the day should not
   add meaningful fatigue or damage the next priority ride.
5. Ground food, sleep and recovery statements in named sources and label
   recovery gadgets as optional because evidence is inconsistent.
6. Replace the fixed post-camp calendar with a staged return based on ordinary
   function, an easy ride and one familiar quality session.
7. Remove stale commercial details and declare the live booking pages as the
   source of truth.

## Measurement

At the 7-day and 28-day checkpoints, compare clicks, impressions, CTR and
average position. Monitor week-plan, camp structure, recovery, fatigue and
post-camp queries; irrelevant mentoring/talent queries; assisted clicks to
`/coaching` and `/training-camps`; and whether preparation and first-timer
queries remain with their declared owners.
