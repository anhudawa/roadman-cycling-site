## Problems in the previous version

The old guide invented a universal four-tier market, mixed self-guided app
subscriptions with human coaching, quoted competitor app prices, claimed UK,
EU and Australian pricing tracked within 10%, stated that apps plateau most
riders in 12–18 months, asserted a typical 5–15% performance gain from coaching,
prescribed coaching tiers from weekly training hours, required a universal
12-week trial and claimed a tier-three programme beats a poorly matched
tier-four coach 80% of the time.

It also compared monthly and four-week prices without annualising them, omitted
setup fees and minimum terms, and treated package names as if they represented
consistent services across providers.

## Decision

1. Replace the invented tier model with a dated audit of public official price
   pages.
2. Include an offer only when it is cycling-specific, recurring, human-supported
   and publicly priced with an identifiable billing period.
3. Preserve provider currencies and avoid time-sensitive exchange conversion.
4. Separate monthly, four-week and annual billing and show that four-week
   subscriptions charge 13 times per 52-week year.
5. Compare named-human input, review cadence, plan changes, calls, messages,
   setup fees, minimum terms and bundled camp days.
6. Keep Roadman's two offers outside the 14-offer external sample and disclose
   the commercial conflict and delivery-model difference.
7. Remove all claims about typical performance gains, hour-based tier rules,
   app plateaus and universal commitment periods.
8. Link provider selection, app comparison and Roadman service intent to their
   declared owners.

## Measurement

per month`, `online cycling coach cost`, `how much does a cycling coach cost`,

whether selection queries stay with the checklist owner; and assisted clicks to
`/coaching`, `/inner-circle`, `/apply` and `/find-your-fit`.
