# Brick training search-owner decision — 26 August 2026

## Decision

Keep and rebuild the established broad owner:

`/blog/brick-workouts-for-ironman`

The stable URL already owns almost all demonstrated demand for `brick training`,
`brick workout`, `brick run` and `brick session`. Broaden its answer from ten
unsupported Ironman prescriptions to the full bike-to-run decision: definition,
evidence, session jobs, progression, pacing, fuelling and safety.

Keep `/glossary/brick-workout` as a concise definition, and keep
`/blog/brick-workouts-cyclists-guide` as the narrower cyclist-to-duathlon
progression. Do not create another broad brick-training URL.

## Trust defects found

The owner previously presented coaching opinions as settled evidence. It
claimed that:

- brick workouts train one defined neuromuscular switch, depleted-glycogen
  substrate utilisation and pacing discipline;
- one to two bricks per week is the correct dose for most athletes;
- a four-to-five-hour bike plus a 60-to-90-minute run is the single most
  predictive Ironman workout;
- riders cannot skip bricks even when they run well after cycling;
- every race-specific brick should use exactly 90 g of carbohydrate per hour;
- high cadence before transition and a fixed running cadence improve the
  switch; and
- long early bricks produce a predictable injury outcome.

The article used two sources that did not support most of those prescriptions,
had no review date or evidence table and did not disclose where direct
brick-training dose evidence was absent.

The cyclist companion repeated a universal 10–15% pace loss, a guaranteed
reduction to 1–5% within weeks, a one-to-two-session ceiling and one settled
mechanism for heavy legs. The glossary repeated the unverified name origin,
frequency and race-pace formula.

## Changes prepared

- Retitled the stable owner `Brick Training for Triathlon: Evidence, Sessions &
Plan`, matching the two high-impression head terms without changing the URL.
- Defined three jobs for a brick: transition familiarity, race execution and
  deliberate schedule efficiency.
- Added six evidence-graded claims, six FAQs, a six-step starter HowTo, named
  review and visible evidence limits.
- Added the 2022 cycle-to-run systematic review plus controlled run-after-bike
  and cadence studies, while making sample and protocol limits explicit.
- Replaced ten prescriptive Ironman sessions with five scalable templates whose
  purpose, not suffering duration, determines the design.
- Removed the universal weekly dose, cadence trick, 90 g/h floor and “most
  predictive session” claim.
- Rebuilt the cyclist companion around run readiness and duathlon-specific
  progression, and corrected the glossary definition.
- Pinned the owner in bounded LLM discovery, added it to the full AI priority
  index, retained it in the recurring IndexNow cluster and added AI benchmark
  prompt 285.

Track total owner clicks, impressions, CTR, position and exposed queries. For
`brick training`, success means improving CTR above 0.5% and moving into the top
seven without losing the owner’s dominant page share. For `brick workout`, the
first threshold is sustained click activity from the frozen zero-click baseline.
Also track `brick run`, `brick session`, `what is a brick run`, Ironman modifiers
and whether the glossary or cyclist companion begins materially competing for
the broad head terms.

For AI benchmarking, prompt 285 should cite the stable owner and preserve the
boundaries: prior cycling often affects the run; response varies; there is no
universal frequency, cadence trick or mandatory monster brick; fuelling scales
to demand; and the run still counts as impact load.

Manual Google URL inspection and “Request indexing” remain a separate approved
action. IndexNow does not submit to Google.
