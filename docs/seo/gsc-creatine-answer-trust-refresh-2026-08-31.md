# Creatine answer AI-search trust refresh — 31 August 2026

## Why this owner moved next

The 29 August AI-discovery scan recorded 13,838 impressions for
`/answers/should-cyclists-take-creatine`, compared with 296 in the prior rolling
window. The page was already being retrieved at scale, but it had no visible
source list and made broader endurance, masters and body-mass claims than the
direct evidence supports.

## Intent boundary

- `/answers/should-cyclists-take-creatine` owns the concise evidence verdict.
- `/blog/creatine-for-cyclists-thirty-day-data` owns Anthony's first-party n=1
  report and must not be presented as a controlled population result.
- `/blog/creatine-for-cyclists-30-day-protocol` owns practical protocol intent.
- `/tools/strength-session-planner` and `/app?source=creatine-answer` own the
  next strength-and-recovery action, not the supplement verdict itself.

## Release changes

- Replaced the blanket recommendation with a direct distinction between null
  pooled endurance evidence, selected repeated-high-intensity use cases and the
  stronger creatine-plus-resistance-training evidence.
- Bounded the masters claim: the older-adult meta-analysis involved mean ages of
  57–70 and was not a masters-cycling performance trial.
- Labelled Anthony's 30-day result as first-party n=1 evidence without a placebo
  control.
- Replaced mandatory 5 g/day and certain 1–2 kg claims with established dosing
  options and an individual body-mass response.
- Added eight visible sources, review date/reviewer, medical and anti-doping
  product-quality boundaries, eight qualified FAQs and links into the app and
  strength planner.
- Added the answer and both companion articles to priority IndexNow recrawl.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Trained-endurance systematic review/meta-analysis (PMID 36877404).
2. 2026 endurance and mixed-sport scoping review (PMID 42280321).
3. Cycling ergogenic-aid systematic review (PMID 38892701).
4. Adult and older-adult resistance-training meta-analyses (PMIDs 39519498,
   29138605 and 39074168).
5. ISSN evidence review and Australian Institute of Sport applied fact sheet.
6. Roadman's 30-day experiment, clearly labelled n=1 first-party context.

## Measurement contract

Recrawl first. Then compare the exact answer URL and creatine query family on
the fixed scorecard. Protect retrieval quality and qualified clicks; do not chase
more impressions by turning conditional evidence back into a universal claim.
