No validation or indexing control was used.

One visible query asked for a source for the 10cm knee-to-wall norm. That is a
useful trust signal: the prior page presented 10cm as a diagnostic threshold
without evidence for a cycling-specific cutoff.

## Content risk in the previous version

The page claimed that cycling progressively reduces ankle mobility, that a
sub-10cm wall test diagnoses meaningful restriction, that three exercises fix
the problem in four to six weeks, and that riders with Achilles symptoms should
move their cleats rearward by a fixed amount. It also described eccentric calf
raises as the single evidence-based treatment and mapped warm-up behaviour,
swelling and joint sounds to diagnoses.

Those claims went beyond the cycling, ankle-test and Achilles evidence and
created a trust risk on a page already averaging the first page of results.

## Decision

1. Keep the URL as the owner of `ankle cycling`, ankle-mobility measurement,
   general calf-strength progression and `does cycling strengthen ankles`.
2. Explain that the weight-bearing lunge test is repeatable but does not have a
   validated universal 10cm cycling pass mark.
3. Separate range, strength, cycling technique and clinical diagnosis.
4. Keep the self-selected pedal technique as the baseline instead of forcing a
   toe-down, heel-down or visually straight ankle style.
5. Route cleat installation and measurement to the cleat guide; do not prescribe
   a fixed rearward move for Achilles symptoms.
6. Use current Achilles clinical guidance only for diagnosed clinical care and
   include urgent ankle and possible rupture boundaries.

## Measurement

Compare the URL and its visible query set at the 7-day and 28-day checkpoints.
Watch clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, growth in the three main
query families and whether evidence-seeking queries receive the revised answer.
