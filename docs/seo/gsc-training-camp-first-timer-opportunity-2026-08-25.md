## Search ownership

- This URL owns first-timer expectations, operator questions, packing, bike
  logistics and booking checks.
- `/blog/cycling-training-camp-preparation-guide` owns the pre-camp training and
  arrival-readiness intent.
- `/blog/cycling-training-camps-what-to-expect-guide` owns the structure of the
  training block and post-camp adaptation intent.
- `/training-camps` owns Roadman dates, price, availability and booking intent.

These are distinct jobs. The editorial pages should link to each other and the
commercial owner without repeating the same “what to expect” promise.

## Problems in the previous version

The old article mixed useful Roadman operating experience with unsupported
rules for every camp. It prescribed six months of riding and a 90km threshold,
promised a reader would not be the slowest rider, called two pace groups and a
follow car the universal test of a serious camp, required 8.5 hours of sleep,
and recommended painkillers plus high-strength embrocation in a generic packing
list. It also invited readers to choose a road camp that the live booking system
now marks sold out.

## Decision

1. Lead with the exact broad query while defining the page as a first-timer
   decision guide.
2. Replace universal fitness thresholds with a comparison between the hardest
   published day and the rider's recent successive-day experience.
3. Turn pace, support, meals, rooms, transfers, bike hire and insurance into
   explicit questions for the operator.
4. Keep Roadman logistics labelled as Roadman's current format rather than the
   definition of a “proper” camp.
5. Ground fuelling, sleep and travel-health statements in named consensus and
   government sources.
6. Remove generic medication advice and stale commercial availability.
7. Declare the live camp page as the source of truth for dates, price and stock.

## Measurement

Compare the URL at the 7-day and 28-day checkpoints. Watch clicks, impressions,
CTR, average position, the exact `cycling training camp` query, first-timer
