## Plateau Type A: Training / FTP Plateau

**Who this is:** The rider whose FTP has been stuck for 6-24 months despite consistent training. Likely spending too much time in the grey zone. Maps to Persona 1 (Tom — The Plateau-Stuck Club Racer).

### Email 1 — Quick Win
- **{{plateau_summary}}:** Your diagnostic suggests your training intensity distribution is off. You're probably spending too much time at moderate intensity — too hard to build your aerobic engine, too easy to create the high-end adaptation you need. This is the single most common pattern I see in serious amateur cyclists.
- **{{quick_win}}:** On your next easy ride, drop your intensity by 10-15 watts below where you'd normally ride "easy." It should feel embarrassingly slow. That's the point. Professor Seiler's research shows that 80% of pro training happens at a pace most amateurs would consider a waste of time. It isn't. It's precisely what builds the aerobic base that makes everything else work.

### Email 2 — Case Study Emphasis
- Lead with FTP stagnation specifically. Emphasise that Chris's power numbers were flat for two years before the system. Highlight the grey zone diagnosis — this is their exact problem.

### Email 4 — Podcast Episode

- **{{podcast_episode_link}}:** Link to the Zone 2 / polarised training episode featuring Professor Seiler or the low-cadence torque training episode
- **{{podcast_episode_context}}:** This is the conversation where Professor Seiler breaks down exactly why most amateur cyclists train in the grey zone and what happens when you fix it. The research is clear — and the coaches behind Pogacar and Froome have been applying this for years.

## Plateau Type B: Nutrition / Body Composition

**Who this is:** The rider who knows their weight-to-power ratio is holding them back but can't figure out how to lose weight without losing power or feeling terrible on the bike. Body composition is the hidden universal motivator. Maps across all personas but especially Persona 3 (comeback athletes).

### Email 1 — Quick Win
- **{{plateau_summary}}:** Your diagnostic points to nutrition and body composition as your biggest limiter. Here's what that actually means: it's not that you eat too much. It's almost certainly that you're eating the wrong things at the wrong times — and the "eat less, ride more" advice you've probably heard is making it worse, not better.
- **{{quick_win}}:** Stop skipping breakfast before your morning rides. Fuel the session properly — even if weight loss is your goal. Under-fuelling your training doesn't make you leaner. It makes you slower, hungrier later, and more likely to reach for rubbish at 9pm. I lost 7kg in 12 weeks while eating more food than I'd ever eaten. The difference was what I ate and when.

### Email 2 — Case Study Emphasis
- Lead with Chris's body composition transformation: 84kg to 68kg, 20% body fat to 7%. Emphasise he ate MORE food. Emphasise power didn't drop — it doubled. This directly counters the "starve yourself thin" fear.

### Email 4 — Podcast Episode
- **{{plateau_topic}}:** nutrition and body composition
- **{{podcast_episode_link}}:** Link to the weight loss / fuelling episode or the "eating like a pro cyclist" episode
- **{{podcast_episode_context}}:** This is the episode where I break down exactly how I lost 7kg without tracking a single calorie, without skipping meals, and without fasted rides. The approach is the opposite of what the cycling internet tells you — and the science backs it up completely.

### Email 5 — Recap Framing
- Emphasise: "Your weight isn't the problem. Your fuelling strategy is. And that's completely fixable with the right system."

## Plateau Type C: Recovery / Fatigue

**Who this is:** The rider who trains hard but never feels fresh. Constantly fatigued, performance inconsistent, maybe picking up niggles and minor illnesses. Training through tiredness because rest feels like laziness. Maps to all personas, especially time-crunched professionals.

### Email 1 — Quick Win
- **{{plateau_summary}}:** Your diagnostic suggests recovery is your biggest limiter right now. And I don't mean you need a rest day — I mean the way your training is structured probably isn't giving your body enough time to adapt to the work you're doing. You're training hard. The problem is you're not recovering hard enough for that training to actually make you faster.
- **{{quick_win}}:** This week, replace one of your hard sessions with a genuinely easy ride — 60-90 minutes, conversational pace, absolutely no pushing. Not a "recovery" ride where you accidentally end up doing tempo. Actually easy. The adaptation happens in recovery, not in the session itself. The best coaches in the world — Dan Lorang, Tim Kerrison — they protect their athletes' recovery as aggressively as they prescribe the hard work.

### Email 2 — Case Study Emphasis
- Emphasise that Chris was overtraining before joining — doing too much, recovering too little. The system didn't just add better sessions; it managed his recovery, his load, and his periodisation properly.

### Email 4 — Podcast Episode
- **{{plateau_topic}}:** recovery and fatigue management
- **{{podcast_episode_link}}:** Link to the self-coaching mistakes episode or the recovery/sleep science episode
- **{{podcast_episode_context}}:** This is the conversation where we break down the five biggest mistakes self-coached cyclists make — and ignoring recovery is right at the top. The research on what happens when you manage training load properly versus just training more is genuinely eye-opening.

### Email 5 — Recap Framing

## Plateau Type D: No Structured Plan

**Who this is:** The rider who trains by feel, has no periodisation, no progression plan, and no accountability. Knows they should have a structured approach but doesn't know where to start or doesn't trust generic plans. Maps strongly to Persona 4 (the podcast listener who knows the theory but hasn't applied it).

### Email 1 — Quick Win
- **{{plateau_summary}}:** Your diagnostic is clear: you're training without a system. You ride when you can, push hard when it feels right, and hope the fitness comes. There's no periodisation, no progression, no structure connecting one week to the next. Here's the honest truth — this is the single biggest reason amateur cyclists plateau. Not talent. Not time. No system.
- **{{quick_win}}:** Before your next ride, decide what it's for. Not just "ride hard" or "go easy." Decide: is this a Zone 2 base ride? An interval session targeting a specific system? A recovery spin? Having a purpose for every single session — even if the plan is simple — is the difference between training and just riding your bike. The coaches I speak to on the podcast all say the same thing: specificity and intention beat volume every time.

- Emphasise that before joining, Chris was essentially training blind — putting in hours with no structure. The transformation didn't come from training more. It came from training with a purpose behind every session.
