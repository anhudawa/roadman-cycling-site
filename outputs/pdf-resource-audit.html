Specific Audit Checks

Do the 4 architecture templates have different content?

NO — They are functionally identical.

All four templates have the same Seiler quote, the same 4 rules, the same day-by-day structure, and the same two empty fields at the bottom. The only differences are session durations (e.g. Tuesday Z2 goes from 30-45min → 45-60min → 1hr → 1hr-1:15). Each file is exactly 306 words. Even the page 2 is identical across all four (blank branded footer page). A premium course should offer genuinely different training philosophy per bracket — a 6-hour rider has fundamentally different constraints and priorities than a 12-hour rider.

Is the Session Library comprehensive enough for 12 weeks?

NO — It covers 10 session types but lacks progression guidance.

The library catalogues Z2 rides (3 types), Threshold/SS (3 types), High-Intensity (3 types), and Strength (3 phases). This is a solid foundation but: there's no guidance on when to progress from one session to another, no "if you can do X, move to Y" rules, no adaptation for different hour brackets, and no indoor/outdoor variants. For 12 weeks of training, members need more than 10 sessions listed in a 2-page table.

Does the S&C Manual properly progress across 3 phases?

YES — Structurally correct, but too thin.

Phase 1 (Foundation, Wk 4-6) has 7 exercises with sets, reps, tempo, and form cues. Phase 2 (Build, Wk 7-9) progresses load and adds Step-Up + Side Plank Hip Dip. Phase 3 (Maintain, Wk 9-12) drops to 2 sets and removes one exercise. The progression logic is sound. However, 352 words across 2 pages is not a "manual" — it's a quick-reference card. No warm-up protocol, no cool-down, no mobility work, no substitutions for equipment limitations, no injury modification guidance.

Are Entry and Exit assessments properly mirrored?

YES — Same 6 domains, comparison-ready.

Both cover: Power, Training Distribution, Body Composition, Sleep, Available Hours/Strength, and Goal. The Exit adds Week 1 vs Week 12 columns for direct comparison. Structurally sound. The Entry is stronger (342w with explanatory context vs 151w pure tables).

Making Weak PDFs Premium-Quality

General Principles

  • Add "Why" sections: Every PDF currently jumps straight to empty fields. Add 2-3 sentences explaining why this tool matters, grounded in expert quotes from the podcast.
  • Add worked examples: Show what a completed version looks like before the blank template. "Tom, 42, Cat 3, 8hrs/week" worked through the whole form.
  • Add "Common Mistakes" callouts: 3 things most people get wrong. This is where Anthony's coaching expertise differentiates from free blog content.
  • Minimum 2 pages per PDF: No single-page PDFs with just headers and empty fields. If it's a form, pair it with instruction.
  • Target 300-500 words minimum per PDF: Currently averaging 163 words. Doubling the average across 46 PDFs would bring the library from 7,500 to ~15,000-20,000 words — still modest but substantive.

Highest-Impact Upgrades (by module)

  • Module 2 — Architecture Templates: Make each genuinely different. 6hr rider gets "ruthless prioritisation" guidance. 12hr rider gets "volume management and recovery" guidance. Add worked example weeks for each bracket.
  • Module 3 — Race Week Nutrition: Expand from 139 words to a proper 7-day carb-loading protocol with actual meals, portions, and timing by hour.
  • Module 4 — S&C Manual: Expand from 2 pages to 6-8 pages. Add warm-up, cool-down, substitutions, progression rules, and video QR codes linking to demo content.
  • Module 5 — HRV Guide: Expand from 123 words to a proper interpretation framework with "green/amber/red" decision rules and screenshots of what patterns look like.
  • Module 6 — Pacing Cards: Expand each scenario (climb/TT/group/sportive) to its own card with specific power targets, HR zones, and mental cues.
  • Module 7 — Season Plan: Add a completed example season plan showing Base→Build→Peak→Race for a typical spring/summer target event.
  • Module 8 — ALL THREE PDFs: Body comp is the hidden universal motivator. Add sample deficit-day and full-fuel-day meal plans with macros. Add a visual plate guide. Add a decision framework for "should I cut weight now or wait?"
  • Module 10 — Self-Talk Framework: Add actual scripts: "When the voice says X, replace with Y." Include 5-6 common suffering scenarios with specific reframes.

Value Perception at $297–397

The uncomfortable truth: At current depth, these 46 PDFs contain roughly the same amount of content as a single well-produced free lead magnet from a competitor like TrainerRoad or FasCat. A $297–397 buyer — especially Persona 1 (Tom) who has already been burned by generic coaching products — will feel disappointed when they download a 76-word PDF. The protocol MDX content is strong (23,000+ words of genuine coaching depth). The PDFs need to match that standard.

What "premium" looks like at this price point

  • Competitors at this price include TrainerRoad annual ($190), FasCat coaching plans ($199-499), and Sufferfest/Wahoo SYSTM annual ($180). All include substantial documentation.
  • Premium coaching PDFs should feel like they were written by a coach for YOU — not like a form you could have made yourself in Google Docs.
  • The brand positioning is "World Tour intelligence, applied to your life." The PDFs currently deliver "empty forms with the Roadman logo on them."
  • At minimum, each PDF should include: a purpose statement, an expert quote, instructions/guidance, a worked example, the blank template, and a "common mistakes" callout.
