## Structure

Sacred Cow arc: contrarian hook (1) → villain (2–4) → insider credibility (3) → evidence layer (5–7) → the honest reckoning (8) → universal principle (9–10) → personal story (11) → cultural critique & call (12).

### Ch 1 — The strongest year of your life is being wasted (5–7 pp)
**Job:** Pain, named precisely. Plateau Tom recognises himself before page 3.
The rider who does everything right — structure, consistency, data — and has been at the same FTP for three seasons. The quiet shame of effort without yield. The diagnosis everyone offers him: *more*.
**Sources:** ep-2105 (Dale's stagnation case), ep-2039 Schrot (time-limited athletes train too hard *and* under-recover), ep-2032 host's own numbers as a teaser for Ch 11.
**Archive support:** moderate–strong.

### Ch 2 — The model you inherited (6–8 pp)
**Job:** Introduce the villain honestly. Base–build–peak as taught by its own populariser.
Friel on record: "you really cannot have too much base… the longer we can make the base period the better" (ep-40). The pyramid: wider base, higher peak. Then the question the model never answers: base built out of *what hours*?
**Sources:** ep-40 (canonical text — Friel defending the model in 2025), ep-2 (host tracing the pyramid idea's reach), club lore ("small ring until January") from is-base-training-dead.
**Needs:** external history sourcing per Decision 4. **Archive support:** strong.

### Ch 3 — Nobody at the World Tour trains the way you think (6–8 pp)
**Job:** Insider credibility. The people who run pro training say two things amateurs don't expect: it's less exotic than you imagine, and they *volunteer* that it doesn't transfer.
Barrett: "a lot of my job really is bringing things back to basics" (ep-38). Wakefield: "I'm not entirely sure we train correctly… our training hasn't evolved" (ep-2132). Lorang reframing elite lessons for amateurs (ep-2134). Impey/Dunne on pro fuelling and physiology that amateurs can't copy (ep-2035/2044).
**Sources:** ep-38, ep-2132, ep-2134, ep-2044, ep-2035. **Archive support:** strong.
**Blocklist note:** Lorang appears in this chapter. His association is stated ONLY as Red Bull–Bora head of performance / Roglič's coach (verified in ep-2134). Nothing else.

### Ch 4 — The arithmetic nobody runs (5–7 pp)
**Job:** The villain's hidden assumption, made explicit. A base phase is a volume instrument; volume is the one input Tom doesn't have. 25 hours vs 9. The model isn't wrong — it's *addressed to someone else*. Friel's Martians line ("they're not really humans, they're Martians") carries it.
Introduce Seiler's three levers — frequency, duration, intensity — and the diagnosis: "what is the order that we use? We go out hard… we use the intensity lever almost from day one, and then we get hit in the face with it" (ep-2148).
**Sources:** ep-2148, ep-2205, ep-40. **Archive support:** strong.

### Ch 5 — Eighty-twenty doesn't shrink (6–8 pp)
**Job:** Evidence layer I. The distribution that survives the time cut.
Seiler's 80/20 across elite sport; the grey-zone trap ("your training just falls into this gray zone all week," ep-2148); the killer transfer claim — host: at six hours a week, still 80% easy? Friel: "Yes, you are" (ep-40). Astana's head of performance calling 90% of base "really easy" and Friel: "I do exactly the same thing."
**Sources:** ep-2148, ep-2095, ep-40, ep-38, ep-2027. **Archive support:** strong (best in archive).

### Ch 6 — The body you're actually training (6–8 pp)
**Job:** Evidence layer II. Masters physiology without fatalism.
Friel: VO2max decline starts mid-30s, upper-body atrophy, fat gain — "the worst of both worlds" (ep-2205). Lipman: endurance persists, fast-twitch goes first (ep-2154). Then the counter-punch — Galpin: "that plasticity just doesn't stop"; fast-twitch is reclaimable at any age. The disagreement between Lipman/Friel (fibre loss as primary enemy) and Galpin (overrated) presented as live science, not smoothed over.
**Sources:** ep-2205, Galpin ep, ep-2154. **Needs:** quantified decline figure → Decision 4. **Archive support:** strong.

### Ch 7 — You don't periodise volume. You periodise recovery. (7–9 pp)
**Job:** The thesis chapter. The actual inversion.
Wakefield's flagship: amateurs can't raise frequency or duration, so when form dips they yank the intensity lever — "4x4 becomes 6x4… and we dig further into that hole. The best way to get out of a hole is to stop digging the hole" (ep-2132). Asked what to copy from Bora: "I would take rest as your key take-home." Friel's 9-day cycle — the first structurally *masters* periodisation in the book (ep-2205). Seiler's stress bucket: the nervous system doesn't itemise job, kids, intervals (ep-2148). Galpin: progress is inverse to volume when recovery capacity is spoken for.
**Sources:** ep-2132, ep-2205, ep-2148, Galpin ep. **Archive support:** strong.

### Ch 8 — What the evidence doesn't say (5–6 pp)
**Job:** The honest reckoning. Attack our own position before the reader can.
Seiler ran the comparison: reverse-ordered intensity blocks vs traditional. "We couldn't find a statistical difference, but to the extent that there tended to be a difference, the traditional model worked well" (ep-2095). Friel hasn't abandoned base. Attia and the host disagree about where the hard 20% goes (ep-2). What survives all of it: the lever order, the 80/20 at low volume, recovery as the constraint. What doesn't: calendar-flipping as dogma. The reader learns the difference between a manifesto and a sales letter.
**Sources:** ep-2095, ep-40, ep-2 (Attia reaction). **Archive support:** this chapter IS the archive's tensions. **Needs Decision 2.**

### Ch 9 — The work your dashboard can't see (5–7 pp)
**Job:** Strength, and the start of the cultural critique of metric-worship.
Friel: for the ageing athlete, gym work is "essential rather than optional" (ep-2205). Teel: heavy lifting generates days of adaptation cost "that you would never ever get credit for a TSS score" (ep-2183) — the system literally cannot count the most important work the masters athlete does. Lipman: two to three sessions a week can more than offset decline. Barrett's episode title does the rest: what FTP misses.
**Sources:** ep-2205, ep-2183, ep-2154, Galpin ep, ep-38. **Archive support:** strong.

### Ch 10 — Consistency is the only metric that survives (4–6 pp)
**Job:** Universal principle. Zoom out from mechanism to law.
Seiler: zoom out on elite or amateur careers and what defines long-term success is consistency — can you adhere? (via ep-38). Barrett: "the most effective training program is the one you can stick to." Seiler's own "my secret is I get the work done" (ep-2148 — verify verbatim against audio). Galpin closing the loop: a plan you won't comply with breaks the consistency loop, and consistency is what gives the outcome. Bigham's fragment as seasoning, not load-bearing (evidence map says don't over-weight).
**Sources:** ep-38, ep-2148, Galpin ep, ep-2106 (light). **Archive support:** strong.

### Ch 11 — I cut my training in half (4–6 pp)
**Job:** Personal story. Anthony's own inversion, with real numbers and real cost.
Skeleton from ep-2032 and ep-2074; flesh from Anthony (Decision 3).
**Archive support:** moderate — deliberately; this chapter runs on first person.

### Ch 12 — Not done yet (4–6 pp)
**Job:** Cultural critique and call. The industry sells masters athletes a youth template and calls their failure a discipline problem. Wakefield: "maybe do less, bring the fun back into it, and you'll actually have a better reward" (ep-2132). What a masters methodology owes its athlete. Where this connects to the Plateau Diagnostic and NDY — stated plainly, no funnel theatrics: the reader was promised an argument, he got one, here's where it continues.
**Sources:** ep-2132, ep-2154 (Lipman's "you get old because you stopped doing sport"), NDY material. **Archive support:** moderate.
