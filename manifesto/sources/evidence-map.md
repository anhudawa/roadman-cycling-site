Speaker attributions below are **inferred from context** and flagged `[UNVERIFIED ASR — speaker inferred: X]`. Treat all as candidate passages to be re-checked against audio before quoting in the paper.

## THEME 10 — Expert DISAGREEMENTS (surface these explicitly)

**Strength: MODERATE.** Real disagreements exist and should be presented as content rather than smoothed over.

1. **Reverse vs traditional order — Seiler contradicts the manifesto's preferred direction.** In ep-2095 (~line 485) Seiler reports his own intervention found *"to the extent that there tended to be a difference the traditional model worked well."* Yet in ep-2148 the host frames the recommended week as "reverse periodization." **The manifesto's strongest single source is equivocal on its central mechanism — address head-on.**

2. **"You can't have too much base" (Friel, ep-40) vs intensity-year-round (host, is-base-training-dead; Seiler's grey-zone caution).** Friel still defends a long 12-week+ base; the host's 2020 solo argues intensity belongs all year. Not a flat contradiction (Friel keeps 80/20 easy *within* base) but a difference of emphasis worth showing.

3. **Fast-twitch loss: how much it matters.** Galpin (~line 536) argues fast-twitch lost *to endurance training* is "not that big of a deal" and those athletes "aging insanely well"; Lipman (~line 544) and Friel (ep-2205) treat fibre/muscle loss as a primary problem to fight. Different risk weightings on the same fact.

4. **Peter Attia zone-2 reaction (ep-2 `roadman-reacts-to-attias-horrible-zone-2-advice`).** Host disagrees with Attia on prescribing 8-min VO2 intervals and the "20% all into VO2/threshold" split (~line 191): "I don't think you take the 20% and you put it into V2 Max or threshold I think that depends on what [the rider needs]." A documented cross-source disagreement on how to spend the hard 20%.

5. **Aero vs sustainability for amateurs (Wakefield, ep-2132 ~line 210):** Wakefield half-disagrees with an aggressive-aero recommendation — for amateurs "anything aerodynamic is based on whether you can hold that position... if they can't hold the position it's pointless." Pro-derived aero advice that doesn't transfer.

## GAPS (thin or absent support)

- **"Reverse periodisation" as a *validated* superior model:** ABSENT/CONTRADICTED. The only controlled comparison in the archive (Seiler, ep-2095) favoured the traditional order. The archive supports *year-round intensity* and *inverting the amateur's lever order* far better than it supports literal reverse-periodised blocks. Frame the paper accordingly.
- **Lydiard / named classic periodisation lineage:** THIN. Friel is credited as the populariser; no substantive discussion of Lydiard, Bompa, or the pro-25h-week origin found by keyword sweep. The "designed for young pros with 25h weeks" premise is *implied* (Friel's "Martians" line, ep-2205 ~line 342: elite athletes "they're not really humans they're martians... they don't need the same things") but never stated as a historical claim. Manifesto will need external sourcing for the origins history.
- **Dan Bigham (ep-2106):** THIN for our themes — episode is ~90% aerodynamics. Usable fragment only: consistency/durability over time drove his physiological gains (~line 477): "training consistency and getting the volume in because that's where the long-term improvements come." Do not over-weight.
- **Hard numeric "minimum effective dose" prescriptions:** THIN. Sources give principles (one quality session/wk + easy volume; 5 rides/wk; 80/20 holds at 6h) but no rigorously-defended minimum-dose study. `minimum effective dose` keyword hits are mostly tangential.
- **VO2max decline quantified (%/decade):** ABSENT as a hard figure in transcript text. Friel/Lipman describe the trajectory qualitatively; no clean "~10%/decade" type stat verified.
- **ep-2177 (Friel fat-loss clip), ep-2035 (Impey fuelling):** not deeply mined — relevant to nutrition/body-comp sub-arguments but peripheral to the periodisation core. Revisit if the chapter outline needs a fuelling section.
