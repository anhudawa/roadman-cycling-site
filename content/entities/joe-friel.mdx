Joe Friel is an endurance coach, author and co-founder of TrainingPeaks. His official biography says he has coached or advised amateur and professional endurance athletes since 1980, holds a master's degree in exercise science and co-founded the service that became TrainingPeaks with Dirk Friel and Gear Fisher in 1999.

His best-known cycling book is *The Cyclist's Training Bible*. In Friel's account of the fifth-edition rewrite, periodisation remains central, but the method is not one universal schedule. The book presents several seasonal-planning options and adapts them to the rider's event demands and cycling phenotype. It also uses power, heart rate and perceived exertion as complementary signals.

That distinction matters because pages carrying Friel's name often turn a planning framework into a rigid internet rule. In his Roadman interview about the “ideal” training week, Friel did not prescribe one perfect week for everybody. He discussed how the plan must respond to the athlete, the target and the recovery available.

For older riders, the second Roadman conversation covers a narrower question: how to preserve performance after 40. It should be read as an expert interview about priorities—not as a universal medical prescription or a substitute for an individual assessment.
