Stephen Seiler is a Professor of Sport Science at the University of Agder in Kristiansand, Norway. His research is widely associated with training-intensity distribution and with describing the polarised pattern found in some successful endurance programmes.

The most important boundary is the denominator. Seiler's 2010 review described athletes training 10–13 times per week and an approximate split in which 80% of **sessions** were low intensity while 20% were dominated by high-intensity work. That is not the same as commanding every cyclist to spend exactly 80% and 20% of total training minutes in two power zones.

The evidence is also more varied than the slogan. Seiler and Kjerland's 2006 observational study followed 11 well-trained junior cross-country skiers for 32 days. A 2023 systematic review of elite-to-world-class endurance athletes found both pyramidal and polarised distributions, varying by sport and season phase. The durable common feature was a high proportion of low-intensity work.

For cyclists, the practical value is a decision framework: protect genuinely easy endurance work, apply high intensity deliberately, define zones using meaningful physiological thresholds, and avoid treating one neat percentage as a law.
