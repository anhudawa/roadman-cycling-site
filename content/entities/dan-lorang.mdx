## Current role

Dan Lorang is the **Head of Performance at Lidl-Trek**. The team announced the appointment on 2 June 2026 and made the new structure effective on 1 August. Its [current staff roster](https://racing.trekbikes.com/team/lidl-trek) lists him in that role.

Lidl-Trek says his remit spans coaching, nutrition, health, recovery, athletics, and data and AI. He also leads the Crivit Performance Center. That wording matters: it describes a broad performance-leadership position. It does not mean he is the day-to-day personal coach of every Lidl-Trek rider.

Older Roadman pages and the two podcast recordings refer to Lorang's previous job at Bora–Hansgrohe, later Red Bull–Bora–Hansgrohe. That was accurate when the interviews were recorded. It is not his current team.

## Coaching background

Lorang is Luxembourgish and studied sports science. In a 2023 first-person interview with That Triathlon Show, he described nearly two decades in triathlon and discussed his work with Lucy Charles-Barclay and Anne Haug, as well as Jan Frodeno up to Frodeno's retirement.

His public record crosses professional cycling and long-course triathlon. Roadman therefore treats him as a source on endurance coaching and performance systems—not as the source for every training claim that circulates under his name.

## What he actually said on Roadman

Roadman's 2024 interview focused on what elite practice can and cannot teach a working amateur. Lorang's central point was consistency: repeatable training requires load and recovery to remain in balance. He also said amateurs should retain a larger margin below the limits professional athletes accept for performance.

The 2025 conversation started with priority races and planning backwards. Lorang then discussed tapering by athlete profile, the importance of work and family stress, and why riders over 40 may need closer attention to total recoverable load. He described power, heart rate and perceived exertion as complementary signals rather than interchangeable numbers.

Those interviews support a decision framework, not a generic “Dan Lorang plan.” They do **not** establish a universal 80/20 split, a fixed two-hard-session ceiling, guaranteed FTP gains in 8–12 weeks, or one prescription for all riders.
