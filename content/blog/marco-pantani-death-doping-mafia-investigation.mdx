Marco Pantani was found dead on 14 February 2004 in room D5 of the Le Rose residence in Rimini, Italy. He was 34.

The original investigation attributed his death to **acute cocaine intoxication with pulmonary and cerebral oedema**. Italian authorities treated accidental death as the more plausible initial explanation. Later inquiries examined homicide allegations, but those allegations are not the formal cause-of-death finding.

That answer is separate from the dispute that began five years earlier at the Giro d'Italia.

## The two Pantani questions

Search results often collapse two different files into one mystery:

1. Was the blood-control process that removed Pantani from the 1999 Giro reliable?
2. Did anyone else cause Pantani's death in Rimini in 2004?

The evidential status is different. Italy's parliamentary anti-mafia commission documented serious problems with the 1999 control and considered sample manipulation plausible. Criminal inquiries into the 2004 death have not displaced the original toxicology-based cause.

## What happened at Madonna di Campiglio in 1999?

Pantani entered 5 June 1999 leading the Giro by 5 minutes 38 seconds. A blood sample taken that morning returned an initial hematocrit value of 53%, then 52% on the repeated check. The UCI health threshold was 50%, so Pantani received a precautionary stop and left the race.

This was a health-based suspension under the rules then in force, not a direct positive test for EPO. The distinction matters when describing the event.

The [Italian Parliament's 2022 report](https://www.parlamento.it/application/xmanager/projects/parlamento/file/Doc.%20XXIII%2C%20n.%2037%20%28Sez.%20II-bis%29.pdf) records three other results that became central to the dispute:

- Pantani's team said a portable check the previous evening read 48%.
- Tests at the UCI-accredited hospital in Imola later on 5 June read 48.1% and 47.6%.
- The platelet count in the Madonna sample differed sharply from the later Imola results.

Those differences do not prove manipulation by themselves. They explain why the process was investigated.

## What the parliamentary commission found

The commission said the protocol required collection and analysis rooms in the same hotel. Pantani's blood was collected at Hotel Touring and analysed at Hotel Majestic, more than a kilometre away.

It also recorded problems with anonymity, sample-code order, the timing used in an earlier court judgment and the delay before results. In its conclusion, the commission said numerous anomalies marked the event and that serious rule violations had weakened safeguards designed to prevent alteration.

The report went further and called manipulation plausible. That is a parliamentary finding based on its inquiry; it is not a criminal conviction and it did not identify a person legally responsible.

## What changed in May 2026?

Prosecutors in Trento had reopened an inquiry into alleged organised-crime and clandestine-betting links around the 1999 control. On **16 May 2026**, a judge archived that inquiry.

The [reported reason for archiving](https://corrieredeltrentino.corriere.it/notizie/cronaca/26_maggio_16/morte-marco-pantani-il-giudice-archivia-il-fascicolo-su-mafia-e-scommesse-e-passato-troppo-tempo-8747fe85-a227-47c3-98be-39eb33ffexlk_amp.shtml) was that the evidence gathered was insufficient to sustain an accusation. The passage of almost 27 years, unavailable witnesses and faded recollections limited what prosecutors could prove.

An archived inquiry does not establish that every 1999 procedure was sound. It means the criminal evidence did not support moving forward with an accusation.

## What is established about Pantani's death?

The parliamentary report summarises the first Rimini investigation: medico-legal experts attributed the death to acute cocaine intoxication, with pulmonary and cerebral oedema, in the context of Pantani's health and prior drug use. Authorities considered an accidental death more plausible than suicide and prosecuted people alleged to have supplied cocaine.

A second inquiry opened after Pantani's family alleged homicide. A Rimini judge archived it after concluding that the evidence supported Pantani's voluntary, autonomous consumption of large quantities of cocaine and antidepressant medication. A 2017 appeal was ruled inadmissible.

Later testimony and scene-handling concerns prompted another Rimini inquiry. Reporting on 16 May 2026 said that file remained open. Roadman has found no later authoritative closure notice, so the accurate status is date-stamped: **open in the latest report we could verify, not “open forever” and not proof of homicide**.

## Did organised crime cause either event?

The 2022 commission investigated whether illegal betting interests connected to organised crime could have benefited from Pantani leaving the 1999 Giro. It documented allegations and procedural anomalies. The subsequent Trento criminal inquiry did not obtain evidence sufficient to sustain an accusation and was archived.

For the 2004 death, the original and second Rimini inquiries did not establish homicide. The later Rimini file is the proper place for any new evidence to be tested. A responsible account does not convert an open file into a verdict.

## The Roadman conclusion

Three statements can all be true:

- The formal cause of Pantani's death was acute cocaine intoxication.
- The 1999 blood-control procedure had serious documented anomalies.
- The Trento criminal inquiry still closed without sufficient evidence to accuse anyone.
