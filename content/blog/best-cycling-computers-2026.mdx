The best bike computer in 2026 is not the one with the longest feature list. It is the one whose controls, screen, mapping and battery fit the rides you actually do—and whose workout workflow fits the platform you already use.

This guide compares nine current options: Garmin Edge 1050, Edge 850, Edge 550 and Edge Explore 2; Wahoo ELEMNT ACE, ROAM 3 and BOLT 3; Hammerhead Karoo; and COROS DURA. It corrects three recurring problems in stale buyer guides: imaginary Edge 1050 solar charging, discontinued Wahoo V2 models presented as current, and battery claims compared as if every manufacturer ran the same test.

## Garmin Edge: four different jobs

Garmin has the broadest native training and navigation ecosystem in this comparison. The current Edge 1050, 850 and 550 share Garmin's performance-led direction; Edge Explore 2 is the simpler navigation-led alternative.

### Garmin Edge 1050

The Edge 1050 is Garmin's large flagship. Garmin specifies a 3.5-inch 480x800 LCD touchscreen, 161g weight, 64 GB storage and up to 20 hours in demanding use or 60 hours in battery-saver mode. It adds a built-in speaker, bike bell, Garmin Pay, on-device course creation and Garmin's training and navigation stack. The figures are listed in [Garmin's current comparison data](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/compare/?compareProduct=1196129&compareProduct=1630197&compareProduct=1630737).

One correction matters: **the Edge 1050 does not have solar charging**. Garmin's solar large-screen model is the Edge 1040 Solar. Choose 1050 for screen clarity, maps and Garmin integration—not imaginary battery hardware.

### Garmin Edge 850

Edge 850 is the compact Garmin for riders who want touch and buttons. Its 2.7-inch 420x600 LCD, 113g weight, speaker and Garmin Pay sit alongside a 12-hour demanding-use or 36-hour saver claim. Touch makes map movement easier; buttons remain predictable in rain and gloves. It is the compact performance pick when 12 hours covers the job with margin.

### Garmin Edge 550

Edge 550 is the 110g button-only sibling. It has the same display size and resolution class and the same battery claims, without touch. Buttons are not automatically a downgrade: they reduce accidental interactions and work consistently in difficult weather. Choose it when Garmin Connect and current Garmin training features matter more than Wahoo BOLT 3's longer headline claim.

### Garmin Edge Explore 2

Edge Explore 2 is easy to miss because it is not the performance flagship. Garmin lists a 3-inch 240x400 touchscreen, 104g weight, 16 GB storage, up to 16 hours in demanding use and 24 hours in saver mode. It supports bike-specific maps, turn-by-turn directions, on-device location search, e-bike routing and Varia compatibility. Those details are on Garmin's [current Edge Explore 2 product page](https://www.garmin.com/en-IE/p/802162/pn/010-02703-10/).

Choose it for simpler touch navigation and Garmin value. Do not assume it has every training-analysis feature of 1050, 850 or 550 merely because the name starts with Edge.

## Wahoo ELEMNT: ACE, ROAM 3 and BOLT 3

Wahoo's current road generation is ACE, ROAM 3 and BOLT 3. Wahoo identifies ROAM V2 and BOLT V2 as no longer in production, so a new-buying guide centred on the V2 generation is stale.

All three current devices use the Wahoo app and support routes, planned workouts and completed-ride syncing with partners. Wahoo's [planned-workout documentation](https://support.wahoofitness.com/hc/en-us/articles/22710662013458-Planned-workouts-with-ELEMNT-ACE-BOLT-3-and-ROAM-3) explains how providers including TrainingPeaks connect.

### Wahoo ELEMNT ACE

ACE is Wahoo's largest head unit: a 3.8-inch 480x720 touchscreen with physical buttons, 64 GB storage, 208g weight and an official battery claim of up to 30 hours. It also includes a wind sensor. The size and weight are substantial, but so are the screen and battery claim. See [Wahoo's current ACE product information](https://support.wahoofitness.com/hc/en-us/articles/22709312101650-ELEMNT-ACE-2024-Product-Information).

Choose ACE when you want the largest Wahoo interface and regularly need all-day battery margin. It is not the compact or light option.

### Wahoo ELEMNT ROAM 3

ROAM 3 is the balanced Wahoo: a 2.8-inch 320x480 touchscreen with physical buttons, 64 GB storage, 109g weight, voice turn prompts and an official claim of up to 25 hours. Those figures come from [Wahoo's ROAM 3 product information](https://support.wahoofitness.com/hc/en-us/articles/26243291046034-ELEMNT-ROAM-3-2025-Product-Information).

That combination suits long sportives, gravel events and riders who preload routes and planned sessions. It gives up Garmin's wider native analysis in exchange for a focused workflow and a stronger standard-use headline claim than the comparable compact Garmins.

### Wahoo ELEMNT BOLT 3

BOLT 3 is the compact Wahoo: a 2.3-inch 240x360 colour display, physical buttons, 32 GB storage, 84g weight, dual-band GPS and up to 20 hours. See [Wahoo's official BOLT 3 specifications](https://support.wahoofitness.com/hc/en-us/articles/26243351942290-ELEMNT-BOLT-3-2025-Product-Information).

For a coached cyclist, it covers the essential job: show the prescribed session, record power and heart rate, follow a loaded route and sync the completed file. It is Roadman's compact training-value pick, subject to live local pricing.

## Hammerhead Karoo

The current Hammerhead device is marketed as **Karoo**, not “Karoo 3.” Its specification advantage is the display: 3.2 inches, 800x480 and 292 pixels per inch, protected by anti-reflective Corning Gorilla Glass. Touch can be locked so physical buttons handle wet or muddy conditions. See Hammerhead's [display](https://support.hammerhead.io/hc/en-us/articles/25696850944795-Karoo-Display) and [hardware specifications](https://support.hammerhead.io/hc/en-us/articles/25687624385691-Karoo-Hardware-Specifications).

Karoo weighs 118.1g, has 64 GB total storage, multi-band GNSS and an IP67 rating. TrainingPeaks workouts and compatible ANT+ FE-C trainer control are supported. Hammerhead describes a typical expected battery range of 9 to 20-plus hours because display and configuration have a large effect.

Choose Karoo when map interaction, screen density and a software-led interface matter more than headline endurance.

## COROS DURA

COROS DURA changes the battery comparison. COROS lists a 2.7-inch MIP colour touchscreen, digital dial and button, 102g weight, dual-frequency satellite support, global offline maps, turn-by-turn navigation, Bluetooth and ANT+ connectivity. It supports power meters, radar and structured workouts built in the COROS app or Training Hub. The current features are documented on the [official DURA page](https://coros.com/dura).

Its 120-hour headline is real within COROS's stated scenario: a standard daytime ride in all-systems GPS without solar. COROS also claims up to two extra hours of riding per hour of solar charging in optimal direct sun. Neither number is a promise for every setup. The detailed table estimates 67 hours with maps, navigation and three accessories in all-systems GPS, and 49 hours in the same use case with dual-frequency GPS.

Choose DURA when audax, bikepacking or multi-day battery margin is the dominant requirement. Garmin, Wahoo and Hammerhead can still be better choices when their navigation, display or training ecosystem removes more weekly friction.

## What actually decides the purchase

### 1. The job, not the score

- Deep Garmin training and map tools: Edge 1050, 850 or 550.
- Simpler touch navigation: Edge Explore 2.
- Focused planned-workout delivery: Wahoo ROAM 3 or BOLT 3.
- Largest Wahoo interface: ACE.
- High-density display and map interaction: Karoo.
- Ultra-endurance battery margin: DURA.

No weighted review score can decide how much each of those jobs matters to you.

### 2. Your longest repeated ride

Buy for the longest ride you do often enough to matter, then leave margin for cold weather, navigation, sensors, battery ageing and forgotten charging. A two-hour rider does not need a 120-hour claim. A multi-day rider should not plan around an “up to” figure that excludes their navigation and accessories.

### 3. Touchscreen, buttons or both

Touch is quicker for map movement. Buttons are more predictable in rain and gloves. Dual-control units provide both but usually cost or weigh more. Year-round riders should treat controls as a safety and usability decision, not a showroom preference.

### 4. Where the training plan lives

If Garmin Connect is the source of truth, Garmin's native metrics and history make switching costly. If TrainingPeaks and a coach determine the plan, Garmin, current Wahoo and Karoo can deliver prescribed sessions; COROS supports structured workouts through its app and Training Hub, with third-party integrations depending on the service and current software. Confirm the exact provider workflow before purchase rather than assuming every sync behaves identically.

### 5. Price only after the shortlist

Prices vary by country, bundle and promotion. Decide the device class first, then compare live local prices. An evergreen guide that declares one temporary price a permanent “budget winner” will age badly.
