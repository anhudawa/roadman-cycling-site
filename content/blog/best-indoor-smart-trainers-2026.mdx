The best indoor smart trainer is the one that fits your bike, works with your chosen training app and is ready when the session starts. A trainer with a bigger power number is not a better purchase if the freehub is wrong, virtual shifting does not work in your app or the connection keeps dropping.

This 2026 guide compares six current direct-drive models: Wahoo KICKR CORE 2 and KICKR v6, Garmin Tacx NEO 3M, Elite Justo 2 and Avanti, and JetBlack VICTORY. The specifications below are current manufacturer claims. Roadman's picks are editorial recommendations by use case; they are not presented as independent laboratory test results.

## Wahoo KICKR CORE 2: best for most riders

Wahoo's current CORE is the **KICKR CORE 2**, part number WFBKTR125. The important upgrade is not a larger headline power number; it is the connection and setup package. Wahoo added Wi-Fi and its KICKR Bridge sensor gateway while retaining Bluetooth, ANT+ and ANT+ FE-C. The official specification is +/-2% accuracy, 1,800 W and a 16% simulated grade.

The CORE 2 is also available in several drivetrain configurations: without a cassette, with an 8–12-speed Shimano-compatible cassette, or with Zwift Cog and Click. That makes it the best-for-most choice because buyers can start with the required drivetrain rather than immediately replacing bundled hardware. See [Wahoo's current KICKR CORE 2 specifications](https://support.wahoofitness.com/hc/en-us/articles/28096790110354-KICKR-CORE-2-Smart-Trainer-Information-FAQ).

Choose it when you want a current, connected direct-drive trainer without paying for the premium models' motion or maximum-resistance features. Do not assume the word “CORE” identifies one product: the original CORE does not have the CORE 2's Wi-Fi hardware.

## Garmin Tacx NEO 3M: best for motion and simulated surfaces

The correct current model name is **Tacx NEO 3M**, not “Tacx NEO 3.” Its distinctive feature is built-in multidirectional motion that can be switched on or off. Garmin also supports simulated surfaces such as gravel and cobblestones in compatible software.

Garmin states less than 1% accuracy, 2,200 W maximum power and a 25% simulated incline. The NEO 3M requires no calibration and can provide progressive resistance without mains power, although powered app control requires the normal setup. Bluetooth and ANT+ are standard; Wi-Fi and Ethernet require Garmin's optional Tacx smart network adapter. Those details are documented on the [Garmin Tacx NEO 3M product page](https://www.garmin.com/en-GB/p/885302/).

Choose it when natural bike movement, surface effects and calibration-free operation justify the premium. Check cassette compatibility carefully: the standard package includes an 11-speed Shimano/SRAM cassette, while other drivetrains may require a cassette or freehub change.

## Elite Justo 2: best premium specification

Elite's flagship **Justo 2** combines an integrated optical torque sensor, less than 1% claimed accuracy, automatic calibration and built-in Wi-Fi. Elite specifies 2,300 W at 40 km/h and a 24% simulated grade. The trainer includes two Flex Feet sets and a pre-installed 12-speed road cassette; it also supports Zwift Cog and Click after the required setup.

The reason to choose the Justo 2 is not that every rider needs 2,300 W. It is the complete premium package: high stated accuracy, automatic calibration, several network options and lateral movement without buying a separate platform. The [Elite Justo 2 specification](https://www.elite-it.com/en/home-trainers/smart-trainers/justo-2) lists the current cassette, power, grade and connection details.

Check whether the included 12-speed road cassette matches your bike. A bundled cassette only saves time when the chain and drivetrain are compatible.

## Wahoo KICKR v6: best for the established Wahoo ecosystem

The **KICKR v6**, released in 2022, remains Wahoo's current fixed premium KICKR in its support directory. Wahoo specifies +/-1% accuracy, 2,200 W, a 20% simulated grade, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ANT+ FE-C and a Direct Connect port. AXIS feet allow a small amount of side-to-side movement, and an 11-speed cassette is included.

Choose it when you value Wahoo's mature setup, app and accessory ecosystem or need its connection options. The [official KICKR v6 specification](https://support.wahoofitness.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000296704-KICKR-v6-2022-Smart-Trainer-Information-FAQ) also lists the included axle adapters and drivetrain support.

Do not pay for the 2,200 W or 20% grade in isolation. For most riders the meaningful differences from CORE 2 are the tighter stated accuracy, heavier flywheel, AXIS feet, included 11-speed cassette and premium construction.

## Elite Avanti: best specification-led alternative

The **Elite Avanti** is the interesting middle option in this set. Elite states +/-1% accuracy, 2,100 W at 40 km/h, an 18% simulated grade, automatic calibration and built-in Wi-Fi. It includes black Flex Feet for 4.5 degrees of lateral movement and supports two simultaneous Bluetooth channels.

Unlike the standard Justo 2, the standard Avanti does **not** include a cassette. That can be an advantage if you already know the exact cassette required, but it must be included in the real purchase and setup cost. Elite lists the supported Shimano/SRAM freehub combinations and optional Campagnolo and SRAM XD/XDR bodies in its [current Avanti documentation](https://www.elite-it.com/en/home-trainers/smart-trainers/avanti).

Choose the Avanti when its specification and local price undercut the premium trainers and you are comfortable supplying the correct cassette. A separate Zwift Ready version with Cog and Click may be available, so compare the exact bundle rather than the model name alone.

## JetBlack VICTORY: best Zwift-first value route

The current JetBlack model is **VICTORY**, not the older VOLT. The current bundle includes Zwift Cog and Click, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth FTMS, ANT+ FE-C, a Bluetooth sensor bridge and a 10 Hz race-response mode. JetBlack states +/-2% accuracy, 1,800 W and a 16% simulated grade. See the [JetBlack VICTORY product specification](https://www.jetblackcycling.com/indoor-cycle-trainers-and-accessories/victory-smart-trainer/) and [technical support page](https://support.jetblackcycling.com/what-are-the-victory-smart-trainer-tecnical-specifications).

There is an important compatibility catch. JetBlack says that riders using apps other than Zwift should remove the Cog and install the correct cassette. The VICTORY supports common Shimano/SRAM cassette arrangements, while SRAM XDR requires another freehub and Campagnolo is not supported according to JetBlack's current guidance. Read the [VICTORY app and cassette guidance](https://support.jetblackcycling.com/what-indoor-training-apps-is-victory-compatible-with) before buying.

Choose it when Zwift virtual shifting is the priority and the delivered local price is compelling. Include cassette tools, another cassette or a freehub in the comparison if you plan to use several platforms.

## Direct drive versus wheel-on: what the category does and does not tell you

A wheel-on trainer keeps the rear wheel in place and presses the tyre against a roller. A direct-drive trainer replaces the rear wheel and connects the chain to a cassette or single cog on the trainer.

Direct drive removes tyre wear, roller tension and tyre-pressure variation from the setup. It normally provides a more stable platform and is the practical default for frequent structured training. But three common claims go too far:

- **Direct drive does not guarantee +/-1% accuracy.** The current models here range from a claimed less than 1% to +/-2%.
- **Direct drive does not eliminate calibration.** Tacx NEO 3M requires none, Elite Justo 2 and Avanti calibrate automatically, while JetBlack still recommends manual calibration after unboxing, moving the trainer or a seasonal temperature change.
- **Direct drive does not guarantee silence.** The drivetrain, cooling fans, floor and room structure remain part of the sound another person hears.

Wheel-on can still make sense for occasional indoor riding, very limited storage or a strict budget. If interval accuracy and low-friction setup are central to the purchase, direct drive is usually the stronger long-term fit.

## Check compatibility in this order

### 1. Frame and axle

Confirm the rear axle type and frame clearance against the manufacturer's model-level compatibility page. “Road bike compatible” is not enough: quick release, 12x142 mm, 12x148 mm and unusual frame designs can require different adapters or be excluded.

### 2. Drivetrain and freehub

Match the bike's cassette speed and brand, then confirm the trainer's freehub. Shimano/SRAM HG, SRAM XD/XDR, Shimano Micro Spline and Campagnolo are not interchangeable. Also check whether the advertised bundle includes a cassette, a Zwift Cog or neither.

### 3. Application and feature

Basic Bluetooth or ANT+ control does not prove every app-specific feature will work. Check [Zwift's current Ready and Cog-compatible trainer list](https://support.zwift.com/en_us/zwift-ready-and-zwift-cog-compatible-trainers-r1x1sNas0), [TrainerRoad's compatible devices](https://support.trainerroad.com/hc/en-us/articles/201682804-Compatible-Devices-and-Sensors) and [ROUVY's model-level trainer directory](https://support.rouvy.com/hc/en-us/articles/14059132272529-Supported-Trainers).

Virtual shifting deserves special attention. A trainer can control resistance in several apps while its Cog and Click shifting works only in Zwift. Model, bundle and firmware all matter.

### 4. Connection path

Bluetooth is simple, ANT+ FE-C remains useful on computers and head units, and Wi-Fi can reduce dependence on a short-range radio link. But Wi-Fi support is not identical: Tacx NEO 3M needs an optional network adapter, KICKR v6 and CORE 2 have built-in Wi-Fi, and Elite's wired Ethernet requires its gateway. Buy for the connection you can actually use in the training room.

### 5. The room

Leave budget and space for a strong fan, sweat protection and a stable mat. Cooling affects the session more than a rarely used extra 4% of simulated gradient. In flats and shared houses, vibration through the floor may matter as much as airborne trainer noise.

## What accuracy means for structured training

The accuracy values on this page are manufacturer tolerances, not a promise that two different power meters will display the same number. A trainer measures power at a different point in the drivetrain from a crank- or pedal-based meter, and every device has its own tolerance and calibration procedure.

For an FTP test or a training block, consistency is more useful than swapping power sources to chase the preferred number. Set zones from the source that will control the workouts, follow the manufacturer's calibration guidance and investigate sudden changes rather than automatically “correcting” a stable offset.

Use the [FTP test tool](/tools/ftp-test) to establish a current baseline, then keep the test protocol and power source consistent.
