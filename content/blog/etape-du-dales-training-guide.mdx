Here is the thing nobody tells you about the Etape du Dales. The total climbing — roughly 4,000 metres — puts it alongside La Marmotte and the Maratona dles Dolomites. On paper, it belongs in Alpine company. In practice, it is a completely different animal. Alpine gran fondos give you twenty-kilometre climbs where you settle into a rhythm and grind at threshold for forty-five minutes. The Dales do not work like that. The same 4,000 metres arrive as dozens of short, vicious ramps — one to three kilometres at 10-20 per cent — with barely enough descent between them to reach for a gel. The climbing never stops. The recovery never comes.

The Etape du Dales does not ask whether you can hold 250 watts for an hour. It asks whether you can produce 350-400 watts for four minutes, recover on a rough descent, and do it again. And again. For 180 kilometres. Training for this event the way you would train for the Marmotte is precisely the mistake that leaves riders walking Park Rash at kilometre sixty.

## Course Profile and Key Sections

The route starts and finishes in Grassington, looping north through the Yorkshire Dales National Park. There is no single defining monster like the Fred Whitton's Hardknott. The difficulty is cumulative — a relentless sequence of short, steep climbs with barely any flat road between them.

### The Opening Miles and Wharfedale (km 0-30)

The route rolls out along Wharfedale — valley floor, stone walls, sheep, the River Wharfe below. These are the easy miles. Groups form quickly. The scenery is stunning.

What most people do: sit on a wheel doing 35km/h and arrive at the first real climb having burned matches they will desperately need a hundred kilometres later. What actually works: find a group riding 25-28km/h, eat your first gel within twenty minutes, and save everything for the climbs.

### Park Rash and the Early Monsters (km 30-70)

Park Rash defines the Etape du Dales. Statistically, 1.6 kilometres at 12 per cent average. Those numbers do not communicate what it actually feels like. The opening bends are steep but manageable. Then the road tilts beyond 15 per cent. Then 20. Then the ramps hit 25 per cent on rough, narrow tarmac with no room to weave for a shallower line. Seated climbing fails above 18 per cent — rear tyre breaks traction, front wheel lifts, cadence drops below the point where smooth pedalling is possible. You stand. You grind.

Here is where it gets really interesting. Park Rash arrives early enough that your legs still have power but late enough that poor pacing shows. The correct strategy: ride the lower slopes at a controlled effort, accept that the steep section will demand everything, and recover fully on the descent. Do not try to ride Park Rash fast. Try to ride it without emptying the tank.

The section between Park Rash and Fleet Moss stacks smaller climbs that would be noteworthy in any other event. They accumulate. Your legs carry fatigue that does not fully clear on the descents. This is the signature demand — the slow, grinding erosion of your reserves.

### Fleet Moss and Buttertubs (km 70-110)

Fleet Moss is the highest paved road in Yorkshire — 5.5 kilometres at roughly 5.5 per cent. After Park Rash, it feels almost rational. A proper climb with a proper gradient. Sit down, find a rhythm, hold 85-90 per cent FTP. Use the [climb time calculator](/tools/climb-time) to estimate your effort — at 3.0 W/kg and 75kg, expect roughly 18-20 minutes.

Buttertubs Pass follows — one of the most famous cycling roads in England since the Tour de France's 2014 visit. Moderate by Dales standards. On a clear day, the summit views stretch across half of Yorkshire. On a typical Dales day, you can see the inside of a cloud.

This section is the psychological halfway point. The temptation is to feel like the hard work is behind you. It is not. Tan Hill and the Coal Road are ahead, and they arrive on legs that have been climbing for five hours.

### The Exposed Moorland Section (km 110-140)

The route climbs to Tan Hill — the highest pub in Britain — across some of the most exposed terrain in England. Open moorland. No shelter. No trees.

Let me break this down. You are at 500-600 metres elevation on exposed moorland in late June. The temperature can be 8-10 degrees lower than the valley floor. Wind hits from the side with nothing to stop it for miles. Rain arrives horizontally. Hypothermia is not dramatic language — it is the actual risk of riding wet and exposed at altitude for an hour with depleted glycogen and insufficient clothing.

The Coal Road separates riders who have paced well from riders who have not. Not steep by Dales standards, but long, exposed, and arriving when your legs have been working for six hours. Steady effort. Keep pedalling.

### The Final 50km Home (km 140-180)

The last fifty kilometres back to Grassington contain three significant climbs. On fresh legs, none would trouble a competent club cyclist. After 140 kilometres and 3,000 metres of climbing, they are a different proposition entirely. This is where the event is won or lost.

The descents here are fast and technical on narrow Dales roads. Bike handling is degraded by fatigue. Grip strength is reduced. Descend conservatively — the time you save descending fast is not worth the risk after eight hours of climbing.

The final roll into Grassington along Wharfedale does not hit you at the finish arch. It hits you in the pub afterwards, staring at a pint, trying to explain what Park Rash actually felt like.

## 12-Week Training Plan Overview

This plan assumes you are already riding 7-9 hours per week and can comfortably ride 120km with 1,500m of climbing. If your base is lower, add four weeks of volume building before starting.

### Phase 1: Base Building (Weeks 1-4)

**Volume:** 7-9 hours per week across 4-5 rides.

Eighty per cent at [Zone 2](/blog/zone-2-training-complete-guide) — conversational pace on hilly terrain. Flat Zone 2 does not prepare you for the Dales. Find hills. Ride them at a sustainable pace. Repeat.

**Anchor session:** One long ride per week, building from 3 hours (week 1) to 4.5 hours (week 4) with 800-1,200m of climbing. Practise eating and drinking on the bike from ride one.

**Quality session:** One midweek ride of 90 minutes with 3x10 minutes at 85-90 per cent FTP on a climb. Enough structure to set the ceiling for later blocks.

**Off the bike:** Two core sessions per week, 20 minutes each. Planks (3x45 seconds), side planks (3x30 seconds each side), dead bugs (3x10 each side), single-leg glute bridges (3x12 each side), hip mobility. A weak core collapses on steep Dales ramps and you lose power through the pedals.

### Phase 2: Hill Power (Weeks 5-8)

**Volume:** 9-11 hours per week.

Two quality sessions per week, both on hills.

**VO2max session:** 5x3 minutes at 110-120 per cent FTP on a steep climb, 3 minutes easy between. Build to 6x4 minutes by week 8. This is precisely what Park Rash demands — above threshold, time-limited, repeated. The [FTP zone calculator](/tools/ftp-zones) sets the targets.

**Over-under session:** 3x12 minutes alternating between 95 per cent FTP (2 minutes) and 105 per cent FTP (1 minute), 5 minutes easy between sets. This trains the ability to clear lactate while still climbing — the exact demand of every Dales ramp.

**Long ride:** Building from 4 hours to 5.5 hours with 1,500-2,000m of climbing. First two-thirds at Zone 2, final third at tempo. Full fuelling rehearsal every long ride.

**Core:** Maintain twice weekly. Add bird-dogs (3x8 each side) and single-leg calf raises (3x12 each side).

### Phase 3: Race-Specific (Weeks 9-10)

**Volume:** 10-12 hours per week. The training matches the event.

**Hill repeats on steep gradients:** Find a climb at 10 per cent or steeper. Ride 6-8 repeats of 3-5 minutes at 4-5 W/kg. Practise standing technique on the steepest sections. Recover on the descent. Nothing simulates the Dales demand better.

**The dress rehearsal ride (week 9 or 10):** 120-140km with 2,500-3,000m of climbing. Full kit, full fuelling plan. If you finish tired but functional, you are ready. If you finish destroyed, adjust pacing or nutrition.

**One over-under session per week:** Same structure as Phase 2 but with 4x12 minutes.

### Phase 4: Taper (Weeks 11-12)

**Volume drops 30-40%.** The fitness is built. You cannot add meaningful fitness in two weeks. Freshness is worth more than any final interval.

**Week 11:** 7-8 hours. Long ride of 3 hours with 800m of climbing at easy pace. One short VO2max session — 4x3 minutes at 110 per cent FTP to keep the top end sharp. Two easy spins.

**Week 12 (event week):** Tuesday: 60 minutes easy with 3x2-minute openers. Thursday: 40 minutes easy with 3x1-minute accelerations. Friday: rest. Saturday: 20-minute spin. Sunday: event day.

## Key Sessions

### Hill Repeat VO2max Session

Find a climb of 3-5 minutes at 8 per cent or steeper. Ride 5-6 repeats at 110-120 per cent FTP with full descent recovery between. If your power drops more than 10 per cent on the final repeat, you started too hard. The goal is repeatability, not a single maximal effort.

### Over-Under Threshold Intervals

3-4 sets of 12 minutes on a climb or rolling terrain: 2 minutes at 93-97 per cent FTP (under), 1 minute at 103-107 per cent FTP (over), cycling four times per set. Rest 5 minutes between sets. This trains your body to clear lactate while maintaining output — the defining metabolic demand of every Dales climb.

### The Progressive Long Ride on Hilly Terrain

Build from 3 hours to 5.5 hours over twelve weeks with real climbing — 1,000-2,500m of ascent. Flat long rides do not prepare you for this event. First two-thirds at Zone 2, final third at tempo. Full fuelling practice on every ride.

## Nutrition Strategy

Fuelling the Etape du Dales requires terrain-aware timing. You cannot eat while climbing at 15 per cent — your breathing is maximal and swallowing a gel is somewhere between impractical and dangerous. The nutrition plan must match the course profile. Use the [fuel planner](/tools/fuel-planner) to calculate your exact requirements.

**The terrain rule:** Eat on descents and valley sections. Never start a climb without having eaten. A flat section or a descent ahead is your feeding window — take it, even if you are not hungry.

**Hourly intake:** 80-100g of carbohydrate per hour from the gun. Gels are the most practical format because they can be consumed in ten seconds on a descent. Bars work in valley sections where you have time to chew. Drink mix in the bottles provides a steady baseline.

**Feed stations:** Stop briefly, eat real food, refill bottles. Do not sit down for more than five minutes — legs stiffen fast in cool Dales air and the first climb after a long stop feels significantly harder.

**The moorland problem:** Cold, wet conditions suppress appetite. Force-feed on a timer — set a 20-minute alarm on your head unit. The bonk from under-fuelling in cold weather arrives fast and recovers slowly.

**The day before:** 8-10g of carbohydrate per kilogram of bodyweight spread across the day. Pasta, rice, bread, potatoes. Familiar food, normal hydration, early to bed.

**Race morning:** Breakfast 3 hours before the start. Porridge, toast with honey, banana, coffee. 100-150g of carbohydrate. Nothing new.

## Equipment and Gear

**Gearing.** Non-negotiable. A 34x32 is the minimum. A 34x34 is better. If your lowest gear is 34x28, you will almost certainly walk sections of Park Rash unless you sit above 4 W/kg at 50 rpm. Check rear derailleur capacity before fitting a larger cassette. The [gear ratio calculator](/tools/gear-ratio) shows your exact ratios.

**Tyres.** 25-28mm. Not slick race tyres — Dales roads are rough in places, with loose gravel, cattle grid approaches, and patched tarmac on steep descents. A tyre with some puncture protection is worth the marginal weight penalty. Tubeless if your wheels support it.

**Clothing for the Dales.** Arm warmers and gilet from the start regardless of forecast. Packable rain jacket in the jersey — moorland at 500-600 metres can be 10 degrees colder than Grassington. Full-finger gloves if rain is forecast. The [wind chill](/tools/wind-chill) on a Dales descent at 60km/h in wet conditions drops to near freezing even in late June.

**Spares.** Two inner tubes, tyre levers, CO2 inflator, chain quick link, multi-tool. A mechanical on exposed moorland is a long, cold wait.

**Bottles.** Two minimum. Three if your frame allows — climbs burn through fluid faster than flat riding.

## Race Day Pacing

The single most important decision on event day is made in the first fifty kilometres. Pace the opening section conservatively — truly conservatively, not what feels conservative on fresh legs — and the final fifty kilometres become hard but manageable. Do not, and they become a survival exercise.

**Km 0-30 (Wharfedale):** 60-70 per cent FTP. Below conversation pace. Let faster riders go. Eat early. This is not the race.

**Km 30-70 (Park Rash and early climbs):** 85-92 per cent FTP on the lower slopes. Accept that steep sections push you to 100-110 per cent — unavoidable on 20+ per cent. Recover fully on every descent. The [VAM calculator](/tools/vam) benchmarks sustainable climbing rates.

**Km 70-110 (Fleet Moss and Buttertubs):** 80-90 per cent FTP. These are proper climbs that respond to steady pacing. Find a rhythm. Sit down. Hold it.

**Km 110-140 (Moorland):** Whatever you have. If you paced the first half correctly, you still have something. If you did not, this is where you discover it.

**Km 140-180 (The final 50km):** Ride each climb as it comes. Do not look at the total distance remaining — look at the distance to the next descent. Keep eating. Keep pedalling.

## Common Mistakes

**1. Going too hard on Park Rash.** Park Rash is kilometre sixty of one hundred and eighty. Riders treat it as the defining test, empty themselves on the steep sections, and spend the next 120 kilometres paying for three minutes of glory. Ride it to survive, not to conquer.

**2. Wrong gearing.** A 34x28 cassette is a recipe for walking. Check your gearing six weeks before the event. A cassette swap may require a new chain and a derailleur capacity check — not a last-minute job.

**3. Underfuelling because the terrain prevents eating.** You cannot eat on a 15 per cent climb but you can eat on the descent that follows it. Plan nutrition around the course profile, not the clock.

**4. Ignoring the weather.** Late June in the Dales is not late June in Surrey. Exposed moorland at 500-600 metres delivers cold rain with alarming regularity. Pack for the worst.

**5. Underestimating the final 50km.** Three significant climbs remain after Buttertubs, and they arrive on legs that have climbed 3,000 metres. The riders who respect the final fifty are the riders who finish well.

## Travel and Logistics

**Getting to Grassington.** Roughly four hours from London by car, 90 minutes from Manchester or Leeds. Nearest train stations are Skipton (20 minutes by car) and Ilkley (30 minutes). Parking on event morning is limited — arrive the day before.

**Accommodation.** Grassington is a small village. B&Bs and pubs with rooms fill fast once entries open. Skipton, Ilkley, and Settle are realistic alternatives. The Dales pubs are excellent and the atmosphere the evening before is worth the early arrival.

**Entry.** Approximately GBP65-85 depending on registration timing. The event typically sells out.

**The Dales atmosphere.** This is not a corporate sportive. Feed stations staffed by locals. Climbs lined with sheep, not spectators. The finish in Grassington is quiet, personal, and more satisfying than any medal-ceremony podium.

## Recovery Plan

Eight to twelve hours of repeated above-threshold climbing depletes glycogen, damages muscle fibre, and accumulates fatigue that takes more than one night's sleep to clear.

**Immediately after:** Eat within 30 minutes — 1.5g of carbohydrate per kilogram of bodyweight plus 25-30g of protein. Recovery shake followed by a proper meal within two hours.

**Day 1 after:** Complete rest. Your legs will be stiff. Normal after 180km of climbing.

**Days 2-3:** Easy 30-45 minute spin if the legs allow it. Zone 1 only.

**Days 4-7:** Gradually return to normal riding. Short, flat, easy. No hills. No intensity. The Dales extract a debt that takes a full week to repay.
