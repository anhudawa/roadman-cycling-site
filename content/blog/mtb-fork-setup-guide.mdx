The most important fork-setup question is not “what do riders my weight run?”
It is “which exact fork and source am I using?”

Fork chassis, generation, travel, air spring and damper can all change the
pressure and damping guidance. Roadman's [MTB suspension calculator](/tools/shock-pressure)
now gives a direct chart value only for the four listed 2026 FOX 38 variants.
For RockShox it follows SRAM's instruction to use the pressure decal or
[Trailhead](https://trailhead.rockshox.com/).

For fork and rear shock as a system, use the
[complete MTB suspension setup guide](/blog/mtb-suspension-setup-complete-guide).

## 1. Identify the fork before choosing PSI

Record these fields:

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Chassis and generation | Air-spring and limit changes can sit under the same family name |
| Variant | FLOAT, Rhythm and e-bike versions can have separate charts |
| Travel | Pressure, sag distance and spacer limits can change with travel |
| Damper | Rebound and compression recommendations depend on the installed damper |
| Maximum pressure | It is a limit, never a target |
| Serial or ID | It resolves the exact manual and service information |

The [2026 FOX 38 manual](https://tech.ridefox.com/bike/owners-manuals/3103/fork--2026-38mm)
has separate pressure columns for standard FLOAT, FLOAT E-Bike+, Rhythm and
Rhythm E-Optimized. It states 140 PSI maximum for FLOAT/E-Bike+ and 120 PSI for
Rhythm. Never copy those limits to another fork.

RockShox's [current suspension manual](https://docs.sram.com/en-US/publications/5ODr3E6BhL1uWDnWhq4ATB/UM%20-%20Suspension)
directs air-fork riders to the fork decal or Trailhead for starting pressure and
rebound. That is more defensible than Roadman's old generic tables because it
resolves the actual product.

## 2. Get a source-backed starting pressure

Use this hierarchy:

1. exact bicycle guidance where the fork is OEM-tuned;
2. exact fork manual or serial/product lookup;
3. the pressure decal on the identified fork;
4. a calculator only when it names the same product, generation and source.

Avoid shortcuts such as “PSI equals rider weight in kilograms” or “fork PSI is
60% of body weight in pounds”. Different columns in one FOX 38 manual already
disprove the idea that one weight formula spans even a single chassis family.

### Worked example: 2026 FOX 38 FLOAT

An 80 kg rider including kit is about 176 lb. The published 170–180 lb band for
the standard 2026 FOX 38 FLOAT lists **93 PSI**. At 170 mm travel, 20% sag is
**34 mm**.

Those two numbers have different jobs:

- 93 PSI is the sourced starting input;
- 34 mm is the measurement used to calibrate the final setup.

FOX notes that actual pressure can differ from its chart with variables such as
riding style, terrain and bike geometry. That is a reason to measure sag—not a
reason for a hidden calculator multiplier.

## 3. Calculate fork sag in millimetres

Use:

`fork travel × target sag percentage = sag distance`

| Travel | 15% | 20% | 25% |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 mm | 15 mm | 20 mm | 25 mm |
| 130 mm | 19.5 mm | 26 mm | 32.5 mm |
| 150 mm | 22.5 mm | 30 mm | 37.5 mm |
| 170 mm | 25.5 mm | 34 mm | 42.5 mm |
| 180 mm | 27 mm | 36 mm | 45 mm |

The table does arithmetic; it does not choose the correct percentage. FOX
specifies 15–20% for the selected 2026 38. Other forks and bikes can differ, so
confirm the exact manual before selecting a column.

## 4. Inflate and equalise as the product requires

Use a high-pressure suspension pump. Set compression controls to the manual's
open position before measuring sag. Add air without exceeding the lowest
applicable limit, then follow the fork's exact equalisation process.

Do not use one generic instruction such as “cycle every fork 15 times”.
RockShox publishes staged instructions for current DebonAir+ products, while a
FOX model can specify its own procedure. The air chambers and pump gauge can
settle differently as pressures equalise.

Likewise, do not deliberately overinflate to account for assumed hose loss. The
air that changes the gauge on reconnection can be the air required to pressurise
the pump hose rather than proof that the fork leaked. Use the manufacturer's
procedure and confirm the result with sag.

## 5. Measure sag consistently

1. Slide the O-ring to the dust wiper.
2. Wear normal helmet, shoes, pads, pack, water and tools.
3. Use a wall or helper and settle into the instructed riding position.
4. Hold still without bouncing.
5. Move the O-ring if the exact procedure requires it while weighted.
6. Dismount carefully.
7. Measure from the wiper to the O-ring.
8. Add or release a small amount of air, equalise again and repeat.

A fixed 5 PSI step is a much bigger relative change in a 55 PSI fork than a
120 PSI fork. “Small amount” should follow the product guidance and pump
resolution, not one site-wide number.

## 6. Set rebound after pressure and sag

Rebound controls how quickly the fork extends. Too little rebound damping can
make the return abrupt; too much can stop recovery between repeated impacts.

Start at the exact pressure-linked setting for the installed damper. The same
FOX 38 manual provides different rebound columns for GRIP, GRIP X and GRIP X2.
Count clicks from the direction named by the manual, record the setting, then
change one click at a time on a repeatable section.

The car-park push test can reveal an extreme setting. It cannot reproduce a
sequence of trail impacts, braking loads and rider inputs, so it is not the final
test.

## 7. Add compression only from a known baseline

Low-speed and high-speed compression describe damper shaft-speed regimes, not
simply slow and fast bicycle speed. Available controls differ by damper.

Begin at the product's published start. Test one change at a time. If a fork is
harsh, first verify product identity, pressure, sag, equalisation and service
condition instead of assuming that opening one control must solve it.

## 8. Treat spacers as progression tuning

FOX says approved volume spacers change mid-stroke and bottom-out resistance.
RockShox says tokens reduce air volume and increase progression. Neither supports
an automatic PSI uplift for each spacer.

Only consider a permitted change after sag is correct and representative riding
shows an end-stroke problem. Check the exact travel-dependent maximum, follow
the official installation procedure or use a technician, then repeat sag and
damping setup.

## 9. Keep a fork setup log

| Date | Fork and travel | PSI | Sag mm / % | Rebound | Compression | Temperature | Trail note |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example | 2026 38 FLOAT, 170 mm | 93 | 34 / 20% | exact clicks from closed | exact damper start | 15°C | baseline |

Always record the rebound/compression reference direction. “Seven clicks” is
ambiguous unless it says seven out from fully closed or another defined start.

## When to stop adjusting

Stop riding and seek qualified inspection for visible damage, oil leakage,
unexpected pressure loss, harsh top-out, abnormal noise, a control that has no
effect or an inability to reach correct sag inside the permitted pressure range.
Never use another rider's setting to tune around a mechanical fault.
