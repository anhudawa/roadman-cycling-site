Before adding a gym session, mark the ride you most want to do well. Then look at what happens before and after the available gym slot. That surrounding work determines whether the slot is useful.

In our [conversation with Art O’Connor](/podcast/ep-2054-i-tried-keegan-swensons-insane-strength-routine-heres-what-n), the strength coach asks what happens to recovery if a cyclist fills every easy day with hard gym work. His approach is to group demanding work, leaving room for easier days.

## Start with the priority ride

Write down the ride’s purpose, its day and the quality you need to preserve. An interval session and a social spin do not have the same role simply because both last an hour.

Next, list the gym slots you can reliably attend. For each one, check the riding immediately before and after it. If you repeatedly arrive at your key session unable to complete the planned work, the arrangement needs review.

This is Roadman’s way of applying the scheduling question. It does not mean every disappointing ride was caused by strength training; sleep, fuelling, illness and the total workload also matter.

## Consider combining demanding work

O’Connor’s suggestion to put strength on harder riding days can leave another day genuinely easier. It also creates a demanding day that has to be tolerable and practical.

If the bike session has priority, completing it before lifting is an option. If fatigue then makes the gym work poor, reduce the gym dose or use another slot. More space between sessions can help you organise the day, but the interview does not establish a compulsory gap that works for everyone.

Keep the arrangement if the sessions remain useful and you can repeat the week.

## Choose movements before chasing numbers

O’Connor starts his assessment with a bodyweight squat on video. He discusses squatting and hinging, single-leg work, pushing, pulling and carries, with adaptations for the person in front of him.

For a beginner, that supports a practical first task: find versions you can control, and get qualified help where technique is uncertain. It does not justify copying a professional rider’s weight, testing a maximum on day one or assuming a weak core is your particular limitation.

## Adjust when the cycling week changes

Record the exercise, weight, sets, reps and effort. Add how the priority ride went. Review the two records together before increasing the gym work.

When riding becomes more demanding, removing an optional exercise or a work set is one possible adjustment. If the schedule itself is the problem, change the slot. Change one thing at a time where practical so you can learn from the response.

O’Connor describes different frequencies and phases in his coaching. He also allows people to stay with preparatory work longer when they need it. Neither three sessions a week nor a fixed date for moving to explosive lifts is an instruction for every listener.

## Build a week you can repeat

Use the [strength-session planner](/tools/strength-session-planner) to compare days. If you need exercise choices and a place to record your work, start with the [beginner strength plan](/blog/cycling-strength-training-12-week-beginner-plan).

The [cyclist strength-training review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40632222/) supports potential performance benefits with low-certainty evidence. It does not validate O’Connor’s exact schedule or promise that adding gym work will leave every ride unaffected.
