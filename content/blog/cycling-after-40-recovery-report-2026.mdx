## What this report will cover

Recovery is the part of the masters-athlete equation that breaks training plans — not the training itself. The prescription for a masters cyclist isn't radically different from a younger rider's. The recovery requirement around it is. The Cycling After 40 Recovery Report 2026 will be a dedicated companion to the [Masters Cycling Training Report](/blog/masters-cycling-training-report-2026), focused entirely on what surrounds the work.

Sections planned for the Q3 2026 release:

1. The 72-hour rule — why hard sessions need more space after 40, and how to plan around it
2. Sleep architecture changes after 40 — what shortens, what fragments, and what to protect
3. The deload cadence question — 2:1 vs 3:1 vs Friel's 9-day cycle in practice
4. HRV after 40 — interpreting the trend when daily noise is high
5. Resting heart rate as the cheapest recovery signal — and the four-signal check-in
6. Recovery rides — what they should actually look like
7. Sleep, alcohol, and the deep-sleep window
8. Perimenopause and [menopause](/blog/menopause-cycling-performance) — sleep architecture, RHR baseline shift, hot-flush thermoregulation
9. Recovery nutrition — the second meal that decides tomorrow's session
10. Recovery monitoring tools we recommend, and the ones to ignore — in the meantime, our [masters recovery score calculator](/tools/masters-recovery-score) gives you a structured check-in you can use today
11. Case studies — masters cyclists who fixed recovery before they fixed training

The report will follow the same template as the existing series: methodology, downloadable supporting data, an honest limitations section, and a working set of expert citations.
