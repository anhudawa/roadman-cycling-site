faq:
  - question: Why is cycling progress not linear?
    answer: >-
      Cycling progress is non-linear because each phase of training exposes a
      new limiting factor, whether that is fitness, recovery, nutrition, or life
      stress. You resolve one problem, which reveals the next, and consistent
      forward movement comes from addressing each obstacle in turn rather than
      expecting steady, uninterrupted improvement.
  - question: How do you keep training consistently when life gets in the way?
    answer: >-
      Consistency comes from treating training as a non-negotiable
      responsibility for your own physical and mental health rather than
      something contingent on perfect circumstances. Anthony Walsh highlights a
      client who maintained training through serious family health crises by
      focusing only on what was within his control and solving the immediate
      obstacle in front of him.
  - question: How do you use Tim Ferriss's easy solution question for training setbacks?
    answer: >-
      When facing a training setback, ask yourself what the easy solution would
      look like if it were simple. This reframe, drawn from Tim Ferriss, strips
      away overthinking and usually reveals a straightforward action, such as
      shortening a session, adjusting intensity, or rescheduling rather than
      abandoning the plan entirely.
  - question: What does The Martian film teach you about cycling improvement?
    answer: >-
      In The Martian, the protagonist survives by solving one problem at a time
      without being paralysed by the scale of his situation. Anthony Walsh
      applies this directly to cycling: identify the single biggest obstacle to
      your performance right now, solve it, then move to the next one, and
      repeat that process until you reach your goal.
