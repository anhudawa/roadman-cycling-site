faq:
  - question: what is a loser mentality in psychology?
    answer: >-
      A loser mentality is a pattern of self-defeating thinking and behaviour
      that keeps people stuck rather than progressing. Anthony Walsh identifies
      its clearest behavioural marker as publicly tearing down successful people
      on social media, which signals deep insecurity and a refusal to take
      personal responsibility for your own outcomes.
  - question: why do people put down successful people on social media?
    answer: >-
      People criticise successful individuals online because it feels easier
      than confronting their own lack of progress. Walsh argues this behaviour
      is rooted in insecurity and functions as a way to protect the ego by
      pulling others down rather than raising your own standards.
  - question: how do I stop wasting mental energy on negative thoughts?
    answer: >-
      Recognise that your daily attention is finite and every minute spent in
      comment-section arguments is a minute stolen from controllable actions
      that actually move you forward. Walsh recommends reframing high performers
      as breadcrumb trails to study and replicate, shifting focus from
      resentment to deliberate effort.
  - question: how can I tell if I have a self-defeating mindset?
    answer: >-
      A reliable signal is whether you regularly react to other people's success
      with criticism rather than curiosity. If your instinct when you see
      someone achieving more than you is to find fault with them publicly,
      Walsh's position is that this pattern is actively working against your own
      progress.
