  pursuing less is really about pursuing what is essential: relationships,
  hobbies, items, and work practices that matter most. Drawing on his own decade

faq:
  - question: Why is doing less actually better for productivity?
    answer: >-
      Doing less forces you to identify what is truly essential rather than
      filling time with low-value activity. Coach Anthony Walsh argues that
      every commitment is an implicit yes to that thing and a no to something
      else, so reducing volume sharpens focus on the relationships, hobbies, and
      work that matter most.
  - question: What does essentialism mean in everyday life?
    answer: >-
      Essentialism means deliberately choosing only the commitments, items, and
      relationships that align with your most important priorities. Rather than
      cutting things randomly, you identify a destination and evaluate every
      habit or obligation against it, turning passive defaults into active,
      intentional choices.
  - question: How do I stop wasting time on things that don't matter?
    answer: >-
      Start by converting implicit nos into explicit ones. Coach Anthony Walsh
      recommends identifying your default time fillers, such as evening
      television, and deciding consciously whether they serve your goals rather
      than simply drifting into them out of habit.
  - question: Why is hustle culture bad for you?
    answer: >-
      Hustle culture quietly transfers control of your time to external demands
      rather than your own priorities. Coach Anthony Walsh spent a decade
      building and abandoning side ventures before returning to coaching,
      concluding that relentless busyness often masks a lack of clarity about
      what actually matters rather than representing genuine progress.
