answerCapsule: >-
  Cycling coach Anthony Walsh argues that traditional base training dogma — no
  big ring, no hills, no intensity until January — has no physiological basis
  and contradicts current endurance research across cycling, running, triathlon,
  and cross-country skiing. The consensus finding is that large volumes of
  low-intensity work, carefully interspersed with high-intensity intervals
  year-round, produces the best results. The practical question is not whether
  to include winter intensity but how much, measured objectively through
  Training Stress Score and week-on-week ramp rates rather than subjective feel
  or club folklore.

faq:
  - question: Is base training still necessary for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      Base training remains essential, but the traditional rules around it are
      outdated. Cycling coach Anthony Walsh argues that blanket bans on
      intensity during winter have no physiological basis and contradict modern
      endurance research. The most effective approach combines large volumes of
      low-intensity riding with carefully placed high-intensity intervals
      throughout the year.
  - question: Should you do high intensity training in winter or stick to easy miles?
    answer: >-
      You should do both, year-round. Research across cycling, running,
      triathlon, and cross-country skiing consistently shows that polarised
      training, predominantly low intensity with structured intervals
      interspersed, produces better results than either approach alone. The
      question is not whether to include winter intensity but how much to
      include relative to your total training load.
  - question: How do you measure training load during base phase?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends using Training Stress Score rather than relying
      on subjective feel or club convention. Monitoring week-on-week ramp rates
      gives you an objective measure of how much load you are adding, which
      helps avoid overreaching during a period when volume is already
      increasing.
  - question: What is the difference between base training and HIIT for endurance sport?
    answer: >-
      Base training builds the aerobic foundation through high volumes of
      low-intensity work, while HIIT sessions target specific physiological
      systems with short, hard efforts. Neither approach alone is optimal. The
      evidence from endurance sport research points to a polarised model where
      the majority of training is easy, with a minority of sessions at high
      intensity, applied consistently across the whole training year.
