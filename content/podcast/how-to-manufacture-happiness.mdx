faq:
  - question: What is the "Good Thing Bad Thing Who Knows" parable about?
    answer: >-
      It is a Taoist parable about a farmer who refuses to judge events as good
      or bad, because every apparent misfortune leads to a blessing and every
      apparent blessing leads to hardship. The story teaches that attaching
      fixed labels to outcomes creates unnecessary suffering, since the full
      consequences of any event cannot be known in the moment.
  - question: How do you manufacture happiness when things go wrong?
    answer: >-
      Happiness is a deliberate practice of reframing rather than a natural
      response to circumstances. Because negative thoughts surface automatically
      while positive ones require active effort, you have to consciously choose
      a constructive interpretation of setbacks. Anthony Walsh draws on Naval
      Ravikant's idea that life is a single player game, meaning your internal
      response is always within your control regardless of external events.
  - question: How do cyclists stay positive after an injury stops their training?
    answer: >-
      The reframing technique from the "Good Thing, Bad Thing, Who Knows"
      parable applies directly here. A training injury can be reframed as forced
      recovery time, an opportunity to address weaknesses, or a reset that
      prevents longer-term burnout. Refusing to immediately label the injury as
      purely bad reduces the psychological damage that compounds the physical
      setback.
  - question: Why do negative thoughts stick more than positive ones?
    answer: >-
      The brain has a negativity bias, meaning threatening or unpleasant
      information is processed more deeply and recalled more readily than
      positive information. Positive thoughts do not linger automatically and
      must be actively brought to mind and reinforced. This is why happiness
      requires deliberate practice rather than simply waiting for good
      circumstances to arrive.
