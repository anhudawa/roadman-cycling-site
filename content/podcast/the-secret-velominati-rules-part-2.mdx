faq:
  - question: What are the Velominati rules for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      The Velominati rules are an unofficial code of conduct for road cyclists,
      covering everything from kit choices to bike etiquette. They originated as
      a tongue-in-cheek but widely respected set of guidelines within the road
      cycling community, and Anthony Walsh breaks them down across a three-part
      podcast series with commentary from his own racing career.
  - question: Why do cyclists have to wear black shorts?
    answer: >-
      According to the Velominati rules, cycling shorts must be black, full
      stop. The rule exists to preserve a clean, professional appearance on the
      bike, and coloured or patterned shorts are considered a violation of
      proper cycling etiquette.
  - question: Should you wear a cycling cap off the bike?
    answer: >-
      The Velominati explicitly prohibit wearing a cycling cap away from the
      bike. It is considered poor form to wear one in a café, bar, or any
      setting where you are not actively riding or have just finished a ride,
      and Walsh highlights this as one of the more commonly broken rules.
  - question: Do cyclists really need to shave their legs?
    answer: >-
      According to the Velominati rules, shaved legs are non-negotiable for any
      serious road cyclist. Walsh illustrates this with a story about Irish
      amateur Roger Aiken at the 2013 Rás, using it to show how strongly the
      cycling community enforces this particular standard.
  - question: What is the Velominati rule about bikes on roof racks?
    answer: >-
      The rule states that any bike carried on a car roof rack must be worth
      more than the car itself. It is one of the more humorous Velominati rules,
      but it reflects the broader principle that a cyclist's priorities should
      always favour the bike over everything else.
