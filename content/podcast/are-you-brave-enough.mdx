faq:
  - question: What is the 8 Week Challenge for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      The 8 Week Challenge is a structured training programme created by Anthony
      Walsh of A1 Coaching. It is built around the Escape-Arrival Framework,
      which asks cyclists to define what they are riding away from and what they
      are riding toward before any training begins. Walsh argues that without
      both of those anchors, motivation and results tend to fall apart.
  - question: What is the Escape-Arrival Framework in cycling training?
    answer: >-
      The Escape-Arrival Framework is a planning model developed by Anthony
      Walsh that separates an athlete's negative motivation, what they want to
      leave behind, from their positive goal, what they are actively riding
      toward. Most amateur cyclists only think about one side, which weakens
      their commitment over time. Defining both gives the training programme a
      much clearer purpose.
  - question: How do I set cycling goals that actually keep me motivated?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends identifying both what you are escaping, such as
      poor fitness, stress, or a lack of structure, and what you are arriving
      at, such as a specific event or a measurable performance target. Having
      only a vague end goal without acknowledging the push factor behind it
      reduces long-term follow-through. Writing both down before the programme
      starts keeps the reasoning visible when motivation dips.
  - question: >-
      How long does it take to see results from a structured cycling training
      programme?
    answer: >-
      Eight weeks of consistent, structured training is enough time for
      measurable aerobic and performance improvements in amateur cyclists.
      Walsh's 8 Week Challenge is designed specifically around that timeframe as
      a defined, manageable block rather than an open-ended commitment. Clear
      start and end points make it easier to stay focused and assess progress
      honestly.
