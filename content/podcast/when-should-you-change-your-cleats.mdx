answerCapsule: >-
  Host Anthony Walsh explains when cyclists should replace their cleats,
  focusing on the two contact points on the underside of the shoe: the front
  clip-in point and the rear platform. Wear on the cleat platform itself, rather
  than the colored outer material, signals immediate replacement, as does any
  unintended foot release during a sprint. Gradual erosion alters foot angle and
  causes injury over thousands of pedal strokes. A practical tip: trace the old
  cleat outline with a marker before removal to position the replacement
  identically.

faq:
  - question: How do I know when my cycling cleats need replacing?
    answer: >-
      Check the two contact points on the underside of your shoe: the front
      clip-in point and the rear platform. Once wear reaches the cleat platform
      material itself rather than the coloured outer layer, replace them
      immediately. Any unintended release of your foot during a sprint is also a
      clear sign the cleats are past their service life.
  - question: Can worn cycling cleats cause knee pain?
    answer: >-
      Yes. As Anthony Walsh explains, gradual erosion of the cleat platform
      slowly alters your foot angle across thousands of pedal strokes. That
      shift in position places consistent, unnatural stress on the knee, leading
      to overuse injury that builds up over time rather than appearing all at
      once.
  - question: How do I keep the same cleat position when replacing cycling cleats?
    answer: >-
      Before removing the old cleat, trace its outline directly onto the sole of
      your shoe with a marker. Use that outline to position the replacement
      cleat in exactly the same spot. This ensures your foot angle and fore-aft
      position remain identical without the need to refit from scratch.
  - question: How often should cyclists replace their cleats?
    answer: >-
      There is no fixed mileage interval because wear depends on how much
      walking you do in your cycling shoes. Anthony Walsh recommends inspecting
      the cleat platform regularly rather than following a set schedule. Replace
      them as soon as the platform material shows wear, regardless of how long
      they have been fitted.
