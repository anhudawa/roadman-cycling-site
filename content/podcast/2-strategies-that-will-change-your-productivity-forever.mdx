faq:
  - question: What is the 90/90/1 rule for productivity?
    answer: >-
      The 90/90/1 rule means spending the first 90 minutes of each working day,
      for 90 consecutive days, on the single most important task that will move
      your top goal forward. You do this before checking email, news, or social
      media. Anthony Walsh credits this as one of the two frameworks that most
      transformed his output.
  - question: How do you block out distractions when trying to focus on deep work?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends what he calls "tight bubble focus," which
      combines a timed phone lockbox, no unnecessary browser tabs open, and
      focus audio from Brain.fm played through noise-cancelling headphones.
      These environmental controls remove the friction points that interrupt
      sustained concentration before they become a problem.
  - question: What is bubble time and how does it improve productivity?
    answer: >-
      Bubble time is a structured period of deep focus where you deliberately
      seal yourself off from all external interruptions using physical and
      digital controls. Walsh argues this practice separates high-output
      producers from average ones, because the quality of your environment
      determines the quality of your concentration.
  - question: Should you check email before starting work in the morning?
    answer: >-
      No. Walsh is clear that checking email, news, or social feeds at the start
      of the day pulls your attention towards other people's priorities rather
      than your own. The 90/90/1 rule is built on the principle that your first
      90 minutes belong entirely to your most important task.
