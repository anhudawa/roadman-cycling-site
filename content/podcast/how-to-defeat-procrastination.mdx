answerCapsule: >-
  Cycling coach Anthony Walsh argues that procrastination, which costs the
  average person an estimated 55 days per year, stems from internal resistance
  most often rooted in fear of failure or disappointment. His central
  recommendation is a procrastination log: when avoidance hits, write down what
  task is being delayed, what outcome feels threatening, and what emotion is
  present, then review entries to spot patterns. Walsh integrates this into a
  daily journal alongside a single priority and three gratitudes, treating
  procrastination as a diagnosable trigger rather than a character flaw.
keyTakeaways:
  - >-
    Procrastination usually signals fear — of failure, of falling short — not
    laziness or poor discipline.
  - >-
    Start a procrastination log: note the task, the outcome you're dreading, and
    the emotion you feel in that moment.
  - >-
    Review your log for repeat offenders — if you keep dodging evening sessions,
    dig into fueling, sleep, or caffeine timing as likely causes.
  - >-
    Anchor your daily journal to one priority and three gratitudes before
    anything else — it sets a baseline that makes avoidance harder to justify.
  - >-
    If the same task keeps getting pushed back, that pattern is data: treat it
    as a diagnostic, then build a specific fix around it.

faq:
  - question: How many days a year does procrastination waste?
    answer: >-
      Procrastination costs the average person an estimated 55 days per year.
      Cycling coach Anthony Walsh uses this figure to reframe procrastination
      not as a minor habit but as a serious drain on productive time that
      warrants deliberate action.
  - question: What is a procrastination log and how does it work?
    answer: >-
      A procrastination log is a written record you keep whenever you catch
      yourself avoiding a task. Anthony Walsh recommends writing down the task
      being delayed, the outcome that feels threatening, and the emotion present
      at that moment. Reviewing entries over time reveals patterns in your
      triggers, making it possible to address the root cause rather than just
      the symptom.
  - question: Why do people procrastinate even on tasks they want to complete?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh argues that procrastination is most often rooted in fear of
      failure or disappointment rather than laziness. The avoidance is a
      protective response to an internal threat, which is why willpower alone
      rarely fixes it. Identifying the specific fear attached to a task is the
      first step toward overcoming the resistance.
  - question: How do you stop procrastinating every day?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends building a short daily journalling routine that
      includes a single priority for the day, three gratitudes, and a
      procrastination log entry whenever avoidance arises. This treats
      procrastination as a diagnosable pattern rather than a character flaw, and
      consistent logging gives you the data needed to spot and break recurring
      cycles.
