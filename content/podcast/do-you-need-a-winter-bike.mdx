answerCapsule: >-
  Host Anthony Walsh argues that cyclists in wet, salted-road climates like
  Ireland and the UK benefit from a dedicated winter bike to protect their
  primary race machine from corrosion and reduce maintenance costs. The
  recommended build prioritises durability over performance: lower-tier
  groupsets such as Tiagra or 105, alloy rims rather than carbon, wider grippy
  tyres like Continental Ultra Sports around 27mm, and crucially full-length
  mudguards. Walsh notes that mudguards keep riders drier than any waterproof
  jacket and are a basic courtesy in group rides. Converting an outgoing race
  bike is presented as the most practical route.

faq:
  - question: Do I need a separate winter bike?
    answer: >-
      If you ride in a wet, salted-road climate like Ireland or the UK, a
      dedicated winter bike is worth having. It protects your primary race
      machine from corrosion and salt damage, which significantly reduces
      long-term maintenance costs. Anthony Walsh recommends converting an
      outgoing race bike rather than buying new.
  - question: What groupset should I put on a winter bike?
    answer: >-
      A lower-tier groupset such as Shimano Tiagra or 105 is the practical
      choice for winter riding. These are durable, widely serviceable, and far
      cheaper to replace when salt and grime take their toll. There is no reason
      to expose an expensive electronic or top-tier mechanical groupset to
      winter conditions.
  - question: Are mudguards worth it on a winter bike?
    answer: >-
      Full-length mudguards are one of the most effective upgrades you can make
      to a winter bike. Anthony Walsh points out that mudguards keep you drier
      than any waterproof jacket, and they also prevent road spray from soaking
      riders directly behind you. On group rides, turning up without them is
      widely considered poor etiquette.
  - question: What tyres are best for winter cycling?
    answer: >-
      A wider, grippy tyre around 27mm is the recommended choice for winter
      roads. Anthony Walsh suggests the Continental Ultra Sport as a solid
      option, as the additional width improves grip on wet and slippery
      surfaces. Avoid carbon rims in winter and opt for alloy wheels, which
      handle braking in the wet more reliably and are far cheaper to replace.
