faq:
  - question: Should cyclists hire a coach or just follow free training plans?
    answer: >-
      Hiring a coach accelerates progress far faster than self-directed trial
      and error. Anthony Walsh argues that paying for expert guidance in your
      twenties saves years of wasted training and prevents the ingrained bad
      habits that come from self-experimentation without accountability.
  - question: >-
      Is it better to focus on one cycling goal at a time or train for multiple
      events?
    answer: >-
      Committing fully to one goal at a time produces better results than
      splitting focus across several projects. Anthony Walsh recommends choosing
      a single target, directing all energy towards it, and only moving on once
      that objective is complete or the season demands a shift.
  - question: Why should cyclists do strength and conditioning training?
    answer: >-
      Strength and conditioning improves on-bike power output and protects
      against injury, but its benefits extend beyond cycling performance.
      Anthony Walsh emphasises that building muscle in your twenties supports
      long-term metabolic health and makes maintaining a healthy body
      composition significantly easier as you age.
  - question: Is yoga worth it for cyclists who are not flexible?
    answer: >-
      Yes, and being inflexible is precisely why cyclists should start. Anthony
      Walsh advocates adopting a beginner mindset and accepting that you will be
      poor at yoga initially, as consistency over time improves mobility,
      reduces injury risk, and supports recovery from heavy training blocks.
