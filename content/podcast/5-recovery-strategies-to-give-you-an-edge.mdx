  - question: 'When does fitness actually improve, during training or recovery?'
    answer: >-
      Fitness improves during recovery, not training. Training creates the
      potential for adaptation, but the actual gains are realised when the body
      repairs and rebuilds, primarily through human growth hormone release
      during deep sleep.
  - question: How do blue light blocking glasses help with cycling recovery?
    answer: >-
      Blue light after sundown suppresses melatonin production, making it harder
      to fall into deep, restorative sleep. Wearing blue-light blocking glasses
      in the evening protects melatonin levels and helps you reach the deep
      sleep stages where the most significant recovery and adaptation occur.
  - question: What should I eat or drink immediately after a hard bike ride?
    answer: >-
      A drink with a 4:1 ratio of carbohydrates to protein consumed within the
      post-ride glycogen replenishment window is the recommended approach.
      Chocolate milk is a practical, readily available option that hits this
      ratio and accelerates recovery between sessions.
  - question: Are sleep trackers and recovery apps worth using for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh advises caution with sleep and recovery trackers. Checking a
      poor score first thing in the morning can negatively affect your perceived
      readiness and actually harm performance more than the sleep deficit
      itself, meaning the psychological impact of a bad score can outweigh the
      physical one.
