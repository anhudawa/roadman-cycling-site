faq:
  - question: Why do cyclists procrastinate on training?
    answer: >-
      Cyclists often procrastinate on training because the task feels
      overwhelming or carries a fear of failure or discomfort. Instead of
      starting the session, riders substitute low-value tasks like cleaning an
      already-clean bike or reorganising their inbox, which feel productive but
      deliver no real progress.
  - question: How does procrastination affect cycling performance?
    answer: >-
      Procrastination disrupts training consistency, which is the single biggest
      driver of cycling improvement. Beyond missed sessions, the nagging
      awareness of an unfinished task prevents full presence during rest and
      recovery, meaning you get neither the training nor genuine rest.
  - question: How do I stop putting off important tasks and actually get things done?
    answer: >-
      The most effective approach is to identify the specific task you are
      avoiding and start it immediately, even for just a few minutes, before
      doing anything else. Recognising that low-value busywork is a substitution
      behaviour, not genuine productivity, is the first step to breaking the
      pattern.
  - question: What does "live slow ride fast" mean for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      "Live slow, ride fast" is a philosophy associated with professional
      cyclist Laurens ten Dam, advocating for a calm, unhurried approach to
      daily life in order to conserve energy and focus for riding. The idea is
      that chronic busyness and mental clutter outside of training undermine
      performance on the bike.
