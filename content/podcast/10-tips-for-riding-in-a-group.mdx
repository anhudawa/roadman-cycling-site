faq:
  - question: How should you ride at the front of a group ride?
    answer: >-
      Riding at the front is a leadership role, not just a turn at the wind. You
      must signal turns clearly, hold at least Zone 3 effort so the riders
      behind are not forced to freewheel, and refuse to sprint orange lights, as
      the group behind cannot see the signal changing and will follow you
      through.
  - question: What are the most important safety rules for group cycling?
    answer: >-
      Never remove your hands from the bars while riding in the bunch, because a
      solo crash immediately becomes a group crash. You should also queue behind
      stationary cars in single file rather than filtering through gaps,
      treating the group like an articulated truck that must pass as one unit.
  - question: What is the correct etiquette when riding in a cycling group?
    answer: >-
      Consistent effort and predictable behaviour are the foundations of good
      group ride etiquette. Avoid sudden accelerations or braking, signal
      hazards to riders behind you, and never half-wheel the rider alongside
      you, as it forces the pace up without agreement and disrupts the rhythm of
      the whole group.
  - question: Why are group rides dangerous and how can you make them safer?
    answer: >-
      Group rides become dangerous when riders at the front make selfish or
      unpredictable decisions, such as jumping amber lights or soft-pedalling
      through corners and forcing the bunch to surge. Anthony Walsh recommends
      treating every action at the front as one that affects every rider behind
      you, which shifts the mindset from individual riding to collective
      responsibility.
