faq:
  - question: What should beginner cyclists know before they start training?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh of Roonvo highlights five essentials: build a relationship
      with a local bike shop, join group rides, dress appropriately for the
      conditions, follow a structured training programme, and treat cycling as a
      team sport. Starting with these habits saves a lot of wasted effort and
      money compared to figuring them out through trial and error.
  - question: Is it better to buy a bike online or from a local bike shop?
    answer: >-
      A local bike shop offers fitting advice, mechanical support, and ongoing
      expertise that an online retailer cannot match. Anthony Walsh recommends
      building a genuine relationship with your local shop rather than chasing
      small discounts online, as the long-term value far outweighs any initial
      saving.
  - question: Why do cyclists spend so much time riding at moderate intensity?
    answer: >-
      Many beginners default to riding constantly at a moderate, uncomfortable
      pace, which Anthony Walsh calls "headless chicken" zone three training.
      This approach limits progress because it is too hard to allow proper
      recovery and too easy to drive meaningful adaptation. Structured training
      with deliberate easy and hard efforts produces far better results.
  - question: How important are group rides for beginner cyclists?
    answer: >-
      Group rides are one of the fastest ways to improve as a beginner because
      you absorb practical knowledge from more experienced riders that you
      simply cannot get from reading articles. Anthony Walsh specifically
      recommends joining a local club or group to learn skills like positioning,
      pacing, and etiquette in real riding conditions.
