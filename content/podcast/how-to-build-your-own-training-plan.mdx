faq:
  - question: How do I build my own cycling training plan from scratch?
    answer: >-
      Start by identifying your target event and work backwards from race day,
      allowing 26 to 36 weeks of preparation. Structure the programme into four
      phases: 12 weeks base, 8 weeks build, 6 weeks peak, and 1 week taper.
      Before scheduling any sessions, block out fixed family, work, and social
      commitments so training fits realistically around your life.
  - question: How many weeks out should I start training for a cycling event?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends beginning your structured training plan 26 to 36
      weeks before your target event. This window provides enough time to
      complete a full periodised cycle of base, build, peak, and taper phases
      without rushing any block. Starting earlier than 36 weeks risks losing
      specificity; starting later than 26 weeks compresses the base phase where
      aerobic foundations are built.
  - question: >-
      What is the difference between base, build, and peak phases in a cycling
      training plan?
    answer: >-
      The base phase (12 weeks) focuses on building aerobic capacity and general
      fitness. The build phase (8 weeks) introduces more intensity and begins to
      reflect the demands of your specific event. The peak phase (6 weeks)
      sharpens fitness with highly event-specific work, followed by a 1-week
      taper to arrive at race day fresh and primed.
  - question: Should I train in Zone 3 when building a cycling training plan?
    answer: >-
      Defaulting to Zone 3 for most sessions is a common mistake when
      self-coaching. Training demands should be matched to the specific type of
      event you are targeting rather than gravitating towards moderate-intensity
      riding out of habit. Structuring sessions around what your event actually
      requires produces far more relevant fitness adaptations.
