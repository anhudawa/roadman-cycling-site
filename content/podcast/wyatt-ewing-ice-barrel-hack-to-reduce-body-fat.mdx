## Key Takeaways

The cycling world spends enormous energy on power-to-weight ratios and nutrition periodisation, and that stuff matters, but most riders are completely ignoring a free daily intervention that takes 11 minutes. Ewing's protocol was simple: cold water, every day, minimum 3 minutes. His starting body fat was 15-16%. He was also coming out of a high-stress career where cortisol levels were chronically elevated, and high cortisol holds onto fat. Once that cortisol came down, the body composition followed. He thinks the cortisol piece was actually what made his response so dramatic. Cold exposure increases brown fat, which is the metabolically active kind, and the body burns through white fat to generate heat. Ray Cronox, a NASA material scientist Tim Ferris has spoken about, worked out that thermogenesis has to be part of the fat-loss equation because the calories-in-calories-out model alone doesn't account for athletes like Michael Phelps consuming 10,000-plus calories a day.

Ewing also had recurring bronchitis every 2-3 months before he started. In the 5-6 years since, it's come back once or twice. He's not claiming cold water cures illness, but his immune system response changed noticeably. It's the same with most of this stuff, the thing that actually works is the thing you do every day, and the Ice Barrel product exists because bathtubs are impractical, stock tanks are uncomfortable, and cold showers eventually stop being enough of a stimulus. His daily protocol in a Colorado winter was 33 degrees Fahrenheit, 3 to 11 minutes, with box breathing afterward. 4 seconds in, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Then flat on the floor to slow the breath back down.
