faq:
  - question: Is the Wicklow Way suitable for bikepacking?
    answer: >-
      The Wicklow Way is rideable but demanding, covering 122 kilometres with
      2,200 metres of climbing. Expect significant hike-a-bike sections, so it
      is not a route you can pedal entirely. Anthony Walsh completed it over two
      days on a loaded gravel bike and found it both rewarding and physically
      punishing.
  - question: How heavy is a fully loaded bikepacking setup?
    answer: >-
      A fully kitted bikepacking rig can easily exceed 20 kilograms once you
      factor in the bike, bags, camping gear, food, and water. That weight makes
      climbs considerably harder and increases the likelihood of having to push
      on technical or steep sections. Pack as light as practically possible
      without sacrificing safety essentials.
  - question: What inner tube valve length do I need for deep section carbon wheels?
    answer: >-
      Deep-section carbon rims typically require valves of 60mm or longer,
      depending on rim depth. A standard 40mm valve will not reach through the
      rim bed, making inflation impossible. Anthony Walsh made this exact
      mistake on the Wicklow Way, so always confirm valve length matches your
      rim depth before leaving home.
  - question: How long does it take to bikepack the Wicklow Way?
    answer: >-
      Most bikepackers split the Wicklow Way across two days, which is what
      Anthony Walsh did on his first attempt. The combination of rough terrain,
      hike-a-bike sections, and the loaded weight of the bike means daily
      mileage is slower than a typical gravel ride. Splitting it at roughly the
      midpoint gives a manageable daily effort.
