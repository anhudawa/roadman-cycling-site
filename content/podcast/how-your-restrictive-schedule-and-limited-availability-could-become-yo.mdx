  traditional winter periodization, training the fitness components that detrain
  fastest first: sprint power, then VO2 max, then threshold, and finally
  endurance. He calls this reverse periodization and reports strong results

  to schedule short, high-intensity blocks during dark winter months and save
  long endurance rides for spring, turning a restrictive schedule and limited
  daylight into a structural training advantage rather than a constraint.

faq:
  - question: What is reverse periodisation for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      Reverse periodisation flips the traditional training order by starting the
      winter with high-intensity work rather than long slow endurance miles.
      Anthony Walsh recommends beginning with sprint power, moving through VO2
      max and threshold, then saving long endurance rides for spring when
      daylight and free time are more readily available.
  - question: How should time-crunched cyclists structure their winter training?
    answer: >-
      Time-crunched cyclists should prioritise short, high-intensity sessions
      during the darker winter months and plan longer endurance rides for
      spring. This approach works with a restrictive schedule rather than
      against it, because the fitness components that detrain fastest, such as
      sprint power and VO2 max, are best trained in short, focused blocks.
  - question: Which fitness components detrain fastest if you stop cycling?
    answer: >-
      Sprint power and neuromuscular fitness detrain the quickest, followed by
      VO2 max, then threshold power, and finally aerobic endurance. This
      detraining hierarchy is the foundation of Anthony Walsh's reverse
      periodisation model, where the most perishable qualities are trained first
      in the programme.
  - question: >-
      Can a busy family and work schedule actually improve your cycling
      training?
    answer: >-
      Yes, when the programme is structured correctly. A restrictive schedule
      forces shorter sessions, which align naturally with the high-intensity
      work that should dominate winter training under a reverse periodisation
      model. Anthony Walsh reports strong results using this approach with
      Roadman Coaching clients who have limited availability during the week.
