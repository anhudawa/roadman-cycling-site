faq:
  - question: What cycling targets can I set during COVID instead of races?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends four unconventional six-week targets: a full
      power profile test across 10-second, 5-minute and 20-minute efforts; a
      specific Strava segment challenge; a multi-day place-to-place adventure
      ride; or a record attempt such as Everesting or a point-to-point time.
      Each gives you a concrete goal to structure training around when race
      calendars are empty.
  - question: How do I structure training when all my races have been cancelled?
    answer: >-
      Choose a specific unconventional target and reverse-engineer your training
      zones from the energy demands that target requires. A Strava segment
      dominated by a short climb, for example, demands different zone emphasis
      than a multi-day endurance ride. Matching your training to the
      physiological demands of the chosen goal keeps the programme purposeful
      and progressive.
  - question: What is Everesting cycling and how do I prepare for it?
    answer: >-
      Everesting involves repeatedly climbing a single hill until your total
      elevation gain reaches 8,848 metres, all in one continuous effort.
      Preparation should focus on extended climbing endurance, pacing strategy
      and fuelling, with training zones weighted heavily towards sustained
      aerobic output across the 5-minute and 20-minute power ranges.
  - question: How do I test my cycling power profile at home?
    answer: >-
      Perform maximal efforts at three durations: 10 seconds, 5 minutes and 20
      minutes, with adequate recovery between each. Record the average power for
      each effort to build a picture of your strengths and weaknesses across the
      neuromuscular, VO2 max and threshold energy systems. Repeat the test after
      a six-week training block to measure progress objectively.
