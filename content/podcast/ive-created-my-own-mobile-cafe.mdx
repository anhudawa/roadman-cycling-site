  In this episode of the Roadman Cycling Podcast, host Anthony Walsh responds to
  Ireland's Level 4 lockdown closure of cafés by building a mobile coffee setup
  on his bike, carrying a travel AeroPress, hot water flask, and Colombian
  coffee in a frame bag. He argues the mid-ride coffee stop functions as a
  psychological reward that reinforces training habits and shortens the
  perceived length of long endurance rides. The episode reframes pandemic
  restrictions as a prompt for inventive solutions rather than a reason to
  abandon winter training.

faq:
  - question: How do I make coffee on a bike ride without a cafe?
    answer: >-
      Carry a travel AeroPress, a small insulated flask of hot water, and
      pre-ground coffee in a frame bag. Anthony Walsh uses this exact setup on
      his road bike, brewing a fresh cup at a chosen stop point mid-ride as a
      direct replacement for a cafe visit.
  - question: What is the best way to carry coffee on a long bike ride?
    answer: >-
      A frame bag is the most practical option for storing a compact brewer like
      an AeroPress alongside a portion of ground coffee. A quality insulated
      flask keeps water hot long enough for a mid-ride brew, typically covering
      rides of two to four hours without significant heat loss.
  - question: Why do cyclists stop at cafes during long rides?
    answer: >-
      The cafe stop serves as a psychological reward that breaks a long ride
      into manageable sections, making the overall effort feel shorter. Anthony
      Walsh argues this mental structure reinforces consistent training habits,
      particularly during winter endurance blocks when motivation is harder to
      maintain.
  - question: Can you still cycle for training during a Covid lockdown in Ireland?
    answer: >-
      During Ireland's Level 4 restrictions, outdoor individual exercise
      including cycling was permitted within a defined radius of home. Cafes
      were closed, but Anthony Walsh adapted by building a mobile coffee setup
      so that the reward structure of a normal long ride could be preserved
      within the rules.
