faq:
  - question: How do I get back on track with diet after falling off?
    answer: >-
      Resume your normal eating habits at the very next meal rather than waiting
      for Monday or the start of a new month. Anthony Walsh draws on Sam
      Harris's "start again" concept, which holds that a single lapse does not
      justify abandoning the rest of the day or week. Progress is non-linear,
      and the speed of your recovery matters far more than the fact that you
      slipped up.
  - question: How do I stop one missed workout ruining my whole week?
    answer: >-
      Recognise that a missed session breaks the streak but does not erase your
      fitness or your programme. Anthony Walsh argues that resuming immediately,
      even imperfectly, is the correct response rather than waiting for a clean
      slate on Monday. Expecting setbacks in advance also helps, because you
      have already decided how you will respond when they happen.
  - question: What is the Sam Harris start again concept?
    answer: >-
      It is the principle that whenever you lose focus or break a habit, the
      correct move is to start again in that very moment rather than writing off
      the remaining time. Anthony Walsh applies this directly to training, diet,
      and daily routines to show that the gap between a lapse and recovery
      should be as short as possible. The idea removes the false logic that a
      bad morning makes the rest of the day a write-off.
  - question: Does an accountability partner actually help you stay consistent?
    answer: >-
      Yes, and attaching a small financial penalty to the arrangement increases
      its effectiveness considerably. Anthony Walsh recommends pairing up with
      someone who has a similar goal and agreeing that missing a target costs
      you both a set amount of money. The mild financial sting makes the
      commitment concrete and gives you a reason to act beyond willpower alone.
