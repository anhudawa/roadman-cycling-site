answerCapsule: >-
  Tour de France stage recovery prioritises the short turnaround to tomorrow's
  race. When rapid glycogen restoration is required in under four hours,
  position guidance supports about 1.2 g/kg/hour carbohydrate, or 0.8 g/kg/hour
  carbohydrate with 0.2-0.4 g/kg/hour protein. Tart cherry and cold-water
  immersion may support selected short-term outcomes, but effects depend on the
  protocol and outcome. Repeated cold-water immersion most clearly threatens
  strength adaptation; endurance effects are mixed, so it is not an automatic
  "undo button" after cycling. Massage can slightly reduce soreness without a
  proven direct performance gain. Pre-sleep protein can raise overnight protein
  synthesis, while daily protein intake and the rider's starting intake remain
  important context.

faq:
  - question: How do Tour de France riders recover between stages?
    answer: >-
      Public footage and rider accounts show immediate food and drink, a team-bus
      meal, massage and sometimes cold-water immersion, followed by a load-matched
      evening meal. Exact routines vary by rider, team, stage and conditions;
      this page does not claim access to Pogačar's private protocol.
  - question: What should amateur cyclists eat after a hard ride?
    answer: >-
      Urgency depends on the next session. When rapid glycogen restoration is
      required in less than four hours, position guidance supports about 1.2
      g/kg/hour carbohydrate, or 0.8 g/kg/hour carbohydrate with 0.2-0.4
      g/kg/hour protein. With a longer turnaround, total intake across meals matters.
  - question: Is tart cherry juice worth it for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      It is optional. A 2026 review found possible benefits for selected strength
      and inflammatory outcomes, but results were heterogeneous, certainty was
      low to moderate and the pooled soreness result was not significant. It is
      not established that every Tour team uses it or every cyclist benefits.
  - question: Do ice baths hurt your training gains?
    answer: >-
      Repeated cold-water immersion after resistance training can attenuate
      strength gains. Reviews do not show the same clear harm to endurance
      adaptation, and short-term recovery effects depend on the protocol and
      exercise. It is a trade-off to individualise, not a universal undo button.
  - question: Does massage actually speed up recovery?
    answer: >-
      A large meta-analysis found no direct improvement in strength, sprint,
      endurance or fatigue, with small benefits for soreness and flexibility.
      A rider may still value comfort or routine, but claims that massage flushes
      lactate or guarantees faster performance recovery are not supported.
  - question: Should I eat protein before bed?
    answer: >-
      Pre-sleep protein can increase overnight protein synthesis after evening
      exercise. It is not proven to improve next-day performance for every
      cyclist, and a professional-cyclist trial found no benefit when riders
      already consumed more than 2.5 g/kg/day of protein.
  - question: Should amateurs copy professional recovery routines?
    answer: >-
      Copy decisions, not theatre. Prioritise enough food, fluid, warmth and
      sleep, then ask how soon the next hard session arrives. Supplements,
      massage and cold water have narrower outcome-specific evidence and should
      not displace those basics.

Grand Tour recovery is a short-turnaround problem: finish one stage, replace what the day removed, travel, sleep and start again. Public footage and rider accounts show food and drink at the finish, team-bus meals, massage and sometimes cold-water immersion. Exact routines vary, and nobody outside a rider's team should claim access to Pogačar's private protocol.

This episode is therefore about the decisions behind between-stage recovery, not a secret checklist. It also has a different search job from our [first-60-minutes Pogačar recovery episode](/podcast/ep-31-5-things-pogacar-always-does-after-a-ride): this page follows the rider from finish line through the evening and asks what must be ready for tomorrow.

## Key Takeaways

The useful question is not whether a recovery tool "works" in the abstract. Ask which outcome, for which rider, after which session, and how soon the next demanding effort arrives. A Tour rider may accept a different trade-off from someone trying to build strength across a twelve-week block.

## Rapid Refuelling Is A Context, Not A Deadline

Stage racing creates a large daily carbohydrate demand, so teams start feeding early and continue across the afternoon and evening. The clearest numerical guidance is narrower than the episode's original claim: if rapid glycogen restoration is required in less than four hours, consider about 1.2 g/kg/hour carbohydrate, or 0.8 g/kg/hour carbohydrate with 0.2-0.4 g/kg/hour protein. That describes the first hours of a compressed recovery period; it is not a universal instruction to consume one fixed dose within sixty minutes of every ride.

An amateur with another long day tomorrow can use the same principle: begin eating, choose tolerable carbohydrate-rich foods and keep the total day in view. When the next demanding session is much later, the clock is less important than replacing enough energy and carbohydrate across normal meals. Use the [post-ride recovery nutrition guide](/blog/post-ride-recovery-nutrition-cyclists) for the practical decision.

## Tart Cherry And Cold Water Have Different Evidence

The newest tart-cherry meta-analysis in trained athletes found possible benefits for selected strength and inflammatory outcomes after exercise-induced muscle damage. It also reported substantial heterogeneity, low-to-moderate certainty, sensitivity to individual studies and no significant pooled effect on soreness. That is enough to call tart cherry an optional experiment, not enough to promise better legs tomorrow or claim every Tour team uses it. Our [tart cherry evidence guide](/blog/cycling-tart-cherry-juice-recovery-guide) covers the decision.

Cold-water immersion has a clearer resistance-training caution. Meta-analyses find that repeated use alongside resistance training can attenuate strength gains, particularly in protocols cooling the trained limbs. Reviews describe endurance adaptation as unaffected or equivocal, however, and short-term recovery effects vary with exercise, timing and protocol. A cyclist combining riding with strength work should avoid turning a context-specific tool into an automatic habit. See the [cold-water immersion guide](/blog/cold-water-immersion-cyclists).

## Massage, Dinner And Pre-Sleep Protein

Massage is better described as a comfort tool than a direct performance intervention. A meta-analysis of 29 studies found no improvement in strength, jump, sprint, endurance or fatigue, with small benefits for soreness and flexibility. If a rider values the routine, that can be enough; it does not validate lactate-flushing or parasympathetic guarantees.

The evening meal has a simpler job: help replace the day's energy, carbohydrate, protein and fluid in a form the rider can tolerate. The large numbers in the episode come from one professional rider's account and illustrate scale, not a prescription for every athlete. Match the meal to the work completed, the next day's demand and the rider's total intake.

[Pre-sleep protein evidence](/blog/bedtime-protein-cyclists-recovery-protocol) supports an overnight protein-synthesis response after evening exercise, especially when the strategy helps reach an appropriate daily total. It does not guarantee better next-day performance. In a six-day professional-cyclist trial, 40 g of casein before sleep performed no better than afternoon protein or an energy-matched carbohydrate control; all riders were already consuming more than 2.5 g/kg/day of protein.

## What This Means For Your Riding

Start with the constraints that matter: time until the next hard session, current food and fluid intake, sleep opportunity, soreness, illness or injury signals, temperature and travel. Then choose the smallest intervention that solves the actual problem. Recovery should not become another source of work.
