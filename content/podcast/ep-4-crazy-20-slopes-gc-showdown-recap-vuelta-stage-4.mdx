  Stage 4 of the 2024 Vuelta a España delivered the race's first genuine GC
  shakeup on the Pico Villuercas finish, a 14.6km climb whose final three
  kilometres never dipped below 15% gradient and included a 600-metre ramp above
  20%. Primož Roglič rode bottom to top at the front and confirmed his form,
  while pre-race contenders including Adam Yates were exposed. The episode also
  covers Julian Alaphilippe's confirmed move to Tudor for 2025 and Kaden Groves
  reversing course to re-sign with Alpecin-Deceuninck.
keyTakeaways:
  - >-
    On steep ramps above 15%, your position in the group matters as much as your
    watts — getting boxed behind someone who's zigzagging or clipping out can
    cost you more than a bad day in the legs.
  - >-
    Heat adaptation works: targeted sauna or hot sessions before a big
    summer event increase blood plasma volume and improve your sweat response,
    helping you stay in the red zone longer.
  - >-
    Don't sprint through what you think is the finish line — always drive a full
    bike length past it before you celebrate, no matter how certain you are.
  - >-
    Losing small amounts of time on every hard climb compounds fast; even 30
    seconds on stage four can put you well over a minute down before the race
    reaches its key mountain stages.
  - >-
    Sodium and sugar are separate problems on long hot rides — you may not need
    more carbs, but you almost certainly need more salt, especially when you can
    see white crystals on your kit afterwards.
