faq:
  - question: What is the Live Slow Ride Fast philosophy in cycling?
    answer: >-
      Live Slow Ride Fast is a philosophy championed by Laurens ten Dam that
      pairs deliberate, unhurried recovery off the bike with high-quality
      training on it. The idea is that hard efforts are only sustainable
      long-term when you properly slow down in daily life, whether that means
      brewing coffee properly, sleeping well, or riding socially with friends
      rather than always training at intensity.
  - question: What is Dirty Kanzelled gravel event and how does it work?
    answer: >-
      Dirty Kanzelled is a 300-kilometre gravel event organised by Laurens ten
      Dam that allows participants to start from anywhere and converge on a
      common finish. It gained significant attention in 2020 when professional
      races were cancelled and riders including Wout van Aert and Niki Terpstra
      took part. The format is designed to be inclusive and adventurous rather
      than a conventional mass-start race.
  - question: What did Laurens ten Dam achieve in his professional cycling career?
    answer: >-
      Laurens ten Dam rode as a professional for 16 years at World Tour level,
      competing for teams including Rabobank, Belkin, Giant-Alpecin,
      LottoNL-Jumbo, and CCC. He recorded top-10 finishes at both the Tour de
      France and the Vuelta a España, establishing himself as a reliable Grand
      Tour climber throughout the 2000s and 2010s.
  - question: How is Laurens ten Dam growing gravel racing in Europe?
    answer: >-
      Since retiring from professional road racing, ten Dam has focused on
      organising and promoting gravel events across Europe and the United
      States, with Dirty Kanzelled as his flagship project. He uses his profile
      from a long pro career to attract both elite riders and amateur cyclists
      to the format, aiming to build a gravel culture in Europe comparable to
