answerCapsule: >-
  The 2021 Milan-Sanremo, won by Trek-Segafredo's Jasper Stuyven in a late solo
  move, is broken down by host Anthony Walsh on the Roadman Cycling Podcast. The
  episode analyses how Ineos Grenadiers' aggressive tactic of placing time-trial
  specialist Filippo Ganna on the front of the Cipressa and Poggio effectively
  set up Julian Alaphilippe's attack rather than their own riders. Walsh argues
  the result demonstrates why the 300-kilometre Monument should not be
  shortened, and flags Remco Evenepoel as the rider to watch for the remainder
  of 2021.
keyTakeaways:
  - >-
    Ineos putting Ganna on the front of the Cipressa and Poggio essentially did
    Alaphilippe's lead-out work for him — team tactics only count if you have a
    genuine finisher to protect.
  - >-
    When no rider has teammates left in a late breakaway, nobody wants to blink
    first — use that hesitation to launch early and make them regret waiting.
  - >-
    Stuyven won because he treated a 50-metre gap like a finish line rather than
    a reason to ease up — commit fully or don't go at all.
  - >-
    Trek's strength comes from having no single superstar, which means opponents
    can't isolate one wheel — a balanced roster creates unpredictable,
    hard-to-mark threats.
  - >-
    Keep an eye on Evenepoel returning from injury — high expectations are
    already baked in, but his results could still surprise even cynical
    observers.

  - question: How did Ineos Grenadiers ride Milan-Sanremo 2021?
    answer: >-
      Ineos Grenadiers placed time-trial specialist Filippo Ganna on the front
      of both the Cipressa and the Poggio climbs to drive a hard pace. As
      Anthony Walsh analyses on the Roadman Cycling Podcast, this tactic
      ultimately set up Julian Alaphilippe's attack rather than benefiting their
      own protected riders.

      true Monument. The length creates the conditions for unpredictable results

      favourites.
