  retrospection," a mental framing technique for getting more out of each day on
  the bike. The premise: imagine a future self, physically unable to ride,

  moment with that gratitude. Walsh pairs this with a nightly autobiography

  verdicts on the sport.

faq:
  - question: What is prospective retrospection in cycling?
    answer: >-
      Prospective retrospection is a mental framing technique where you imagine
      a future version of yourself, no longer able to ride, looking back on your
      current riding days as the good old days. Anthony Walsh introduces it on
      the Roadman Cycling Podcast as a way to re-enter each ride with genuine
      gratitude rather than taking it for granted.
  - question: How do I appreciate cycling more and stop taking it for granted?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends picturing yourself as an older, physically
      limited version of yourself who would give anything to be out on the bike
      today. Returning to your current moment with that perspective makes
      ordinary rides feel like the privilege they actually are.
  - question: How do I reflect on my day as a cyclist to make it more meaningful?
    answer: >-
      Walsh suggests a nightly autobiography exercise where you ask whether the
      day's actions would make a chapter worth reading. If the answer is no, it
      signals a need to be more deliberate the following day rather than simply
      going through the motions.
  - question: How should I think about a crash or setback in a race?
    answer: >-
      Walsh advises reframing a crash or poor result as a lesson rather than a
      verdict on your ability or the sport itself. Treating setbacks as
      information rather than failure keeps them from becoming reasons to quit
      or lose motivation.
