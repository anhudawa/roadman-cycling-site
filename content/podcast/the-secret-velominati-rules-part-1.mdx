  The Velominati Rules are a published, unofficial and deliberately
  tongue-in-cheek code of road-cycling etiquette maintained by Velominati, the
  self-described “Keepers of the Cog.” They are culture, not UCI regulations or
  safety advice. In part one, Anthony Walsh discusses Rule 5, sharp tan lines,
  bad-weather riding, the n+1 bike joke and the etiquette of joining an
  unfamiliar group. The official Velominati page remains the source of record.

faq:
  - question: What are the Velominati rules for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      The Velominati Rules are a published, unofficial and tongue-in-cheek code
      of road-cycling etiquette maintained at velominati.com. They cover bikes,
      kit, riding behaviour and café culture. They are not UCI regulations,
      traffic law, bike-fitting guidance or safety rules.
  - question: What is the n+1 rule in cycling?
    answer: >-
      The n+1 rule states that the correct number of bikes to own is always one
      more than you currently have, while s-1 jokes about stopping one bike
      short of separation from a partner. It is cycling humour, not purchasing
      advice.
  - question: What is Velominati Rule 19 about wheel sucking?
    answer: >-
      Rule 19 states that latching onto an unfamiliar group and sitting in the
      wheels without contributing is poor etiquette. The correct approach is to
      introduce yourself and ask before joining the group, rather than silently
      taking a free ride from strangers. Real group safety and the ride leader's
      instructions take priority over the joke code.
  - question: What does the Velominati say about cycling in bad weather?
    answer: >-
      The Velominati treat riding in rain, cold, and poor conditions as a
      badge of commitment. It is deliberately exaggerated; riders should use
      their own judgement about visibility, road conditions, temperature and
      safety.
