  single most useful mindset shift for athletes, creators, and professionals is
  starting projects before feeling qualified or equipped. Drawing on his own
  podcasting trajectory—early episodes recorded on a camera shotgun mic with
  poor audio—he frames "working to publish" as the antidote to research-mode

  now and iterate the production, skill, and audience over time, treating
  criticism from non-supporters as a useful filter rather than a reason to
  delay.

faq:
  - question: How do I start a project when I don't feel ready?
    answer: >-
      Start anyway, using whatever equipment or skills you currently have.
      Anthony Walsh of the Roadman Cycling Podcast began recording on a camera
      shotgun mic with poor audio quality, then improved over time. Waiting
      until you feel ready is a form of procrastination dressed up as
      preparation.
  - question: How do I stop procrastinating and just start creating?
    answer: >-
      Shift from "researching to learn" mode into "working to publish" mode.
      Anthony Walsh argues that shipping imperfect work forces real-world
      feedback and skill growth far faster than continuing to plan. The first
      version does not need to be polished; it needs to exist.
  - question: Is it okay to publish imperfect work?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Imperfect work published now will always outperform perfect work that
      never gets finished. You iterate on production quality, skills, and
      audience over time, and each published piece teaches you more than
      additional preparation ever could.
  - question: How do I deal with criticism when starting something new?
    answer: >-
      Treat negative feedback from people who are not properly invested in your
      success as a useful filter rather than a reason to stop. Anthony Walsh
      recommends distinguishing between constructive critique from supporters
      and discouragement from bystanders, and only letting the former influence
      your decisions.
