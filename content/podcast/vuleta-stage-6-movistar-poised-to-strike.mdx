Movistar have Enric Mas at 25 seconds, Superman Lopez at 36, Valverde at 41. That's three riders in the top five and a new directeur sportif who looks like he actually wants to win. The question is whether they play it smart or waste it chasing a team prize nobody cares about.

## Key Takeaways

Magnus Kort Nielsen held on from the break today because the peloton got the calculation wrong. The break went smarter than usual — when Jumbo started winding it up with 40k to go, the break matched them, and the gap never came down the way the sprinters' teams planned. Nielsen held on, Roglic rolled in second looking completely in control, and Kenny Elissonde lost the red jersey on the climb. Quickstep's Bagioli got second. Mas fifth. Insane ride from Mas, that kind of climbing form doesn't appear from nowhere going into Milan-San Remo next season.

Roglic is the favourite and he looks it. But the workload question is real. He rode the Tour, then the Olympics where he won the time trial, and now he's straight into the Vuelta. That's a serious block of racing. At some point the legs just go. Movistar's best move is to send Lopez and Valverde up the road into breaks on the hard stages ahead, make Jumbo-Visma burn matches chasing, and see which of their GC riders comes through freshest. Three hard stages before the rest day. If they play it right, we won't even see the damage until the day before the rest day.
