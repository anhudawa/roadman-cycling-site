faq:
  - question: How do you mentally recover from a cycling crash?
    answer: >-
      Mental recovery after a crash is often harder than physical healing and
      must be treated as a priority, not an afterthought. Anthony Walsh
      recommends addressing morale management before returning to structured
      training, as unresolved fear and lost confidence can permanently end a
      cycling career even when the physical injuries are minor.
  - question: How long should you wait before cycling again after an injury?
    answer: >-
      There is no single timeline, because returning too early without fixing
      the root cause of the injury makes a second occurrence both physically
      worse and psychologically devastating. Anthony Walsh advises diagnosing
      and resolving the underlying cause first, whether that is a bike fit
      issue, fatigue, or technique, rather than masking symptoms with
      painkillers and rushing back to riding.
  - question: How do you come back to cycling after a serious crash?
    answer: >-
      A structured return protocol works best, starting with low-intensity
      riding to rebuild confidence before adding load or speed. Anthony Walsh
      draws on crashes at 70kph in the Pyrenees and a high-speed bunch sprint
      incident in Detroit to argue that riders who skip this graduated approach
      frequently never fully return to the sport.
  - question: Why do cyclists retire after seemingly minor injuries?
    answer: >-
      A minor injury that is not properly treated at its root cause sets the
      rider up for a repeat incident, and the second crash carries a compounded
      psychological toll that many riders cannot overcome. Anthony Walsh
      highlights that it is rarely the severity of a single crash that ends
      careers, but rather the accumulated mental damage from repeated incidents
      that were never properly rehabilitated.
