keyTakeaways:
  - >-
    Wait a full 10 symptom-free days before touching the bike after COVID — not
    10 days from testing positive.
  - >-
    After those 10 days, ease back with a return-to-train phase first; don't
    jump straight into VO2 or threshold work.
  - >-
    Chest pain, shortness of breath, or heart arrhythmia post-COVID are red
    flags — get medical screening before riding again.
  - >-
    A resting HR 10 beats above your baseline and on-bike HR 30–40 beats too
    high are signs something is properly wrong, not just detraining.
  - >-
    If symptoms persist or return after rest, stop telling yourself stories and
    book the GP appointment — the worst outcome is knowing the truth.

    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends waiting a minimum of 10 symptom-free days before
      resuming any training after COVID-19. Returning too soon risks cardiac

  - question: why is my heart rate so high when cycling after covid
    answer: >-
      Post-viral inflammation, particularly suspected myocarditis, can elevate
      on-bike heart rates by 30-40 beats above normal training zones. Walsh
      noticed his resting heart rate sitting roughly 10 beats above his usual
      baseline of 32-36 bpm, with effort-based heart rates far exceeding
      expected ranges during rides.
  - question: can covid cause myocarditis in athletes
    answer: >-
      Yes, COVID-19 is associated with post-viral cardiac inflammation,
      including myocarditis, in endurance athletes. Walsh reports symptoms
      consistent with this condition following his infection, and stresses that
      any chest pain or arrhythmia after COVID warrants immediate medical
      screening before training resumes.
  - question: how to return to cycling training after covid 19
    answer: >-
      Walsh advises a structured seven-day return-to-train phase before
      attempting any intensity work such as VO2 efforts. Jumping straight back
      into hard training after COVID significantly increases the risk of
      prolonged cardiac symptoms and a much longer overall recovery period.
