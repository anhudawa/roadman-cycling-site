faq:
  - question: Is overnight success a real thing?
    answer: >-
      Overnight success is largely a myth. What looks like a sudden rise is
      almost always the visible end result of years of quiet, consistent work
      that happened out of public view.
  - question: How long does it take to become successful at something new?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends committing to a minimum two-year horizon before
      judging whether something is working. Quitting before that point means
      abandoning progress that is almost certainly already building beneath the
      surface.
  - question: Why do people quit new goals so quickly?
    answer: >-
      The primary cause is social media comparison. Measuring your early
      progress against someone else's curated highlight reel is a broken
      framework, because you are contrasting a work-in-progress against a
      polished peak moment that took years to reach.
  - question: How did Primož Roglič become a professional cyclist so late?
    answer: >-
      Roglič appeared to rise rapidly in cycling, but his success was built on a
      decade of high-level endurance training as a ski jumper. His aerobic base
      was already well developed before he ever turned professional on the bike,
      making his apparent overnight success anything but.
