answerCapsule: >-
  Australian WorldTour rider Chris Harper of Pinarello Q36.5 details his exact
  fueling for a Giro d'Italia mountain stage, totalling roughly 6,800 calories
  across the day. Breakfast hits 200g of carbs via pancakes, banana, yogurt and
  toast; on-bike intake ramps from 80g to 120g of carbs per hour; post-stage
  recovery combines a tart cherry shot, a fructose-boosted shake and a rice or
  wrap-based meal. Harper notes rest days still require around 3,700 calories,
  and that modern carb targets have replaced the ketogenic approaches
  popularised by Team Sky a decade ago.
keyTakeaways:
  - >-
    Start your first on-bike hour at 80g of carbs, then step up to 120g per hour
    for the harder hours that follow.
  - >-
    Front-load breakfast with easily digestible carbs — pancakes, banana, yogurt
    and toast can hit 200g without feeling heavy before a stage.
  - >-
    Time your final pre-race snack as late as possible — Harper was eating his
    rice cake 30 seconds before leaving the bus.
  - >-
    Rest days still need serious calories; 3,700 kcal on a recovery day is not
    under-fueling, it's the baseline for adapting to Grand Tour load.
  - >-
    The Team Sky keto image was likely deliberate misdirection — copying
    low-carb approaches from social media posts can actively harm young riders.

  - question: How many calories do pro cyclists eat during a Grand Tour stage?
    answer: >-
      According to WorldTour rider Chris Harper, a full Giro d'Italia mountain
      stage day totals roughly 6,800 calories. That figure includes a 200g
      carbohydrate breakfast, on-bike fuelling of 80 to 120g of carbs per hour,
      and a structured post-stage recovery meal.
  - question: What do pro cyclists eat for breakfast before a mountain stage?
    answer: >-
      Chris Harper's pre-stage breakfast at the Giro d'Italia includes pancakes,
      banana, yogurt and toast, adding up to around 200g of carbohydrates. The
      goal is to arrive at the start line with glycogen stores fully topped up
      without causing gastrointestinal distress during the stage.
  - question: How many carbs per hour do pro cyclists eat during a race?
    answer: >-
      Chris Harper targets 80g of carbohydrates per hour at the lower end and
      ramps that up to 120g per hour during the hardest stages of the Giro.
      Reaching the higher end requires a mix of glucose and fructose sources so
      the gut can absorb carbohydrate through multiple transport pathways
      simultaneously.
  - question: What should cyclists eat for recovery after a hard stage race day?
    answer: >-
      Chris Harper's post-stage recovery routine combines a tart cherry shot, a
      fructose-boosted shake and a rice or wrap-based meal. The tart cherry
      targets inflammation, the shake restores muscle glycogen rapidly, and the
      solid meal provides sustained recovery nutrition in the hours that follow.
  - question: How many calories do pro cyclists eat on rest days?
    answer: >-
      Chris Harper still consumes around 3,700 calories on Giro d'Italia rest
      days, even without racing. Calorie intake stays high because the body
      continues repairing muscle tissue and restoring glycogen between stages,
      and dropping intake too sharply would compromise performance in the days
      that follow.
