answerCapsule: >-
  Host Anthony Walsh, a cycling coach, lays out a head-to-toe system for
  dressing on cold-weather training rides, built around checking the forecast
  the night before and kitting up from the ground up. Key recommendations
  include neoprene shoe covers (which absorb water as added insulation), merino
  wool socks, bib tights with a chamois rather than shorts with leg warmers, and
  a merino base layer—short-sleeved above zero, long-sleeved below. The
  underlying principle: winter cycling is a high-risk environment where
  inadequate kit, not just inexperience, is what makes riders unsafe.
keyTakeaways:

    ground up — feet first, head last.
  - >-
    Choose bib tights with a built-in chamois over shorts and leg warmers; it's
    a cleaner, warmer system for winter riding.
  - >-
    Pick neoprene for shoe covers and gloves — it absorbs water and uses that
    moisture as extra insulation rather than letting you freeze.
  - >-
    A merino wool base layer is your winter default: short-sleeve above zero,
    long-sleeve below, and always fitted close to the skin.
  - >-

    nothing and gets you out of more cold-weather pinches than any expensive

faq:
  - question: What should I wear cycling in cold weather?
    answer: >-
      Start from the ground up: neoprene shoe covers, merino wool socks, bib
      tights with a chamois, and a merino base layer. Switch to a long-sleeved
      base layer when temperatures drop below zero. Cycling coach Anthony Walsh
      recommends checking the forecast the night before so you can kit up
      correctly rather than guessing on the morning.
  - question: Are bib tights better than leg warmers for winter cycling?
    answer: >-
      Bib tights with a built-in chamois are the better choice for cold-weather
      riding. Leg warmers can slip, leave gaps, and expose the knee to cold air,
      which is a real injury risk. Bib tights provide consistent coverage and
      eliminate the cold spot that forms where shorts and warmers meet.
  - question: What shoe covers are best for cycling in the rain and cold?
    answer: >-
      Neoprene shoe covers are the recommended option for winter training.
      Unlike waterproof covers that keep water out entirely, neoprene absorbs a
      small amount of water which then acts as an additional layer of insulation
      around the foot. They hold up well in wet and cold conditions that would
      leave standard covers failing quickly.
  - question: What base layer should I wear for winter cycling?
    answer: >-
      A merino wool base layer is the best option for cold-weather training
      rides. Wear a short-sleeved version when temperatures are above zero, and
      switch to long-sleeved below zero. Merino regulates temperature
      effectively, manages moisture without becoming clammy, and keeps you
      comfortable across a wide range of conditions.
