faq:
  - question: how to build mental toughness for cycling
    answer: >-
      Mental toughness is built through deliberate daily exposure to discomfort,
      such as riding when you have no motivation or completing sessions in bad
      weather. Anthony Walsh argues that consistency in hard moments compounds
      over time into a significant advantage over competitors who quit at the
      first sign of resistance. The goal is to develop what David Goggins calls
      a calloused mind, where difficulty no longer triggers the impulse to stop.
  - question: what does "the obstacle is the way" mean for athletes
    answer: >-
      The Stoic principle means that friction and resistance are not reasons to
      quit but signals that you are on the correct path. Walsh reframes it
      practically for cyclists by arguing that whenever training feels hard or
      uncomfortable, that feeling confirms you are doing the right thing. Most
      competitors stop at that point, which is precisely why pushing through it
      creates a real competitive edge.
  - question: why is it important to do hard things every day
    answer: >-
      Doing hard things daily trains your mind to treat discomfort as normal
      rather than as a threat. Walsh contends that modern life makes softness
      the default, so athletes who deliberately choose difficulty separate
      themselves from the majority who avoid it. Over time, this builds a
      discipline advantage that is harder to close than any physical fitness
      gap.
  - question: how do you stay disciplined with cycling training when you lack motivation
    answer: >-
      Motivation is unreliable, so discipline must replace it as the foundation
      of a training programme. Walsh recommends riding on the days you least
      want to ride, because those sessions do more to build mental resilience
      than the sessions you look forward to. Treating low-motivation days as
      deliberate practice, rather than obstacles, is what separates consistent
      performers from inconsistent ones.
