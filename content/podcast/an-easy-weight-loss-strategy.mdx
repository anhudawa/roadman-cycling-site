answerCapsule: >-
  Host Anthony Walsh, a cycling coach, outlines a simple weight loss protocol
  that aligns eating windows with training load rather than restricting
  calories. On rest days, use a compressed six-hour feeding window, skipping
  breakfast and eating between roughly 2pm and 8pm. Do recovery rides under 60
  minutes fasted, and ride the first hour of endurance sessions fasted before
  consuming up to one gram of carbohydrate per kilogram of bodyweight per hour.
  On interval days, eat normally to protect training adaptations.
keyTakeaways:
  - >-
    On rest days, compress your eating into a six-hour window — roughly 2pm to
    8pm — and skip breakfast entirely.
  - >-
    Keep recovery rides under 60 minutes and do them fasted; eat normally once
    you're back home.
  - >-
    On endurance rides, start fasted and only begin fuelling after the first
    hour, targeting around 1g of carbs per kg of bodyweight per hour.
  - >-
    Never restrict food on interval days — eating normally there protects the
    training adaptations you're working hard for.
  - >-
    You don't need to overhaul your diet for this to work; the compressed window
    alone limits total intake even if food quality stays imperfect.

faq:
  - question: How do I lose weight while cycling without losing performance?
    answer: >-
      Align your eating windows with your training load rather than cutting
      calories across the board. On rest days and easy recovery rides, compress
      your feeding window or ride fasted, then eat normally on interval days to
      protect training quality and adaptation.
  - question: Should I do fasted cycling for weight loss?
    answer: >-
      Fasted riding works well for short recovery sessions under 60 minutes and
      for the first hour of longer endurance rides. Anthony Walsh recommends
      avoiding fasted training on interval days, as under-fuelling hard sessions
      will compromise adaptation and ultimately slow progress.
  - question: What is a 6 hour eating window for weight loss?
    answer: >-
      A six-hour feeding window means compressing all your meals into a six-hour
      period, for example eating between 2pm and 8pm and skipping breakfast.
      Anthony Walsh uses this approach on rest days to create a calorie deficit
      without disrupting training performance.
  - question: >-
      How many carbs should I eat during a long training ride when trying to
      lose weight?
    answer: >-
      Consume up to one gram of carbohydrate per kilogram of bodyweight per hour
      once you are past the first fasted hour of an endurance session. This
      keeps energy available for sustained effort while still taking advantage
      of the fat-burning benefits of starting the ride fasted.
