faq:
  - question: How do I motivate myself to go for a bike ride when I don't feel like it?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends using the "last time meditation" before your
      ride. Tell yourself this could be the last time you ever do this ride, and
      your brain shifts from resistance to full engagement almost immediately.
      The technique overrides procrastination by making the familiar feel
      significant.
  - question: What is the last time meditation and how does it work?
    answer: >-
      The last time meditation is a Stoic-derived mental practice where you
      consciously frame an activity as potentially its final occurrence. Because
      everything does have a last time, the thought is not forced or
      false. This reframing sharpens focus and produces a more present,
      enjoyable experience.
  - question: How do Stoic techniques help with motivation in sport?
    answer: >-
      Stoic philosophy encourages awareness of impermanence, which strips away
      complacency and routine numbness. Anthony Walsh applied this by walking
      the medieval walls of Girona as if for the last time, after dozens of
      previous visits, and found it his most engaged experience yet. The same
      principle transfers directly to cycling, training, or any repeated
      activity.
  - question: How do I stop procrastinating before a workout or bike ride?
    answer: >-
      Frame the session as potentially the last one you will ever do. This
      single mental shift removes the sense that there will always be another
      opportunity tomorrow. Walsh reports it produces an immediate motivation
      boost and gets you out the door with a positive attitude rather than
      reluctance.
