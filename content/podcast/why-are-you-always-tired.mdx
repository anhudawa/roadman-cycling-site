faq:
  - question: Why am I always tired no matter how much sleep I get?
    answer: >-
      Chronic tiredness that persists despite adequate sleep hours is almost
      always traced back to the intersection of three variables: sleep quality,
      diet, and physical training. Anthony Walsh argues there is no fourth fix,
      meaning no supplement or vitamin shot substitutes for addressing these
      fundamentals directly. If sleep hours are sufficient but fatigue remains,
      the quality of that sleep or deficiencies in diet and exercise are the
      most likely culprits.
  - question: how does caffeine affect sleep and tiredness the next day?
    answer: >-
      Caffeine has a half-life of five to seven hours, meaning a 500mg coffee
      consumed in the afternoon still leaves roughly 125mg active in your system
      at 5am. That residual caffeine disrupts sleep quality even when you have
      no trouble falling asleep, leaving you tired the following day. Anthony
      Walsh recommends a firm midday cutoff for caffeine consumption to prevent
      this compounding cycle of fatigue.
  - question: what are the main causes of tiredness and low energy in adults?
    answer: >-
      According to Anthony Walsh, the root causes of persistent tiredness come
      down to three variables: poor sleep, inadequate diet, and insufficient or
      unstructured physical training. These three factors operate as continuums
      rather than boxes to tick once and forget. Improving all three
      consistently is the only reliable route to sustained energy levels.
  - question: does exercise help with tiredness or make it worse?
    answer: >-
      Regular physical training is one of the three core variables that directly
      influences chronic fatigue, and neglecting it is a common contributor to
      low energy. Anthony Walsh treats training as a continuum, meaning ongoing,
      progressive movement matters far more than occasional bursts of activity.
      Structured exercise improves sleep quality and metabolic health, both of
      which reduce day-to-day tiredness over time.
