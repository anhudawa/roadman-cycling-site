      your training week, and monitor overall stress load including work and
      family demands. If consistently tired, reduce training volume by 20-30
      percent for a week and see if energy returns.

    answer: >-
      Overtraining syndrome is rare in amateur cyclists, but non-functional

      stacking life stress on top of training stress. One to two weeks of
      reduced load usually resolves the issue.

Most cyclists blaming fatigue on training load are missing the actual problem. Anthony Walsh breaks down why the answer has nothing to do with supplements and everything to do with three variables you already know about but aren't measuring properly.

## Key Takeaways

Caffeine has a half-life of 5-7 hours. That means a double espresso at 4 p.m. still leaves around 250mg in your system at 11 p.m. Anthony's current fix is a 12 p.m. cutoff. Not because coffee is the enemy, but because that's where the math stops working against your sleep.

Eight hours in bed is not eight hours of sleep. Matt Walker's research draws a hard line between seven and eight hours, and most people counting eight hours in bed are actually getting under seven once you account for time to fall asleep and night wakings. The other thing worth checking: what you're leaving unresolved before bed. Open loops, an unanswered text, a conversation that's still going, keep part of your brain running all night. Close them before you go down.

Walsh references sleep researcher Matt Walker's book Why We Sleep for the distinction between time in bed and actual sleep duration, noting that 45 minutes to fall asleep plus two 15-minute night wakings reduces an 8-hour sleep window to under 7 hours. Walsh also draws on James Nestor's book Breath and a conversation with freediver Claire Walsh, who recorded a 7.5-minute breath hold, to support nasal breathing during sleep. His position is that persistent fatigue in cyclists is determined by the intersection of sleep, diet, and training, with no supplement replacing optimisation of those three variables.
