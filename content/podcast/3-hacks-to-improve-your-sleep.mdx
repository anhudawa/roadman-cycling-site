faq:
  - question: Does eating before bed affect sleep quality?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Eating in the 60 to 70 minutes before bed, especially sugars and
      refined carbohydrates, suppresses melatonin production and causes a
      hypoglycaemic dip mid-sleep that fragments your recovery. Anthony Walsh
      specifically calls out a late bowl of cereal as a common offender for
      cyclists. Avoiding food in that window is one of the most direct ways to
      protect sleep quality.
  - question: What is the difference between sleep quantity and sleep quality?
    answer: >-
      Sleep quantity is simply the number of hours you spend in bed, while sleep
      quality refers to how undisturbed and restorative that sleep actually is.
      Anthony Walsh draws on Matthew Walker's research to argue that quality
      matters more than quantity, because training adaptations only materialise
      during deep, uninterrupted sleep supported by adequate melatonin levels.
  - question: How does melatonin affect cycling recovery?
    answer: >-
      Melatonin regulates the onset and depth of sleep, which is when the body
      processes training stress and builds fitness. Suppressing melatonin
      through late-night eating or other habits reduces sleep depth and
      interrupts the recovery process. Anthony Walsh argues that protecting
      melatonin production is central to turning training load into actual
      adaptation.
  - question: Why is sleep the most important part of recovery for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      Sleep is the primary window in which the body repairs muscle tissue,
      consolidates motor patterns, and responds to training stimulus. Anthony
      Walsh frames sleep not as a passive activity but as the cornerstone of the
      entire recovery process. Without consistent, high-quality sleep, the
      benefits of even well-structured training are significantly diminished.
