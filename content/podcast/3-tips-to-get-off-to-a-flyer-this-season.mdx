answerCapsule: >-
  Host Anthony Walsh outlines three preparation errors that derail cyclists at
  the start of the road season, which typically runs March through September.
  First, pace motivation so it peaks at target events rather than burning out in
  March. Second, rehearse race-day fuelling in training, building tolerance to
  roughly 60 grams of carbohydrate per hour from gels and bars to avoid
  gastrointestinal issues mid-event. Third, transition from winter bike to race
  bike gradually, since differing geometries combined with rising training
  intensity are a common injury trigger.

faq:
  - question: How do I avoid burning out before my target cycling races?
    answer: >-
      Pace your motivation so it peaks at your key target events rather than
      letting it spike in the opening weeks of the season. Anthony Walsh
      recommends treating early-season enthusiasm as a resource to be managed,
      not spent all at once, so you arrive at your most important races with
      form and drive intact rather than fatigued and flat.
  - question: How much carbohydrate should I take on during a road race?
    answer: >-
      Aim to build tolerance to roughly 60 grams of carbohydrate per hour from
      race-day products such as gels and bars. Practise this intake during
      training rides rather than leaving it until race day, as introducing
      unfamiliar fuelling under race intensity is a common cause of
      gastrointestinal problems.
  - question: >-
      Can switching to my race bike cause injury at the start of the cycling
      season?
    answer: >-
      Yes, moving straight from a winter bike to a race bike is a frequent
      injury trigger because the two bikes often have different geometries.
      Anthony Walsh advises making the transition gradually, particularly as
      training intensity is already rising at this point in the season, which
      increases stress on muscles and joints adapting to a new position.
  - question: How do I prepare for the road cycling season starting in March?
    answer: >-
      Start rehearsing race-day habits now, well before March, rather than
      waiting until the season begins. That means practising your fuelling
      strategy, gradually introducing your race bike, and managing your training
      motivation so you build into the season rather than peaking too early and
      fading before your target events arrive.
