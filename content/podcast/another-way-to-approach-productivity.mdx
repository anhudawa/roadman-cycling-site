faq:
  - question: What is productive rest and how does it improve productivity?
    answer: >-
      Productive rest means scheduling deliberate recovery periods into your
      day, week, and month before burnout forces you to stop. Anthony Walsh
      argues it should be treated as a planned input to your output, not a
      reward you earn after exhausting yourself. Building rest in proactively
      preserves long-term capacity rather than repeatedly draining and crashing.
  - question: Why do to-do lists and calendars make productivity worse over time?
    answer: >-
      Calendar and task-driven systems prioritise output without accounting for
      recovery, so they gradually erode the capacity needed to sustain that
      output. Anthony Walsh compares the body to a battery that degrades when
      constantly depleted and propped up with caffeine rather than genuinely
      recharged. Without fixed stop times and scheduled rest, the system
      produces diminishing returns.
  - question: How many days off per week do you need to recover properly?
    answer: >-
      Two consecutive days off are more restorative than two separate single
      days, because the first day is largely spent decompressing from work mode.
      Anthony Walsh recommends treating this block as non-negotiable rather than
      a gap that fills with tasks when schedules get busy. Consistency in when
      you stop working is as important as consistency in when you start.
  - question: How often should you take multi-day breaks to avoid burnout?
    answer: >-
      Multi-day breaks should be pre-booked into your calendar at regular
      intervals rather than taken reactively once exhaustion sets in. Anthony
      Walsh frames these as scheduled maintenance for your output capacity, not
      a holiday earned after hitting breaking point. Treating them as fixed
      appointments prevents the creep of overwork that makes sustained
      productivity impossible.
