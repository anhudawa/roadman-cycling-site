faq:
  - question: What are the Velominati rules for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      The Velominati rules are an unofficial code of conduct followed by serious
      cyclists, covering everything from equipment standards to riding etiquette
      and café culture. They originated as an underground set of guidelines and
      have become widely referenced in the cycling community. Anthony Walsh
      covers a selection of them across a three-part series on his podcast.
  - question: What does Velominati Rule 45 say about stem height?
    answer: >-
      Rule 45 states that you must slam the stem, meaning the handlebar should
      be as low as possible with a maximum stack height of 2cm above the
      headset. Spacers above this limit are considered a violation of the rules.
      The principle is that a slammed stem signals commitment to both
      performance and aesthetics.
  - question: Can you put cycling sunglasses in your jersey pocket?
    answer: >-
      According to Velominati Rule 35, stashing glasses in a jersey pocket is
      not acceptable because they will scratch against other items. Eyewear
      should be worn at all times during a ride, not pocketed. The rule also
      states that riding without eyewear at all is a violation in its own right.
  - question: What coffee should you drink after a bike ride?
    answer: >-
      Velominati Rule 56 specifies that post-ride coffee must be an espresso or
      macchiato, ordered from an independent café wherever possible. Milky
      drinks like lattes or flat whites are not in keeping with the spirit of
      the rule. The café stop is treated as an integral part of the cycling
      ritual, not an afterthought.
  - question: How often should you wash your cycling kit?
    answer: >-
      Velominati Rule 53 requires that kit is freshly laundered before every
      ride, with no worn-through chamois permitted under any circumstances.
      Turning up to a ride in unwashed or degraded kit is considered a serious
      breach of cycling etiquette. The condition of your chamois in particular
      is treated as a direct reflection of your standards as a cyclist.
