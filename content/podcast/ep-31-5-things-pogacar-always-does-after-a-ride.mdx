The finish line starts a sequence of practical decisions, but a WorldTour ritual is not automatically a scientifically proven protocol. This episode uses the routine associated with Tadej Pogačar to distinguish what is visible, what is inferred and what controlled research can support.

## Key Takeaways

An active cool-down is useful for an immediate transition and lowers blood lactate faster than passive rest. That does not make lactate a waste product that must be flushed, nor does it prove that a fixed 20-minute spin speeds tissue repair or next-day performance. If a cool-down feels good, keep it easy and avoid allowing optional extra work to delay feeding, warmth or travel.

Refuelling urgency depends on the next session. When another demanding effort follows in less than four hours, the ISSN position stand recommends aggressive carbohydrate intake at about 1.2 g/kg/hour, or about 0.8 g/kg/hour carbohydrate plus 0.2-0.4 g/kg/hour protein. That is a specific rapid-turnaround case. It does not establish a universal 15-minute deadline, and the arithmetic may resemble 4:1 without making one commercial drink ratio mandatory for every ride.

Tart cherry is plausible but optional. A 2026 systematic review in trained athletes reported possible benefits for selected strength and inflammatory outcomes after exercise-induced muscle damage, but effects were heterogeneous, some sensitivity analyses depended on individual studies, and certainty ranged from very low to moderate. The pooled analysis did not find a significant effect on soreness. Product, dose, timing and the athlete population matter.

Layers after a cold or wet finish are a sensible comfort and temperature-management choice. A repeatable sequence may also reduce decision load after a stressful race. We have not found evidence that the ritual itself protects immunity or that it creates a measurable parasympathetic switch. Those are coaching interpretations, not established outcomes of the four steps.

Stretch if it helps you relax or because flexibility is a separate goal, but do not promise it as a recovery accelerator. Recent meta-analyses found no significant benefit from post-exercise stretching alone for soreness, strength, performance or flexibility compared with no stretching, and the earlier evidence was scarce and very low confidence.

For an amateur rider, prioritise the basics: stop safely, get dry or warm when conditions demand it, start replacing fluid and food in proportion to the session, and plan around the time until the next hard ride.
