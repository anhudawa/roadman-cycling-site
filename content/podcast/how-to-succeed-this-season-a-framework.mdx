faq:
  - question: Why do cyclists on the same training plan get different results?
    answer: >-
      Training load alone does not determine performance outcomes. Coach Anthony
      Walsh argues that factors such as nutrition timing, psychological
      resilience, strength work, and stress management all interact with the
      training stimulus, and neglecting any one of them significantly reduces
      the gains produced by the others.
  - question: What is reverse periodisation in cycling?
    answer: >-
      Reverse periodisation flips the traditional training calendar by building
      intensity and race-specific work earlier in the season before adding
      volume. Coach Anthony Walsh includes it as a core component of his Roadman
      Blueprint because it aligns training stress more precisely with the
      demands of target events.
  - question: How important is nutrition for amateur cycling performance?
    answer: >-
      Nutrition is a structured component of the Roadman Blueprint, not an
      afterthought. Walsh prescribes event-specific fuelling strategies
      alongside calibrated calorie deficits to optimise body composition,
      arguing that even well-trained cyclists leave substantial performance on
      the table through poorly timed or poorly calculated eating habits.
  - question: How do you stay motivated to train on the bike all year round?
    answer: >-
      Sustained motivation requires a deliberate sports psychology strategy
      rather than relying on willpower or enthusiasm alone. Coach Anthony Walsh
      addresses this directly in the Roadman Blueprint and covers it in detail
      in his free web class, which also examines the specific habits that
      separate WorldTour professionals from amateur riders across a full season.
