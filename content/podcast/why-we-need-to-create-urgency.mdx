Anthony Walsh runs a solo episode on urgency and why thinking about your own death is one of the more useful things you can do before a ride. It's a short one, but it's the kind of thing that sits with you.

## Key Takeaways

The death-bed exercise Walsh talks about here is worth actually trying. Sit down and ask yourself what day you'd pay a million quid to go back to. Nobody ever says the car or the watch. It's always the dog walk, the ride over the hills with the right song on, the Tuesday you can't really remember but somehow felt like everything. Walsh uses that every morning in his journaling to figure out what actually matters that day, rather than just being busy.

The morning routine he runs through — salt water, red light, three-minute cold shower, coffee, five to ten minutes journaling — none of that matters on its own. It's about where your head is before you look at the training plan. The urgency piece is what ties it together. Walsh reckons that five minutes thinking about the fact you're going to die does more for your training than another set of intervals. Hard to argue with that.
