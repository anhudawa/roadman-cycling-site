answerCapsule: >-
  Coach Anthony Walsh argues that incorrect saddle height costs cyclists 6–8% in
  power output when off by as little as 5 centimeters, and is the leading cause
  of knee injuries he sees in clients. He compares three setup methods—LeMond
  (88.3% of inseam to bottom bracket), the crank-based method (109% of inseam
  from pedal insert to saddle top), and the heel method—recommending the
  crank-based approach. When in doubt, err lower: high saddles consistently
  produce worse power and higher injury rates than low ones.

    that's too high consistently produces worse power and more knee injuries
    than one that's slightly low.

    answer: >-
      Incorrect saddle height costs you 6–8% in power output, even when the
      saddle is only 5 centimetres off the optimal position. Coach Anthony Walsh

  - question: What is the correct saddle height for cycling?
    answer: >-
      Coach Anthony Walsh recommends the crank-based method: set your saddle so
      the distance from the pedal insert to the top of the saddle is 109% of
      your inseam length. This method is more reliable than the LeMond formula
      or the heel method, both of which tend to produce less accurate results.
  - question: Is it better to have your saddle too high or too low?
    answer: >-
      A saddle that is too low is the lesser of two evils. Coach Anthony Walsh
      finds that saddles set too high consistently produce worse power output
      and higher injury rates than those set slightly too low. When uncertain,
      always err on the side of a lower position.
  - question: Can wrong saddle height cause knee pain cycling?
    answer: >-
      Yes, incorrect saddle height is the leading cause of knee injuries Coach
      Anthony Walsh sees in his clients. Both an excessively high and
      excessively low saddle place abnormal stress on the knee joint, making
      proper setup essential for long-term health and performance.
