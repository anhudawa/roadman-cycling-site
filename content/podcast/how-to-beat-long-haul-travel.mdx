  Recorded mid-transit from Bogotá, host and former professional cyclist Anthony
  Walsh outlines a practical protocol for arriving off a long-haul flight ready
  to ride. Core tactics include hydrating at roughly 500ml per hour, going light
  on electrolytes and sodium to prevent bloating, wearing blue-light blocking
  glasses, and breaking up sitting with air squats, stretching and frequent
  walks to the bathroom. Walsh also endorses Compex-style electrical muscle
  stimulation during the flight to maintain circulation, alongside a
  well-stocked carry-on with pre-downloaded entertainment to avoid relying on
  in-flight Wi-Fi.

faq:
  - question: How much water should you drink on a long haul flight?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends drinking around 500ml of water per hour
      throughout a long-haul flight. Cabin air is extremely dry and dehydration
      directly impairs muscle function and recovery, so consistent intake rather
      than drinking in large bursts is the key approach.
  - question: How do you avoid swollen legs on a long haul flight?
    answer: >-
      Walsh recommends breaking up long periods of sitting with air squats,
      stretching, and regular walks to the bathroom to keep circulation moving.
      Using electrical muscle stimulation, such as a Compex device, during the
      flight is also an effective way to maintain blood flow without leaving
      your seat.
  - question: Do compression socks and electrolytes help on long flights?
    answer: >-
      Walsh advises going light on electrolytes and sodium during the flight
      specifically to avoid bloating and water retention, which can leave legs
      feeling heavy on arrival. Keeping sodium intake low in the air is a
      practical way to reduce that sluggish feeling when you land.
  - question: How do you sleep on a long haul flight to recover faster?
    answer: >-
      Walsh wears blue-light blocking glasses to support melatonin production
      and make it easier to sleep in line with your destination time zone.
      Combining this with a well-stocked carry-on of pre-downloaded
      entertainment means you are not relying on in-flight Wi-Fi and can control
      when you switch off properly.
