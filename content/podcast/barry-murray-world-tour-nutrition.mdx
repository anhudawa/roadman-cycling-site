faq:
  - question: how many carbs should endurance athletes eat per day
    answer: >-
      Barry Murray recommends capping carbohydrates at roughly 15–20 percent of
      total daily intake for endurance athletes. This is not a zero-carb
      approach — foods like porridge, fruit, and even pizza remain on the table
      within that range. Murray argues that the sports nutrition industry has
      oversold carbohydrates and that many athletes perform better on a
      lower-carb, fat-adapted diet.
  - question: what does a WorldTour cycling nutritionist recommend for race day fuelling
    answer: >-
      Barry Murray, who has worked with WorldTour riders including Steve
      Cummings, focuses on periodised nutrition rather than relying heavily on
      carbohydrates across every training day. On the bike, fuelling strategy is
      adjusted to match intensity and duration. The goal is to train the body to
      use fat efficiently so that carbohydrate stores are preserved for
      properly high-intensity efforts.
  - question: does low carb diet work for professional cyclists
    answer: >-
      Barry Murray's work with BMC Pro Cycling and individual WorldTour riders
      shows that a low-carb approach can work at the highest level of
      professional cycling. Low-carb does not mean zero-carb, and athletes still
      consume carbohydrates strategically around hard sessions. The approach
      prioritises fat adaptation while retaining the flexibility to use
      carbohydrates when race demands require it.
  - question: how do cyclists strengthen their immune system naturally
    answer: >-
      Barry Murray points to ancestral lifestyle practices as a foundation for
      immune resilience, including sleep quality, stress management, and
      nutrient-dense whole foods. Reducing reliance on ultra-processed
      carbohydrates helps lower systemic inflammation, which supports immune
      function. These principles are particularly relevant for athletes who are
      immunocompromised through heavy training loads.
