faq:
  - question: What is Training Stress Score in cycling power meter training?
    answer: >-
      Training Stress Score (TSS) is a measure of how much work a ride demands
      from your body. Cycling coach Rick from the A1 Coaching podcast explains
      it with a simple anchor point: one hour of all-out effort equals 100 TSS
      points, making every other ride easy to contextualise against that
      baseline.
  - question: >-
      What is the difference between CTL and ATL on a Performance Management
      Chart?
    answer: >-
      Chronic Training Load (CTL) represents your overall fitness built up over
      weeks and months, while Acute Training Load (ATL) represents the fatigue
      accumulated from recent training. Rick from A1 Coaching describes CTL as
      simply fitness and ATL as simply fatigue, which makes the Performance
      Management Chart far less intimidating to interpret.
  - question: What is Training Stress Balance and how does it affect race performance?
    answer: >-
      Training Stress Balance (TSB) is the difference between your fitness and
      your fatigue, and it is essentially a measure of freshness. A positive TSB
      means you are fresh enough to perform at your best, which is why coaches
      manipulate it deliberately in the weeks before a target race.
  - question: >-
      How do cycling coaches time a peak for an important race using a power
      meter?
    answer: >-
      Coaches manage the ramp rate between Chronic Training Load, Acute Training
      Load, and Training Stress Balance to bring an athlete to peak fitness
      while reducing fatigue at the right moment. Rick from A1 Coaching uses
      this approach to explain how professional programmes, including
      preparation for events like the Tour de France, are structured around this
      relationship rather than guesswork.
