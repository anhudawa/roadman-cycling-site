faq:
  - question: What is the hardest climb in the Vuelta a España?
    answer: >-
      The Alto de l'Angliru is widely considered the hardest climb in the Vuelta
      a España. It stretches 12.5 kilometres at an average gradient of 10.3%,
      with individual pitches reaching 24%. Hugh Carthy's stage victory there
      brought it to widespread attention as one of the most brutal finishing
      climbs in professional racing.
  - question: What is the average gradient of Monte Zoncolan?
    answer: >-
      Monte Zoncolan averages approximately 15% across its eight hardest
      kilometres, making it one of the steepest sustained climbs in the Giro
      d'Italia. There is no significant relief on the upper slopes, which is why
      it consistently causes the peloton to shatter within a few kilometres of
      the base.
  - question: What climb do professional cyclists use as a test climb in Girona?
    answer: >-
      Rocacorba is the benchmark test climb for professional cyclists based in
      Girona. The climb is 13.8 kilometres long and is used routinely by pros
      living in the area to measure fitness and compare efforts. Its consistent
      gradient makes it ideal for tracking performance over a season.
  - question: What makes a cycling climb the hardest in the world?
    answer: >-
      The hardest climbs are defined by sustained double-digit gradients rather
      than length alone. A climb that averages 10% or more for several
      kilometres, with no flat sections to recover on, causes far greater
      physiological stress than a longer climb at a gentler gradient. The
      Angliru and Zoncolan are prime examples of this distinction in
      professional racing.
