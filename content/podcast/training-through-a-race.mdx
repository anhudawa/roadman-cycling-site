faq:
  - question: Should I taper before every race?
    answer: >-
      No. Only your priority races warrant a full taper. Lower-priority events
      should be treated as training stimulus, with hard sessions continuing
      right up to and including race day. Reducing load for every race
      sacrifices valuable training weeks across a long season.
  - question: How did elite cyclists train through races historically?
    answer: >-
      Eddy Merckx is the clearest example. In the week before winning the Tour
      de France by 18 minutes, he raced a criterium, competed on the track, rode
      a kermesse, and completed a 270km solo training ride. That volume was
      deliberate, not reckless, and it formed part of a structured preparation
      block.
  - question: What is racing through an event in cycling?
    answer: >-
      Training through a race means treating a lower-priority event as a hard
      training session rather than a target performance. You make no special
      adjustments to your programme beforehand, and you may even add volume
      around the race itself to accumulate training load for a bigger goal later
      in the season.
  - question: How can I add volume around a club race without extra time?
    answer: >-
      Ride to and from the event rather than driving. This banks meaningful
      kilometres before and after the race effort without requiring a separate
      training day, and it keeps your weekly volume on track when the race
      itself does not justify a taper.
  - question: Did Mathieu van der Poel ever use a race as a training ride?
    answer: >-
      Yes. At Kuurne-Brussels-Kuurne, van der Poel rode 85km solo off the front
      in what was widely interpreted as a deliberate race-simulation training
      turn rather than a genuine podium attempt. It is a clear example of a
      professional using a race calendar strategically to prepare for
      higher-priority targets.
