  food philosophy but metabolic flexibility: the body's ability to switch
  between burning carbohydrates and stored fat for fuel. He contends that

faq:
  - question: What is metabolic flexibility in diet?
    answer: >-
      Metabolic flexibility is the body's ability to switch between burning
      carbohydrates and stored fat for fuel depending on availability. Most
      people lose this ability through constant carb-heavy snacking, which keeps
      insulin elevated and blocks access to fat stores. Restoring it is what
      Anthony Walsh argues should be the true goal of any ideal diet.
  - question: How does intermittent fasting improve metabolic flexibility?
    answer: >-
      Intermittent fasting lowers insulin levels during the fasting window,
      which allows the body to begin drawing on stored fat for energy. Anthony
      Walsh recommends a 16:8 protocol, meaning 16 hours fasted and an 8-hour
      eating window. This mimics the ancestral feast-and-fast pattern that the
      human metabolic storage system was built around.
  - question: Does keto or vegan diet build metabolic flexibility?
    answer: >-
      Both ketogenic and vegan diets can build metabolic flexibility, as can a
      paleo approach. Anthony Walsh's position is that the specific food
      philosophy matters less than whether it breaks the cycle of constant
      snacking and chronically elevated blood sugar. The mechanism, not the food
      label, is what produces fat adaptation.
  - question: Why do I struggle to burn fat even when dieting?
    answer: >-
      The most common reason is that frequent carbohydrate intake keeps insulin
      elevated throughout the day, which signals the body to store fat rather
      than burn it. Anthony Walsh explains that the body cannot access fat
      stores while insulin is high, regardless of overall calorie intake.
      Introducing fasting periods is the most direct way to lower insulin and
      restore fat-burning capacity.
