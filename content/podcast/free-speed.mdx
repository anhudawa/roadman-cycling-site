faq:
  - question: What is tapering in cycling and does it actually work?
    answer: >-
      Tapering is a planned reduction in training load in the days before a
      target event, allowing the body to recover and perform at its peak.
      Anthony Walsh of the Roadman Cycling Podcast argues it is the most
      overlooked free performance gain available to amateur cyclists, requiring
      no extra money, weight loss, or additional miles.
  - question: How long should a cycling taper be before a race?
    answer: >-
      A taper of seven to fourteen days is the recommended window before a
      target event. During this period, training volume should be cut
      significantly while high-intensity work is preserved to keep the legs
      sharp without accumulating fatigue.
  - question: Should you cut intensity or volume when tapering for a cycling event?
    answer: >-
      Cut volume, not intensity. The common mistake among amateur cyclists is
      reducing hard efforts during a taper, which leaves the legs feeling flat
      on race day. Keeping intensity high whilst reducing overall load is the
      correct approach.
  - question: What workouts should I do in the last 3 days before a cycling race?
    answer: >-
      A Tabata protocol is recommended in the final three days before race day:
      20 seconds of hard effort followed by 10 seconds of rest, repeated across
      a four-minute block. This preserves neuromuscular sharpness without adding
      meaningful fatigue to the body.
