faq:
  - question: What does a physiotherapist do in a World Tour cycling team?
    answer: >-
      A World Tour physio manages rider health across the entire race calendar,
      including crash assessment, soft-tissue treatment, and daily recovery
      protocols during Grand Tours. Scott Murphy explains that during three-week
      stage races the role extends to coordinating medical decisions on the
      road, monitoring rider wellbeing, and advising team management on whether
      a rider is fit to continue. The job combines clinical expertise with
      logistics, operating inside a tightly scheduled environment where
      decisions often have to be made in minutes.
  - question: Should a cyclist remount after a crash in a race?
    answer: >-
      Not without immediate assessment, even if the rider feels able to
      continue. Scott Murphy highlights that the first person to reach a fallen
      rider is usually a mechanic with a spare bike, which creates pressure to
      remount before any medical check has taken place. Teams have an ethical
      duty to override a rider's instinct to continue when there is a genuine
      risk of serious harm, and experienced medical staff should make that call,
      not the rider themselves.
  - question: What supplements do professional cyclists use during Grand Tours?
    answer: >-
      Professional teams use carefully periodised supplement protocols that
      typically include caffeine, nitrates, beta-alanine, and carbohydrate
      products timed around stages. Scott Murphy discusses how supplement use
      inside the peloton is structured around race demands, with products
      selected for efficacy and compliance with anti-doping regulations. The
      priority is always food-first nutrition, with supplements filling specific
      gaps that diet alone cannot address across three weeks of consecutive
      racing.
  - question: How do pro cycling teams structure off-season strength and conditioning?
    answer: >-
      Off-season strength work in World Tour teams is periodised to build a
      physical foundation before returning to high-volume cycling training.
      Scott Murphy explains that riders use the winter block to address muscular
      imbalances and improve functional strength in areas like the posterior
      chain and core, which are difficult to develop on the bike. The programme
      is individualised, taking into account each rider's race calendar, injury
      history, and role within the team.
