  heavier, less reliable standard.

faq:
  - question: Why do disc brakes rub on road bikes?
    answer: >-
      Disc brake pads rub because the rotors flex and warp easily, especially
      after heat builds up on long descents. Even minor wheel removal and
      reinsertion can misalign the calliper enough to cause constant rubbing.
      Anthony Walsh argues this is a fundamental design flaw rather than a setup
      issue.
  - question: Are disc brakes worth it on road bikes?
    answer: >-
      For most road cyclists they are not worth the trade-offs. Walsh points to
      pad rub, rotor warping on mountain descents, seized pads when wheels are
      removed, and added weight as reasons the technology falls short. Chris
      Froome has publicly echoed the same criticism, suggesting the problems
      affect professionals and amateurs alike.
  - question: What are the disadvantages of disc brakes on road bikes?
    answer: >-
      The main disadvantages are persistent pad rub, rotors overheating and
      warping on long alpine descents, pads locking together when a wheel is
      removed, and greater mechanical complexity compared to rim brakes. Walsh
      uses the Alto de Letras descent as a real-world example of where disc heat
      build-up becomes a serious problem.
  - question: Do pro cyclists prefer rim brakes or disc brakes?
    answer: >-
      Opinion is divided, but Walsh highlights Chris Froome's recent public
      criticism of disc brakes as evidence that even at the highest level the
      technology is not considered ready. Rim brakes remain lighter, simpler to
      maintain, and free from the rotor-warping and pad-rub issues that disc
      systems regularly produce.
