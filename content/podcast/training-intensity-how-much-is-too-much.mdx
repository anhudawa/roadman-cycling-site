answerCapsule: >-
  Cycling coach Anthony Walsh explains why training zones, derived from
  threshold testing, form the shared vocabulary between coach and athlete that
  makes structured improvement possible. Without zones, descriptions like "I
  rode 22 km/h for 10 minutes" carry no useful information, but a prescription
  such as 60 minutes in zone two with two 15-minute blocks in zone four is
  unambiguous. The episode also addresses why fitness plateaus occur: the body
  adapts to repeated stress, so progress requires deliberately varying intensity
  rather than riding at the same effort every session.

faq:
  - question: why do cyclists need to do a threshold test
    answer: >-
      A threshold test establishes your personal training zones, which coaching
      expert Anthony Walsh describes as the shared vocabulary between coach and
      athlete. Without zones, ride data like speed or perceived effort is
      meaningless to a coach trying to prescribe structured work. Threshold
      testing turns vague descriptions into precise, repeatable benchmarks.
  - question: what are cycling training zones and how do they work
    answer: >-
      Training zones divide your effort into numbered bands based on a
      percentage of your threshold power or heart rate. Anthony Walsh explains
      that once zones are established, a coach can prescribe sessions precisely,
      such as 60 minutes in zone two with two 15-minute blocks in zone four,
      leaving no ambiguity about the intended intensity. Each zone targets a
      different physiological adaptation, so mixing them across a programme
      produces well-rounded fitness gains.
  - question: why have I stopped getting faster despite cycling regularly
    answer: >-
      Fitness plateaus happen because the body adapts to repeated stress and
      stops responding once that stress becomes familiar. Anthony Walsh points
      out that riding at the same effort in every session is the most common
      cause of stalled progress. Deliberately varying intensity across your
      weekly programme forces new adaptations and restarts improvement.
  - question: how do coach and athlete communicate about training intensity
    answer: >-
      Training zones provide the common language that makes coach-to-athlete
      communication precise and actionable. Anthony Walsh notes that a rider
      saying they held 22 km/h for 10 minutes tells a coach nothing useful,
      whereas a zone-based prescription is unambiguous for both parties.
      Threshold testing is the foundation of that shared vocabulary, which is
      why Walsh argues it is essential even before structured training begins.
