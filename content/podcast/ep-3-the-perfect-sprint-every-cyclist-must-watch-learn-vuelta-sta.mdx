  Roadman Cycling Podcast breaks down Stage 3 of the 2024 Vuelta a España, where
  Jasper Philipsen-rival Wout van Aert took his first win since February with
  what the hosts argue is the tactically perfect sprint of the season. The
  analysis details how van Aert exploited a Carapaz attack that drained
  Alpecin's lead-out, jumped early at 250 metres, and angled to the barriers to
  force Kaden Groves to brake and switch sides on an uphill finish. The episode
  also covers transfer news including Julian Alaphilippe and Mark Cavendish to
  Tudor Pro Cycling.
keyTakeaways:
  - >-
    Van Aert's win shows that jumping early at 250m and angling to the barriers
    can neutralise a faster sprinter — study the positioning, not just the
    power.
  - >-
    If you're the slower sprinter, force your rival to choose a line early;
    making them brake and relaunch on an uphill finish effectively ends their
    race.
  - >-
    On wide roads, position is never locked in — teams must keep lifting the
    pace just to hold their place, which is why average speeds stay insane into
    the final kilometres.
  - >-
    A late flyer at 1.7km out can work in amateur racing because no one wants to
    sacrifice their sprint lead-out for a teammate — knowing when to attack
    matters as much as fitness.
  - >-
    Cavendish's team spent weeks at altitude just to survive the time cuts — if
    a sprinter at that level needs that much climbing prep, revisit how much
    climbing fitness you're quietly neglecting.
