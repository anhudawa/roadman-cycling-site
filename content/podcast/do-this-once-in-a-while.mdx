faq:
  - question: Should I leave my Garmin at home sometimes when cycling?
    answer: >-
      Yes, periodically riding without a GPS computer delivers mental recovery
      benefits that structured, data-driven training cannot replicate. Cycling
      coach Anthony Walsh recommends doing this occasionally to use the bike as
      a meditative tool rather than a performance instrument. The absence of
      metrics removes the pressure to hit numbers and allows your mind to
      decompress properly.
  - question: What are the mental health benefits of unstructured cycling rides?
    answer: >-
      Unstructured rides reduce mental fatigue, restore clarity, and help
      resolve decisions you have been stuck on. Anthony Walsh describes
      returning from a five-hour ride without data targets feeling mentally
      clear and more productive, despite starting the ride feeling frazzled and
      reluctant. The lack of structure shifts the focus from performance to
      presence, which is where the mental recovery actually happens.
  - question: How do you motivate yourself to ride when you don't feel like it?
    answer: >-
      Remove all structure and expectations from the ride entirely. Walsh found
      that going out with a training partner and no GPS computer transformed a
      reluctant start into a five-hour ride that cleared his head completely.
      Framing the ride as a mental reset rather than a training session lowers
      the psychological barrier to getting out the door.
  - question: What is the Dalai Lama principle applied to cycling training?
    answer: >-
      The principle states that on your busiest, most stressful days you should
      meditate for longer, not shorter. Applied to cycling, Walsh uses this to
      argue that when you feel most reluctant or overwhelmed, an unstructured
      ride without data is exactly what you need rather than something to skip.
      It reframes the ride as recovery for the mind rather than a demand on your
      time.
