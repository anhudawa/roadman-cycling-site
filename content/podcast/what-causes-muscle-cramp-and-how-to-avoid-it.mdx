faq:
  - question: What actually causes muscle cramps when cycling?
    answer: >-
      Muscle cramps are primarily caused by a malfunction in the central nervous
      system's ability to regulate muscle tension, not simply by dehydration or
      electrolyte loss. The nervous system loses control of the signals telling
      the muscle when to contract and relax, which triggers the cramp. While
      hydration is often blamed, the research supporting electrolyte deficits as
      the main cause is limited.
  - question: How do I stop getting cramps on long bike rides?
    answer: >-
      The most reliable prevention is graded adaptation, which means increasing
      ride distance, terrain difficulty, and intensity in small, controlled
      steps. Coach Anthony Walsh recommends avoiding large jumps in training
      load or equipment changes so the nervous system has time to adapt.
      Consistent, progressive overload across multiple rides is far more
      effective than any mid-ride supplement strategy.
  - question: Can a bad bike fit cause muscle cramps cycling?
    answer: >-
      Yes, sudden or large bike-fit changes can overload the nervous system and
      trigger cramps. Anthony Walsh uses the example of a 2cm saddle height
      adjustment, recommending it be broken into four increments of 0.5cm across
      separate rides rather than made in one go. Making significant fit changes
      immediately before a target event is a common and avoidable mistake.
  - question: Do electrolytes actually prevent cycling cramps?
    answer: >-
      The evidence linking electrolyte deficits directly to muscle cramps is
      weak, and relying solely on electrolyte supplements is unlikely to solve a
      recurring cramp problem. The central nervous system's role in muscle
      tension signalling is a more accurate explanation for why cramps occur.
      Focusing on gradual training adaptation will address the root cause far
      more effectively than adjusting your nutrition strategy alone.
