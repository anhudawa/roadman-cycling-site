faq:
  - question: What tools should I carry on a road bike ride?
    answer: >-
      At minimum, carry a spare inner tube, a pump or CO2 inflator, tyre levers,
      and a multi-tool that includes a chain breaker. Anthony Walsh learned this
      lesson first-hand after breaking a chain in the Wicklow Mountains with no
      way to fix it. These four items cover the vast majority of roadside
      mechanical problems.
  - question: Why do cyclists fall over at traffic lights when clipped in?
    answer: >-
      The most common cause is shifting your weight onto the foot that is still
      clipped in as you slow to a stop, leaving you unable to unclip in time.
      Walsh identifies this as one of the most frequent mistakes he sees among
      road cyclists. To avoid it, get into the habit of unclipping the same foot
      every time you approach a stop, well before you actually come to a halt.
  - question: What tyre pressure should I use for road cycling?
    answer: >-
      The correct pressure depends on your tyre width, your body weight, and the
      road surface you are riding on. Running too much pressure reduces grip and
      makes punctures more likely on rough roads, while too little causes
      handling problems. Walsh recommends researching the recommended range for
      your specific tyre and adjusting based on conditions rather than simply
      inflating to the maximum stated on the sidewall.
  - question: What are the most common mistakes beginner cyclists make?
    answer: >-
      According to former professional cyclist Anthony Walsh, the most prominent
      mistakes include riding without basic repair tools, falling victim to the
      unclip crash at stops, and making poor tyre choice and pressure decisions.
      Each of these errors is avoidable with a small amount of preparation and
      habit-building before you head out on the road.
