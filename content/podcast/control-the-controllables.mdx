  - question: What does "control the controllables" mean in sport?
    answer: >-
      "Control the controllables" is a principle popularised by cycling
      performance director Dave Brailsford, meaning you focus your energy only
      on factors within your direct influence and ignore everything else.
      Anthony Walsh applies this to everyday life, arguing that most stress
      comes from fixating on things you cannot change, particularly the constant
      stream of negative content on social media.
  - question: How do I stop being overwhelmed by negative news and social media?
    answer: >-
      Walsh recommends treating social media as noise rather than signal and
      deliberately filtering out content that produces no actionable outcome.
      The core habit is redirecting that mental energy toward four daily inputs
      you can control: steps, hydration, meditation, and sleep.
  - question: What are simple daily habits to improve mental health and wellbeing?
    answer: >-
      Walsh identifies four concrete daily controllables: walking roughly 20,000
      steps, drinking two to three litres of water with sea salt added,
      meditating for 15 minutes using a guided app, and protecting eight hours
      of sleep. These habits are presented as the foundation of internal
      happiness rather than waiting for external circumstances to improve.
  - question: Does drinking water with sea salt actually do anything?
    answer: >-
      Adding sea salt to water improves electrolyte balance, supporting
      hydration at a cellular level rather than just increasing fluid intake.
      Walsh recommends dosing two to three litres of water with sea salt daily
      as one of the four non-negotiable controllables in his routine.
