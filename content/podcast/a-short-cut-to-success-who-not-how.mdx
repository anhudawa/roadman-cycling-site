  Host Anthony Walsh, a cycling coach and former pro, argues that the fastest
  route past any training plateau is replacing the question "how do I solve
  this?" with "who already knows the answer?" He recounts paying a
  cycling-specific strength coach fifty dollars overnight for a core program
  that outperformed months of his own research. The principle, drawn from his
  own progression in the sport, applies beyond cycling to nutrition, equipment
  choices, and life decisions: outsource expertise rather than absorb it, and
  progress compounds instead of stalling during self-study.

  - question: How do I break through a cycling training plateau faster?
    answer: >-
      The fastest way through a plateau is to find someone who has already
      solved the problem and pay for their expertise directly. Anthony Walsh
      argues that asking "who knows the answer?" instead of "how do I figure
      this out?" removes months of unproductive self-study and lets you act on
      proven solutions immediately.
  - question: Is hiring a cycling coach worth the money?
    answer: >-
      Yes, especially when you are stuck on a specific problem like strength,
      nutrition, or pacing. Anthony Walsh paid fifty dollars for an overnight
      core programme from a cycling-specific strength coach and got better
      results than months of his own research had produced. The cost of
      expertise is almost always lower than the cost of trial and error.
  - question: Who not how productivity principle explained
    answer: >-
      The "who not how" principle means outsourcing a problem to a specialist
      rather than spending time learning to solve it yourself. Anthony Walsh
      applies this across cycling coaching, nutrition, and equipment decisions,
      finding that progress compounds when you act on existing expertise instead
      of stalling during self-study.
  - question: How do amateur cyclists use their training time more efficiently?
    answer: >-
      Identify the specific area where progress has stalled, then find a
      specialist in that area rather than researching it independently. Walsh
      recommends this approach for everything from core strength programmes to
      bike fit, because a targeted expert delivers a tested solution far quicker
      than general online research can.
