Tyler Hamilton turned pro in 1995 and spent three years doing what everyone else did: long, slow distance, counting hours, not intensity. Then he picked up an SRM in 1998 and his career changed. On this episode of the Roadman Cycling podcast, Hamilton walks through what that shift actually looked like and what it means for how he coaches athletes now.

## Key Takeaways

Hamilton's biggest observation about the weekend warriors he coaches now is that they ride too hard on their easy days and then can't hit the numbers when the hard intervals come. He says when it's time to do the work, his best clients are only at 80% because they've been riding in zone three all week. The riders who follow the prescription to the letter and send the power file back, those are the ones who improve. He's been saying this for years and it matches what every coach with real data behind them has found.

On fasted riding, Hamilton says he'll use it depending on the athlete's goals, weight situation and current fitness. He did three hours fasted himself the morning of this recording, fuelled for the last two hours. Hard, he said. Not something you start with. On bike handling, he's clear that there's no number for it but it accounts for a massive chunk of what separates Sagan from a strong amateur. His advice: go find a grass field, work on picking things up without unclipping, tap wheels with a training partner, get uncomfortable on the bike. Do it now, not when you're twelve.
