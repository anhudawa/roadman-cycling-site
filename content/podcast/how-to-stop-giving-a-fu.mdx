  - question: How do I stop caring what people think of me?
    answer: >-
      Start by recognising that the urge to fit in is an evolutionary survival
      mechanism, not a reliable guide to how you should live. Anthony Walsh
      argues that redirecting the mental energy spent managing others'
      perceptions toward chosen pursuits is the most practical first step. The
      discomfort fades as the rewards of acting on your own terms accumulate.
  - question: Why am I scared to start cycling because of what people will think?
    answer: >-
      Fear of judgement is one of the most common hidden barriers stopping
      people from taking up cycling. Anthony Walsh addresses this directly,
      noting that the perceived social risk of looking like a beginner is far
      outweighed by the fitness and community gains that follow. Most
      experienced cyclists are far too focused on their own riding to judge
      newcomers.
  - question: How does worrying about other people's opinions hold you back in life?
    answer: >-
      Constantly managing how others perceive you consumes mental bandwidth that
      could go toward building skills, fitness, and meaningful relationships.
      Anthony Walsh draws on his own shift away from a conventional legal career
      to illustrate how the instinct to conform enforces mediocrity rather than
      protecting you from real harm. The cost is not one dramatic missed
      opportunity but a slow accumulation of choices made for the wrong reasons.
  - question: How do I stop seeking validation from others and build confidence?
    answer: >-
      Confidence built on others' approval is unstable because it depends on
      factors outside your control. A more reliable approach is to commit to
      pursuits chosen on your own terms and let the results speak for themselves
      over time. Anthony Walsh recommends treating the freed-up mental energy as
      a practical resource to invest in fitness, community, and work that
      actually matters to you.
