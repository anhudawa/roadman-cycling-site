faq:
  - question: Why do I feel so tired and groggy in the morning even after enough sleep?
    answer: >-
      Morning grogginess, sometimes called sleep inertia, occurs because the
      body needs physical movement, natural light, and proper hydration to
      complete the transition from sleep to full wakefulness. Lying still in dim
      indoor light after waking prolongs the groggy feeling significantly.
      Addressing all three factors immediately on rising is the most effective
      way to clear it quickly.
  - question: How do I stop feeling tired in the morning without a complicated routine?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends three actions taken immediately after waking:
      move your body straight away (he uses a mini trampoline), get exposure to
      circadian-effective light within minutes of rising, and drink 750ml to one
      litre of water with around half a teaspoon of Himalayan sea salt. These
      three steps require no early alarm or lengthy morning ritual and are
      backed by research on how the body regulates alertness.
  - question: Does drinking water in the morning help with tiredness?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The body loses fluid overnight and that deficit contributes directly
      to low energy and poor concentration after waking. Anthony Walsh
      recommends drinking 750ml to one litre of water with roughly half a
      teaspoon of Himalayan sea salt to aid absorption and restore electrolyte
      balance quickly.
  - question: Does morning sunlight actually help you wake up and feel more alert?
    answer: >-
      Direct sunlight exposes the eyes and skin to specific light wavelengths
      that signal the circadian system to suppress melatonin and boost
      alertness. Standard indoor lighting and screens do not deliver the
      required wavelengths, so they offer little benefit. Anthony Walsh suggests
      going outside within minutes of waking or, on overcast days, using a
      red-light device such as a Joovv unit as a practical alternative.
