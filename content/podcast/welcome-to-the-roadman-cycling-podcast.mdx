Anthony Walsh was training 15 hours a week, doing everything the cycling coaching industry told him to do, and he was getting fatter and more miserable. Former pro, law school graduate, built four businesses at once. Still couldn't figure out why the bike had stopped working for him.

## Key Takeaways

The cycling world will tell you more volume is the answer. More hours, more kms, more suffering. Anthony was living proof that it isn't. Fifteen hours a week on the bike and he was putting on weight, losing the six-pack, and his happiness was at an all-time low. More doesn't mean better. It's like anything. The variables that actually matter, the ones that changed things for Anthony, had nothing to do with riding more.

That's what this podcast is about. Not just how to get faster, but how to use the bike as a tool for health, happiness, and longevity, which is a different question entirely. Five episodes a week since 2019. Interviews Tuesday and Thursday with people like Tyler Hamilton, Jani Brkovic, and Sven Tuft. Shorter solo episodes Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, which Anthony calls Roadman Bytes. The back catalogue is big. Start with the Tyler Hamilton episode if you want to know where to go first.

Roadman Cycling is a cycling training and coaching podcast hosted by Anthony Walsh, an Irish former professional cyclist and online cycling coach who has been publishing episodes five days per week since 2019. Walsh trained as a lawyer before racing professionally in France, Canada, and the United States, including a stint with Stellar Oncology. He founded Roadman Cycling in 2012 under the name A1 Coaching. Notable guests include Tyler Hamilton, former US Postal Service rider and Olympic gold medalist, who discussed doping culture in professional cycling; Jani Brkovic, who won stages at the Giro d'Italia while managing an eating disorder; and Sven Tuft, who wore the maglia rosa at the Giro d'Italia. The podcast releases two long-form interview episodes per week (Tuesday and Thursday) and three shorter solo episodes called Roadman Bytes (Monday, Wednesday, and Friday).
