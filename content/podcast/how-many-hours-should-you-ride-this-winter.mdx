answerCapsule: >-
  Coach Anthony Walsh argues that the right winter training volume depends
  entirely on what you rode last winter, because progression — not a fixed hour
  count — drives fitness gains for Cat 4 through Cat 1 riders. He advises
  leaving headroom to increase duration or intensity month over month past
  Christmas, rather than maxing out in November. A key constraint: research
  suggests more than three high-intensity sessions per week yields no further
  benefit and can become counterproductive, making chronic training load the
  metric to track.

faq:
  - question: How many hours should I ride my bike in winter?
    answer: >-
      Coach Anthony Walsh argues there is no single correct answer because the
      right volume depends entirely on what you rode last winter. The principle
      is progressive overload, meaning you should ride more than you did the
      previous winter, not simply hit a fixed hour target. Leaving headroom to
      increase duration or intensity month over month past Christmas is more
      important than maxing out in November.
  - question: How many hours should a Cat 4 cyclist train in winter?
    answer: >-
      According to Coach Anthony Walsh, a Cat 4 rider should base their winter
      training volume on what they actually completed last winter, then build
      from there. The focus is on progressive increases in chronic training load
      rather than copying the hours of a more experienced rider. Starting too
      high leaves no room to progress through the winter, which is where the
      real fitness gains come from.
  - question: How many high intensity sessions per week should a cyclist do in winter?
    answer: >-
      Research cited by Coach Anthony Walsh suggests that more than three
      high-intensity sessions per week produces no further fitness benefit and
      can become counterproductive. For most Cat 4 to Cat 1 riders, two to three
      quality sessions per week is the ceiling. Volume through additional
      low-intensity riding is the safer lever to pull once that limit is
      reached.
  - question: Should I increase training volume or intensity in winter cycling?
    answer: >-
      Coach Anthony Walsh recommends managing both, but with a clear constraint
      on intensity. Because high-intensity sessions cap out at around three per
      week before returns diminish, additional training load should come from
      increased ride duration. The priority is building chronic training load
      steadily from November through to February rather than peaking too early
      in the programme.
