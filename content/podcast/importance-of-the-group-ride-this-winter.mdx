faq:
  - question: Why is the group ride important in winter training?
    answer: >-
      The winter group ride develops skills that structured training platforms
      like TrainerRoad and Strava cannot measure or replicate: cornering lines,
      wheel positioning, in-group feeding, and out-of-saddle technique. Anthony
      Walsh argues these skills form the foundation of race-ready cycling and
      are best learnt by riding consistently with others through the winter
      months.
  - question: How do you fit a group ride into a structured training plan?
    answer: >-
      Reserve one day per week specifically for a low-intensity group ride and
      treat it as non-negotiable within your programme. It does not replace
      structured intervals but sits alongside them, covering the technical and
      tactical development that periodised plans leave out.
  - question: >-
      Should experienced cyclists stay in their local club or join a separate
      training team?
    answer: >-
      Experienced A1 riders should stay in their home clubs and mentor newer
      riders rather than forming insular pop-up groups. Walsh makes the case
      that the presence of stronger riders in a club environment accelerates the
      development of less experienced cyclists and keeps the group ride valuable
      for everyone involved.
  - question: What skills can you only learn on a group ride?
    answer: >-
      Cornering as part of a bunch, changing a jacket at speed, eating and
      drinking without losing position, and reading wheel spacing in varying
      conditions are all skills that only come from repeated group riding. No
      indoor training platform or power metre can replicate the unpredictable
      dynamics of riding in a real group on open roads.
