Tyler Hamilton started doping in the spring of 1997. A US Postal team doctor walked into his room after a week-long race in southern Spain and offered him a red testosterone pill. He said it was time to start. Hamilton rode eight Tours de France after that, won Olympic gold in the Athens time trial, and spent years waking up at 3am running committee meetings in his own head about how it was all going to fall apart.

## Key Takeaways

Hamilton sat in front of a grand jury for seven hours and told the whole truth. He described walking out of that courtroom as losing a hundred-kilogram backpack. Everything before that, the denials, the 2004 Tour of Spain positive for a second blood type, the years of covering it up, he says he wishes he had just come clean at the first positive and taken the suspension. He didn't, and it dragged on for years. The federal investigation is what finally forced his hand.

What comes through in this conversation is something his high school coach told him back in 1987 or 1988, when heart rate monitors were just arriving on the scene. The coach said: don't forget to listen to your body. Hamilton thinks that matters more now, not less, with TSS and CTL and ramp rate all telling you things your legs might contradict. He started using an SRM with the US national team in 1994, was on it full-time by 1998. The numbers were always there. The problem was treating them as the whole picture.

Tyler Hamilton is a former professional cyclist who competed in eight Tour de France races and won the Olympic gold medal in the individual time trial at the 2004 Athens Games. He began doping in spring 1997 when a US Postal Service team doctor offered him a testosterone pill after a stage race in southern Spain. Hamilton tested positive at the 2004 Vuelta a España when a second blood type was detected in his blood, consistent with autologous blood transfusion. He subsequently denied the charge before eventually testifying to a federal grand jury for seven hours, which he has described as the turning point in his recovery. Hamilton's autobiography, co-written with journalist Daniel Coyle, details the systematic doping culture at US Postal. The 2004 Olympic gold medal was returned following the broader federal investigation into the team. Italian coach Luigi Cucchini is credited by Hamilton as the originator of the 40-20 VO2max interval protocol widely used in professional cycling training.
