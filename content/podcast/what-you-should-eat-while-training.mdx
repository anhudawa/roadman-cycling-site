  Host Anthony Walsh, founder of the Roadman Cycling podcast, outlines a
  practical fueling strategy for rides longer than 90 minutes, the threshold
  beyond which muscle glycogen stores run out. He recommends eating small
  amounts of carbohydrate every 30 minutes rather than larger doses hourly,
  citing Sean Kelly's rule that if you're hungry, it's already too late. Walsh

  wraps and pastries cut into bite-sized portions, then shifting to cereal bars
  and sports nutrition only late in hard sessions or races.

faq:
  - question: What should I eat while cycling training?
    answer: >-
      Prioritise real food such as wraps, pastries, and cereal bars over
      commercial gels and bars, particularly in the early and middle portions of
      a ride. Anthony Walsh recommends saving sports nutrition products for the
      final stages of hard sessions or races, when convenience matters more than
      food quality.
  - question: How often should you eat on a bike ride?
    answer: >-
      Eat small amounts of carbohydrate every 30 minutes rather than larger
      portions every hour. Anthony Walsh cites Sean Kelly's rule that if you
      feel hungry, it is already too late, meaning you need to stay ahead of
      your energy needs rather than reacting to hunger.
  - question: Do you need to eat on rides under 90 minutes?
    answer: >-
      For rides shorter than 90 minutes, muscle glycogen stores are generally
      sufficient to sustain the effort without eating. Once a session exceeds 90
      minutes, those stores run low and regular carbohydrate intake becomes
      essential to maintain performance and support recovery.
  - question: Are energy gels better than real food for cycling?
    answer: >-
      Real food is the better choice for most of a ride, particularly wraps and
      pastries cut into bite-sized portions that are easy to eat on the bike.
      Anthony Walsh recommends gels and commercial bars only late in hard
      sessions or races, when practicality outweighs the benefits of whole food.
