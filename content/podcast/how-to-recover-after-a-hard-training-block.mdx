answerCapsule: >-
  In this episode of the Roadman Cycling Podcast, host Anthony Walsh draws on
  his Girona training camp to explain how to recover from a heavy training
  block. The central principle is supercompensation: training itself does not
  build fitness, it creates the potential for adaptation that is only realised
  during recovery. Walsh outlines a practical post-camp week structured around
  complete rest days, active recovery rides, eight hours of sleep, and
  hydration, with intensity reintroduced only after five to six days to avoid
  the common post-camp illness cycle.
keyTakeaways:
  - >-
    Training doesn't make you fitter — recovery does; protect the week after any
    hard block as fiercely as the block itself.
  - >-
    Expect your body to hold off illness during a hard camp, then crash
    afterwards if you neglect sleep, hydration, and easy days.
  - >-
    Keep the first five to six days post-block completely free of intensity —
    mix full rest days with easy spins, not intervals.
  - >-
    Hit at least eight hours of sleep and three litres of water daily the week
    after a big training load.
  - >-
    Swap processed foods for single-ingredient ones post-camp — less digestive
    stress means more resources left for actual recovery.

faq:
  - question: How long should you rest after a hard cycling training block?
    answer: >-
      Plan for five to six days of genuine recovery before reintroducing any
      intensity. This window includes at least one or two complete rest days
      followed by easy active recovery rides, allowing the supercompensation
      process to complete and actual fitness gains to be realised.
  - question: What is supercompensation in cycling training?
    answer: >-
      Supercompensation is the process by which the body adapts to training
      stress and rebuilds to a higher level of fitness than before. The hard
      training block itself does not make you stronger; it creates the potential
      for adaptation that is only fulfilled during the recovery phase that
      follows.
  - question: How do you avoid getting sick after a cycling training camp?
    answer: >-
      The most common cause of post-camp illness is returning to normal training
      load too quickly before the immune system has recovered. Anthony Walsh
      recommends prioritising eight hours of sleep per night, staying well
      hydrated, and keeping rides easy for the first five to six days after the
      camp ends.
  - question: What should an active recovery ride look like after a hard training block?
    answer: >-
      An active recovery ride should be easy, well below any threshold
      effort, and focused purely on flushing the legs rather than accumulating
      fitness. The goal is to promote circulation and aid muscle repair without
      adding further fatigue to an already stressed system.
