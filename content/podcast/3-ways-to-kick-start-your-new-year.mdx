faq:
  - question: How do I start cycling training in January?
    answer: >-
      Coach Anthony Walsh recommends three concrete actions: clear your kitchen
      of processed foods, alcohol, and sugar to remove daily temptation; book a
      target event and announce it publicly so financial and social stakes hold
      you accountable; and hire a coach to avoid the trial-and-error mistakes
      that cost self-taught riders entire seasons.
  - question: Should I hire a cycling coach at the start of the new year?
    answer: >-
      Yes, and Coach Anthony Walsh argues it is the highest-impact investment
      you can make in January. A coach bypasses the trial-and-error cycle that
      consumes months of training time, and because time is non-renewable,
      shortcutting avoidable mistakes is worth far more than the financial cost
      of the coaching fee.
  - question: How do I stay accountable to my cycling goals in the new year?
    answer: >-
      Book a specific target event as early as possible and tell people about
      it. The combination of a paid entry fee and public commitment creates real
      consequences for backing out, which is a far stronger motivator than a
      private resolution made on 1 January.
  - question: Does diet matter at the start of a new cycling training programme?
    answer: >-
      It is one of the first things to address. Coach Anthony Walsh recommends
      removing processed foods, alcohol, and sugar from the kitchen entirely,
      because eliminating the physical presence of temptation is more effective
      than relying on willpower each day. A clean home environment makes
      consistent nutrition the path of least resistance from the outset.
