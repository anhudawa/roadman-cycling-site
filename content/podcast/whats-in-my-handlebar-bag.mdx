answerCapsule: >-
  In this solo episode of the Roadman Podcast, host Anthony Walsh catalogues the
  contents of his cycling handlebar bag as a practical ride-survival kit. The
  inventory includes a cotton cap (for café hair and rain runoff), cable ties, a
  chain power link, a packable rain jacket, a lightweight keychain lock to deter
  opportunist thieves, an Ass Saver mudguard, a multi-tool with chain breaker,
  pump, CO2, tubes, a patch kit, insulating tape, and spare power-meter
  batteries. Walsh argues the cheap "boil-in-the-bag" rain jacket is the single
  most versatile garment in a cyclist's wardrobe.

faq:
  - question: What should I keep in my handlebar bag for cycling?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends carrying a packable rain jacket, inner tubes, a
      multi-tool with chain breaker, CO2 canisters, a pump, a patch kit, a chain
      power link, cable ties, and an Ass Saver mudguard. A lightweight keychain
      lock is also worth including to deter opportunist thieves when stopping
      briefly. These items cover the most common mechanical and weather
      emergencies on a road ride.
  - question: What is the best packable rain jacket for cycling?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh argues that a cheap, lightweight "boil-in-the-bag" rain
      jacket is the single most versatile garment a cyclist can own. It packs
      small enough to sit permanently in a handlebar bag without taking up
      meaningful space. The priority is packability and wind protection rather
      than premium waterproofing.
  - question: Do I need a lock on a road bike ride?
    answer: >-
      A small keychain lock is worth carrying even on road rides where you do
      not plan to stop for long. It is not intended to stop a determined thief
      but will deter opportunist theft while you are inside a café. Anthony
      Walsh keeps one permanently in his handlebar bag for exactly this purpose.
  - question: What size handlebar bag do I need for a road bike?
    answer: >-
      A compact handlebar bag needs to fit a rain jacket, two inner tubes, a
      multi-tool, pump, CO2, patch kit, cable ties, a chain link, spare
      batteries, and a cotton cap at minimum. Most riders find a bag in the 1 to
      3 litre range sufficient for this kind of ride-survival kit without adding
      significant weight or affecting handling.
