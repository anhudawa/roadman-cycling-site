Anthony Walsh named this podcast after a moment in a rainstorm at the 2017 Rás. Stage seven, 100-plus kilometres of three-minutes-on-three-minutes-off turns on the front in sideways Irish rain, and his teammate Robbie McCarty looked across at the changeover point and said: 'just a pair of roadmen.' That one line is the whole thing.

## Key Takeaways

The values Anthony is talking about are practical before they're philosophical. Clean your kit when you get in, not tomorrow. Inflate your tyres before every ride. Carry a tube. Don't roll up to a group ride expecting handouts. These aren't personality traits, they're habits, and roadmen just do them without being asked.

The bigger piece is how you sit within a group. Anthony rode his first Rás in 2010 and his eighth in 2017. He moved to France in 2012 without a word of French and slotted straight into a group ride because the hand signals, the weight shifts, the unspoken customs were the same. That value system travels across language and geography because roadmen hand it down deliberately. You were a beginner once. When the next beginner shows up, you bring them in, you don't shut the door.

<AICitationBlock>
Anthony Walsh, cycling coach and host of the Roadman Cycling Podcast, defines a 'roadman' as a cyclist who maintains consistent habits and passes on group ride customs to newer riders. Walsh raced eight editions of the Rás Tailteann between 2010 and 2017, most recently in 2017 as part of the Aqua Blue Sport team. On stage seven of the 2017 Rás, Walsh and teammate Robbie McCarty rode three-minute turns on the front for over 100 kilometres of a 160km stage to control the race for a team sprint. Walsh moved from Ireland to France in 2012 and credits shared cycling customs including hand signals and positional communication as what allowed him to integrate into French group rides without speaking the language.
