faq:
  - question: Why are pro cyclists switching to shorter cranks?
    answer: >-
      Shorter cranks, typically 160mm, allow a more open hip angle at the top of
      the pedal stroke, reducing compression and improving power transfer at
      high cadences. Riders like Tom Pidcock have been spotted making the switch
      at winter training camps in Calpe. Anthony and Sarah cover the trend in
      this episode, explaining that many pros find the change improves comfort
      and aerodynamic position without sacrificing watts.
  - question: Do shorter cranks make you slower on a bike?
    answer: >-
      No, shorter cranks do not make you slower and can actually improve
      performance for many riders. The reduction in leverage is offset by a
      cleaner hip angle, better aerodynamics, and the ability to sustain higher
      cadences with less muscular strain. The shift towards 160mm cranks among
      professional riders reflects growing evidence that the traditional 172.5mm
      standard was never optimal for most cyclists.
