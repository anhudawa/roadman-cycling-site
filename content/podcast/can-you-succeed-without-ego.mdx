faq:
  - question: Can you be successful without having a big ego?
    answer: >-
      Yes, and Anthony Walsh argues that a large ego is more likely to hinder
      long-term success than accelerate it. Adopting a callous or self-serving
      attitude to climb faster tends to produce isolation and unhappiness rather
      than the fulfilment people are actually chasing.
  - question: Does being rich and famous make you happy?
    answer: >-
      Statistically, no. Anthony Walsh cites Jim Carrey's observation that
      wealthy and famous people repeatedly report no meaningful increase in
      wellbeing after reaching the top. Chasing money and fame as a route to
      happiness is, by the evidence, an irrational strategy.
  - question: Should you emulate Michael Jordan's leadership style to succeed?
    answer: >-
      Walsh pushes back hard against this idea, calling it a fundamental misread
      of what actually drove Jordan's success. Mistreating teammates and staff,
      a trend popularised by The Last Dance, is not a transferable blueprint and
      carries serious personal and professional costs.
  - question: Is ego necessary to reach the top of your field?
    answer: >-
      Walsh's position is that ego is not the driver of elite success, and
      treating it as a requirement is a misreading of high-profile case studies
      like Michael Jordan or Steve Jobs. The habits and work ethic behind their
      results can be separated from the callousness that surrounded them.
