answerCapsule: >-
  Host Anthony Walsh, an Irish cycling coach, argues that riding in city traffic
  is a distinct skill most cyclists underestimate, and offers three concrete
  tactics for urban commuters. First, run a rear light and wear bright colours
  even in daylight, following pro peloton practice. Second, "take the lane"
  approaching left-hand junctions to prevent vehicles cutting across your path,
  regardless of horns behind you. Third, avoid hugging the gutter, where broken
  pavement and drainage grates collect, and claim road space rather than letting
  buses squeeze you into hazards.

faq:
  - question: Should I use a rear light when cycling in daylight?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Anthony Walsh recommends running a rear light during the day,
      following the same practice used by professional peloton riders.
      Visibility in urban traffic is too important to leave to chance, and a
      flashing rear light makes you significantly more conspicuous to drivers
      even in bright conditions.
  - question: How do I stop cars cutting across me at junctions when cycling?
    answer: >-
      Take the lane as you approach a left-hand junction rather than staying
      close to the kerb. This prevents vehicles from pulling alongside and
      cutting across your path. Anthony Walsh recommends holding that position
      confidently even if drivers behind you sound their horns.
  - question: Is it safer to cycle close to the kerb in traffic?
    answer: >-
      No. Hugging the gutter puts you in the worst part of the road, where
      broken surfaces, drainage grates, and debris collect. Anthony Walsh
      advises claiming road space instead, which also stops buses and large
      vehicles from squeezing you into those hazards.
  - question: What colours should I wear when cycling in the city?
    answer: >-
      Wear bright, high-visibility colours rather than dark or muted kit.
      Anthony Walsh points to professional riders as a model here, noting that
      making yourself as visible as possible in urban traffic is a basic and
      consistently overlooked safety habit.
