faq:
  - question: can you shit yourself cycling from an energy drink
    answer: >-
      Yes, and it is more common than athletes admit. Caffeine stimulates gut
      motility, and combining a high-sugar, high-caffeine drink with physical
      exertion and adrenaline can trigger urgent bowel movements, especially if
      the product is unfamiliar to your digestive system.
  - question: why should you not try new nutrition on race day
    answer: >-
      Your gut responds differently to new formulas under race stress than it
      does at rest. Anthony Walsh learned this firsthand when trialling a High5
      energy drink during a ride in the Wicklow Mountains, which ended in him
      soiling himself on the bike. Always test products in low-stakes training
      sessions first.
  - question: how does caffeine affect digestion during cycling
    answer: >-
      Caffeine increases intestinal contractions and speeds up gut transit time,
      which is useful for alertness but risky during hard efforts. When combined
      with concentrated sugar and the stress hormones produced during intense
      riding, an unfamiliar caffeine dose can cause cramping, urgency or worse.
      Stick to products your stomach already tolerates.
  - question: new energy drink made me need the toilet while cycling what happened
    answer: >-
      The combination of caffeine, sugar and exercise-induced adrenaline
      overloaded your gut, particularly if the drink was one you had not trained
      with before. Anthony Walsh uses exactly this scenario as a coaching
      lesson: never introduce untested nutrition during a threshold test, race
      or any session where performance data matters, as it risks both your
      result and your dignity.
