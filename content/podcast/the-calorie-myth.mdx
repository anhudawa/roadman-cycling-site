  takeaway: prioritise macronutrient quality and carbohydrate content over
  arbitrary calorie totals when training for leanness.

faq:
  - question: Why doesn't calorie counting work for weight loss?
    answer: >-
      Calorie counting ignores how different foods affect your hormones,
      particularly insulin. Two meals with identical calorie totals can produce
      completely different fat-burning responses depending on their
      macronutrient composition, so a calorie is not simply a calorie when it
      comes to body composition.
  - question: Does eating more carbs stop you burning fat?
    answer: >-
      Yes. High-carbohydrate meals trigger a significant insulin response, and
      elevated insulin directly shuts down fat oxidation. Until insulin levels
      drop back down, your body cannot access stored fat as a fuel source,
      regardless of how large a calorie deficit you are in.
  - question: What should cyclists eat to lose body fat without counting calories?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends building meals around protein and fat rather than
      obsessing over calorie totals. Practical examples include eggs with smoked
      salmon at breakfast, fish-based salads at lunch, and steak with sweet
      potato at dinner, keeping carbohydrate content low enough to maintain
      active fat-burning pathways throughout the day.
  - question: Is a high carb diet bad for cycling body composition?
    answer: >-
      For cyclists specifically targeting leanness, a diet dominated by
      carbohydrates will repeatedly suppress fat oxidation through insulin
      release. Prioritising macronutrient quality and moderating carbohydrate
      intake allows the body to burn fat more consistently, even during
      lower-intensity training periods.
