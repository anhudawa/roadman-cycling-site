  - question: What is the cycling tradition of giving kit to younger riders?
    answer: >-
      Cycling has a long-standing culture of experienced riders passing on kit,
      knowledge, and support to those coming up behind them. Anthony Walsh
      argues this tradition has declined as the sport has become increasingly
      focused on performance metrics and personal gain. He cites a viral story
      of Peter Sagan stopping to fix a stranded cyclist's puncture in Gran
      Canaria as an example of the older, more generous spirit of the sport.
  - question: Did Peter Sagan really stop to help a stranded cyclist?
    answer: >-
      Yes, Peter Sagan was filmed stopping to fix a puncture for a stranded
      amateur cyclist in Gran Canaria, and the story went viral. Walsh uses this
      incident in the episode to argue that helping fellow riders, whether
      fixing a puncture or passing on kit, is a core part of what cycling should
      be. He sees Sagan's gesture as a reminder of a culture that is
      increasingly rare.
  - question: Should you stop to help a cyclist with a puncture?
    answer: >-
      Yes, stopping to help a stranded rider is a well-established cycling
      tradition and one Walsh argues riders should actively revive. Offering a
      spare inner tube, a CO2 cartridge, or simply waiting with someone is a
      small gesture that makes a significant difference. The episode makes the
      case that this kind of mutual support is what separates cycling from
      simply being a data-driven fitness pursuit.
