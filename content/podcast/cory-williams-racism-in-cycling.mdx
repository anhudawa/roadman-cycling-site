    answer: >-
      Yes, and Cory Williams speaks directly to this in the podcast. He
      describes having to work significantly harder than white counterparts to
      secure sponsorships and racing opportunities, despite being one of the
      fastest criterium riders in the world. Cycling has historically taken a
      neutral stance on racial discrimination, which Williams argues actively
      enables it.
  - question: What is the Legion cycling team and why was it founded?
    answer: >-
      Legion is a Los Angeles-based professional cycling team co-founded by Cory
      Williams with an explicit diversity mandate. It was established to create
      structured pathways into competitive cycling for riders who face racial
      and structural barriers to entry. Williams positions Legion as a working
      model for what inclusive professional cycling can look like.
  - question: Why are there so few Black cyclists in professional road racing?
    answer: >-
      The barriers are structural rather than athletic. Cory Williams explains
      that Black riders face compounding disadvantages in accessing sponsorship,
      development programmes, and team opportunities, regardless of their
      performance level. These systemic obstacles filter out talented riders
      long before they reach the professional peloton.
  - question: How can professional cycling become more racially inclusive?
    answer: >-
      Williams argues that teams and governing bodies need to move beyond
      neutrality and take an active position against racial discrimination in
      the sport. The Legion team model, which centres diversity as a core part
      of its mission rather than an afterthought, is one concrete example of how
      organisations can change who gets to race at the top level.
