  Host Anthony Walsh, founder of A1 Coaching with over eight years of athlete
  coaching experience, identifies the most common error among time-crunched
  cyclists: chasing average speed, which traps riders at a perceived effort of
  seven out of ten and confines roughly 90 percent of training to zone three.
  The fix is zonal training built from a 20-minute threshold test, with heart
  rate users extending to 24 minutes and averaging the final 20 to account for
  cardiac lag. Performance gains require distributing hours across multiple
  physiological systems, not repeating one.

faq:
  - question: Why is chasing average speed bad for cycling training?
    answer: >-
      Chasing average speed pushes most riders into zone three for the majority
      of their training, which sits between easy and hard without delivering the
      full benefits of either. Anthony Walsh of A1 Coaching identifies this as
      the single most common mistake among time-crunched cyclists, as it locks
      effort at around seven out of ten and prevents the physiological variety
      needed for real performance gains.
  - question: What is zone 3 cycling and why should you avoid training there too much?
    answer: >-
      Zone three is a moderate-intensity band that feels comfortably hard but
      stops short of true threshold effort. Spending roughly 90 percent of
      training time there, as many riders do when chasing speed, means you are
      neither building a deep aerobic base nor developing threshold and
      high-intensity systems, so fitness progress stalls over time.
  - question: How do you do a 20-minute threshold test for cycling zones?
    answer: >-
      Ride as hard as you can sustain for 20 minutes and record your average
      power or heart rate. Anthony Walsh recommends heart rate users extend the
      effort to 24 minutes and average only the final 20 to correct for cardiac
      lag, giving a more accurate threshold figure from which to calculate
      training zones.
  - question: How should a time-crunched cyclist structure their training zones?
    answer: >-
      Build a zonal training programme from an accurate threshold test, then
      distribute your available hours across multiple physiological systems
      rather than repeating the same moderate effort every ride. Each zone
      targets a different adaptation, and spreading work across them is what
      produces measurable fitness improvements when training time is limited.
