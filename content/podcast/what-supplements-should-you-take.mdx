faq:
  - question: What supplements should endurance cyclists take?
    answer: >-
      Anthony Walsh recommends a core stack of iron, vitamin B12, and vitamin D
      for endurance cyclists. Iron is flagged as the most performance-relevant,
      since standard recommended daily allowances are too low for athletes
      training at high volumes.
  - question: Should I get a blood test before taking supplements?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Anthony Walsh advises getting a full blood panel from your GP before
      starting any supplement protocol. This ensures you are addressing genuine
      deficiencies rather than supplementing blindly, and prevents unnecessary
      or counterproductive intake.
  - question: How do I improve iron absorption from supplements?
    answer: >-
      Take iron alongside vitamin C, which significantly improves absorption.
      Avoid tea, coffee, and other tannin-containing drinks around the time of
      dosing, as tannins and caffeine both inhibit how effectively your body
      takes up iron.
  - question: Can supplements replace a poor diet for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      No. Supplements only work effectively alongside a balanced diet and cannot
      compensate for poor nutrition. They are intended to fill specific gaps
      when dietary intake falls short, not to serve as a substitute for whole
      foods.
