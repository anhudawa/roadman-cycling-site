faq:
  - question: How do I know if my daily habits are aligned with my goals?
    answer: >-
      Map out your entire week in time blocks and compare how you actually spend
      your hours against the outcomes you say you want. Anthony Walsh recommends
      treating this like tracking calories: the data exposes where your time is
      drifting without you realising it. The gap between intended and actual
      behaviour is where most habits need attention.
  - question: How do I do a habit audit to improve my routine?
    answer: >-
      Write out a full week hour by hour, then assess each block honestly
      against your stated goals. Walsh uses this method as a diagnostic tool
      rather than a form of self-criticism, so the aim is reflection, not guilt.
      Once you can see the pattern clearly, you can make deliberate adjustments
      to restore the habits that matter.
  - question: How can I use lockdown to build better habits?
    answer: >-
      Walsh argues that lockdown acts as a magnifying glass, making it easier to
      spot which behaviours are serving you and which are not. With fewer
      external distractions, your default routines become more visible and
      therefore easier to analyse and change. Use the reduced noise as an
      opportunity to reset rather than waiting for normal life to resume.
  - question: How do I make time to read more when I am too busy with work?
    answer: >-
      Walsh identified that his own creation and work tasks had gradually
      crowded out reading for pleasure, and resolved to restore it by treating
      it as a scheduled time block rather than a leftover activity. Assigning
      reading a fixed slot in your weekly plan, the same way you would a meeting
      or training session, prevents it from being consistently displaced by
      busier priorities.
