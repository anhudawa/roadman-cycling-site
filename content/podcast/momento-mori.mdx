faq:
  - question: What is Memento Mori and how does it apply to cycling?
    answer: >-
      Memento Mori is a Latin phrase meaning "remember you will die," used by
      Stoic philosophers including Marcus Aurelius as a daily reminder of
      mortality. In cycling, Anthony Walsh applies it as a mental framework that
      stops riders becoming arrogant during good form and helps them accept
      crashes or setbacks without being destroyed by them.
  - question: What injuries did Egan Bernal sustain in his 2022 crash?
    answer: >-
      Bernal collided with a parked truck in Colombia and suffered broken
      vertebrae, a punctured lung, and a broken leg. The crash was widely
      considered life-threatening and raised serious questions about whether he
      would race professionally again.
  - question: What happened to Imogen Cotter in her cycling crash?
    answer: >-
      Irish National Champion Imogen Cotter was hit head-on by a car while
      training. Walsh references her crash alongside Bernal's as a reminder that
      serious collisions can happen to elite riders at any level of the sport,
      regardless of preparation or fitness.
  - question: How do Stoic principles help athletes deal with injury and adversity?
    answer: >-
      Stoicism teaches that most outcomes are outside our control, so energy is
      better spent on our response than on the event itself. Walsh draws on
      Marcus Aurelius to argue that riders who regularly reflect on mortality
      treat both success and suffering with greater equanimity, which shortens
      recovery mentally as well as physically.
