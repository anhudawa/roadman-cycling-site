faq:
  - question: How do I convert a van into a mobile podcast studio?
    answer: >-
      You need reliable power, acoustic treatment, and a stable internet
      connection. Anthony Walsh's Honda Step Wagon build combines a leisure
      battery setup with satellite Wi-Fi for remote uploads, which solves the
      connectivity problem that defeats most mobile studio attempts. Keep the
      interior layout flexible so the same space works for sleeping and
      recording.
  - question: What is the best campervan for stealth camping in Europe?
    answer: >-
      Compact MPVs and minivans like the Honda Step Wagon are popular choices
      because they look ordinary parked on a street and offer enough interior
      length for a flat bed. A pop-up roof adds sleeping space without
      increasing the vehicle's footprint when driving. Stealth builds avoid
      obvious campervan features like external vents or roof boxes that attract
      attention.
  - question: How do you get internet access in a campervan for live streaming?
    answer: >-
      Satellite internet, such as Starlink, is the most reliable option for
      remote locations where mobile data signals are weak or absent. Anthony
      Walsh's van build specifically includes satellite Wi-Fi to allow episode
      uploads and live broadcasts from Tour de France mountain stages. A 4G
      router with a multi-network SIM is a cheaper backup for areas with
      reasonable mobile coverage.
  - question: >-
      How do podcasters broadcast live from cycling events like the Tour de
      France?
    answer: >-
      Most remote podcast productions rely on a combination of mobile data and
      pre-recorded segments, but signal dropout on mountain climbs like the
      Angliru makes this unreliable. Anthony Walsh's approach is to park a
      self-contained mobile studio at the roadside so the recording environment
      is controlled regardless of weather or fatigue. This removes the
      dependency on race hospitality infrastructure or tent setups that leave
      presenters too tired to perform well.
