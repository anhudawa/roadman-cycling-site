  Coach and host of the A1 Show podcast breaks down Eliud Kipchoge's
  sub-two-hour marathon (1:59:40) and extracts the physiological and
  psychological lessons for amateur cyclists. The episode unpacks the three
  variables Michael Joyner identified in 1991 as the ceiling of endurance
  performance: VO2 max, lactate threshold, and economy. A key argument is that
  VO2 max alone is misleading without efficiency context, and that most cyclists
  set goals too conservatively, hitting targets without satisfaction because the
  ambition was wrong from the start.

      performance?
    answer: >-
      Physiologist Michael Joyner identified VO2 max, lactate threshold, and

  - question: Why is VO2 max a misleading measure for cyclists?
    answer: >-
      A high VO2 max score tells you very little without knowing how efficiently
      a rider converts that oxygen into power output. Two cyclists with
      identical VO2 max values can produce vastly different performances
      depending on their riding economy and the percentage of that ceiling they
      can sustain before lactate accumulates.

      close to achieving.
