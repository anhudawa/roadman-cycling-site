## What this week is for

You're at Week 11. The system is built. Architecture, fuelling, strength, recovery, power, mental game — every pillar has been addressed and you've lived inside the framework for ten weeks. Most courses end here. We don't, and the reason matters.

The hardest skill in this entire course isn't running an interval at the right power. It isn't fuelling the long ride. It isn't lifting a weight with good form. It's making smart decisions when life happens — when you miss a session, when you get a cold, when work blows up, when the legs feel weird and you don't know if it's fatigue or fitness. That's self-coaching, and that's what this week teaches.

By Sunday night, you should be able to run the next 12 weeks of training without us. You'll still want the community, the conversations, the updated plans — that's what Not Done Yet is there for. But the dependence on a programme telling you what to do next? That ends this week. You become the coach.

## The five common mistakes self-coached athletes make

Before we build the toolkit, the diagnostic. Across our work with the community, five patterns come up over and over in self-coached riders:

**1. Using AI for coaching decisions.** I'm going to take a clear position here. AI is useful for pulling reference data, comparing studies, and answering specific questions. It is not a coach. Coaching is a relationship — between an athlete and someone who knows their context, history, life pressures, and recovery patterns. The first three weeks of any new coaching engagement are the coach learning the athlete. AI doesn't have those three weeks. Don't use AI for the daily go/no-go call.

**2. No periodisation.** Doing the same training week, month after month, without progression or recovery. We covered this in Week 7 — the cure is the mesocycle structure. Self-coached riders often default back to the same week because it's safer. Don't.

**3. Ignoring recovery.** Training through fatigue, treating rest days as optional, never running a true recovery week. Week 5 covered this. The fix is running a 3:1 mesocycle structure and protecting recovery weeks like a religion.

**4. Tracking the wrong metrics.** Obsessing over TSS, weekly hours, or strava-segment PRs while ignoring the metrics that actually predict adaptation: sleep average, intensity distribution, weekly RPE summary, body composition trend. Pick three to track. Ignore the rest.

**5. Training alone without feedback.** No accountability, no second opinion, no feedback loop. The community exists for this reason — when you don't have a coach, you need other riders to mirror you. Post in the threads. Share the data. Ask the questions.

If any of those five patterns sound familiar, [Five Mistakes Self-Coached Cyclists Make (And How to Fix Them)](/blog/five-mistakes-self-coached-cyclists-make) is the standalone version of this diagnostic.

If two of those five describe you over the next three months, it's a flag. Self-coaching works when the discipline is internal. The framework below is how you build that discipline.

## The weekly self-coaching review — 15 minutes, every Sunday

This is the single most important habit from this module. Block 15 minutes every Sunday for the rest of your cycling life.

The review covers six questions:

**1. What did I actually do this week?** Pull the data. Not what was planned — what happened. List sessions, durations, average heart rate or power, RPE.

**2. What was the intensity distribution?** Calculate green / yellow / red percentage. Compare to the polarised target.

**3. How did I feel?** Sleep average, leg feel, motivation, mood. The subjective scale matters as much as the data.

**4. What's the trend?** Compare this week to the last three weeks. Are the green-zone sessions getting easier at the same heart rate? Are the quality sessions producing the right power? Is body composition trending where it should?

**5. What am I changing for next week?** Based on the trend, what's the adjustment? More volume? More recovery? Different session order? Be specific.

**6. What's the one thing I'm watching?** Pick one thing — a single metric, a single sensation, a single behaviour — and watch it across the coming week. Not five. One.

That's the review. Fifteen minutes. The template in your downloads has the prompts and the space to write.

This is what professional coaches do for their riders. You're now doing it for yourself. The discipline of the review is the discipline of self-coaching.

## The decision tree — common scenarios

Self-coaching is mostly making good decisions in unclear situations. The Decision Tree Cards in your downloads cover the most common ones. Some highlights:

**Scenario: I missed today's session.**

- Was it just one session, in an otherwise on-track week? → Skip it. Don't try to make it up. Move on.
- Was it the long ride? → Push it to tomorrow if possible. If not, swap with the next quality session and slide the structure.
- Has this been the third missed session in two weeks? → Audit. Something is off — work, sleep, motivation, or fitness. Don't keep grinding through.

**Scenario: I'm getting sick.**

- Symptoms above the neck (sniffle, mild sore throat) → Easy ride only. No quality work. Sleep extra.
- Symptoms below the neck (chest, fever, body aches) → Off the bike. Full stop. Resume only when symptoms have been gone for 48 hours, then start at 50% load.

**Scenario: Travel week.**

- Plan for fewer hours. Pick two or three sessions you can realistically do. Keep one quality session and one long ride if possible. Strength becomes one bodyweight session.
- Don't try to maintain normal volume. Travel is real life stress. Match the load to reality.

**Scenario: My morning numbers are bad and a quality session is on the plan.**

- Two days in a row of bad numbers → Swap to easy ride or rest. Don't push through.
- One bad day after a hard session yesterday → Probably normal post-session fatigue. Trust the plan.
- One bad day with no obvious cause → Run a 10-minute warm-up. If the legs come good, do the session. If not, swap to easy.

**Scenario: I'm flying, the legs feel great, I want to do extra.**

- This is the most dangerous self-coaching moment. Don't add load impulsively. The reason you feel great is the plan. Do the planned session, then go for a bonus easy ride if you must — never bonus quality.

These aren't exhaustive — the Decision Tree Cards give you more — but they're the situations that come up most. Memorise the principles.

## When to back off — Lorang's two-interval rule, again

This is worth repeating because it's the single most useful real-time decision tool in coaching:

> "If you are doing these intervals and you can stay in that frame, you continue. But if you drop in the first two intervals already — the quality at that point — then we skip and you just make an easy run. And we reset the program."

Two intervals. If they're both off the prescribed power or RPE by more than 5%, you switch to easy. No grinding through. No "I'll just do the last one." Save the legs. The body is telling you it can't make the adaptation today.

The hardest part of self-coaching is making this call without ego. There's no one else to give permission. You give it to yourself, you write down what happened, you move on.

## Planning the next mesocycle — the post-course block

This week, plan your first post-course four-week mesocycle. Use the Mesocycle Builder Worksheet.

The plan needs five things:

**1. The phase.** Is the next block another Build, a Peak into a goal event, or a Base re-set? Most riders coming out of this course will benefit from one more Build mesocycle (Weeks 13–16) before deciding whether to peak for an event or return to Base.

**2. The progression.** What's getting harder, and how? Volume? Intensity? Specific sessions? Pick one variable to progress meaningfully.

**3. The 3:1 structure.** Three loading weeks, one recovery week. Don't deviate.

**4. The non-negotiables.** What stays the same? Strength twice a week. Long ride. Sleep target. Identify what holds while everything else flexes.

**5. The single watched metric.** What's the one number you're tracking across this block? Pick it before you start, not retrospectively.

That plan, in your hands, is what you've earned in 11 weeks. Not a templated programme. A plan you understand.

## What "becoming your own best coach" actually means

It does not mean you don't need anyone else. It means:

- You make the daily go/no-go calls without paralysis
- You know which metrics matter and which to ignore
- You recognise the patterns in your own physiology
- You build plans you understand, not plans you copied
- You ask better questions of better experts because you know what you don't know

The riders who stay sharp into their 40s, 50s and 60s aren't the ones with the most expensive coach. They're the ones who built durable self-coaching habits early and used coaches and communities to refine, not to replace.

That's what this week is for.

## Common mistakes this week

**Skipping the weekly review because the week was unremarkable.** The unremarkable weeks are the ones the review compounds across. Every Sunday. Fifteen minutes. No exceptions.

**Building the next mesocycle by copying the last one.** Progression. Pick one variable, move it.

**Ignoring the watched metric because you don't trust it.** If you've picked the metric and you don't trust it, you picked the wrong metric. Pick a better one — but pick one.

**Going back to AI for coaching decisions.** I've already said it. Don't.

**Treating Week 12 as the finish line.** Week 12 is the start line for the next phase. The system continues. The community continues. The work continues.
