## You did the work

Twelve weeks ago you ran a difficult audit and put numbers on the page that probably weren't comfortable. You built the architecture, fuelled it properly, added strength, learned to recover, ran sweet-spot, dialled torque, addressed the head game, and learned to coach yourself.

This week we measure. Then we reflect. Then we look forward.

The course doesn't end here — the system does. What ends today is being a programme follower. What starts today is being a practitioner. The next 12 weeks, the next 12 months, the next decade of your cycling life — they happen on the back of the foundation you've laid in the past 84 days. The right thing to do this week is honour the work by measuring it honestly and planning what comes next with the same rigour.

## The Exit Assessment

Same six domains as Week 1. Same conditions if possible — same scale, same protocol, same time of day. The Exit Assessment Workbook is in your downloads.

**1. Power baseline.** Retest. Same protocol you used in Week 1 if possible. Same warm-up. The number you produce here is meaningful — it's the answer to the question that brought you to the course in the first place.

**2. Real training load review.** Pull the last 12 weeks. What was your actual completed volume? What was the intensity distribution? Compare to the four weeks before the course started.

**3. Body composition.** Same scale, same conditions, same time of day. Measure waist, hip, weight. Take Week 12 photos for direct comparison with Week 1.

**4. Sleep average.** What's been your real seven-night average for the past fortnight? Compare to your Week 1 baseline.

**5. Subjective.** Energy, mood, motivation, leg feel — same 1–10 scales as Week 1.

**6. Goal review.** Did you hit your primary 12-week goal? Your supporting goals? The single measurable target?

Don't massage the numbers. The numbers are how we learn. If you didn't hit the target, the data tells you why — almost always it's an input (sleep, fuelling, stress, intensity discipline) rather than an effort problem. If you blew through the target, that's also data — it tells you what's possible from here.

## What the data usually shows at Week 12

Across the riders we work with through this system, the typical Week 12 picture:

- **Power up by 8–25 watts.** Smaller for riders who came in already well-trained. Larger for riders who came in with the most leaks.
- **Body composition meaningfully shifted.** 3–6kg lower, with body fat percentage following.
- **Intensity distribution holding above 70% green.** The polarised discipline has stuck.
- **Sleep up 30–60 minutes from baseline.** The single biggest non-training intervention.
- **Power-to-weight ratio improved by 5–12%.** This is the number that actually matters on the climbs, and it usually moves more than absolute power.
- **Subjective scales up across the board.** Energy, mood, motivation, leg feel — riders feel different in their bodies, not just on the bike.

If three of those six describe your Week 12 numbers, the system worked. If five or six describe you, you're going to be hard to recognise from where you started.

If the numbers haven't moved as much as you'd hoped — that's also useful information. Twelve weeks is a meaningful block, but it's also a short block. Some adaptations take 6–12 months to compound. The system is what produces the long-term result. Twelve weeks is where the discipline became a habit. The watts come.

## The reflection — five pillars, five paragraphs

Write three to five sentences per pillar. Honest. Rough. The 12-Week Review is a private document — write it for yourself.

**Coaching.** What about my training architecture changed? What's the most important habit I'll carry forward? What did I get wrong in the first four weeks?

**Nutrition.** What's the change in my relationship with food? Where am I still leaking? What fuelling rule is going to stay non-negotiable?

**Strength.** Did I do the work? What did the gym give me on the bike? What's the plan for keeping it through the next 12 weeks?

**Recovery.** Has my sleep changed? My stress management? What's the recovery habit I almost gave up but kept, and what did it produce?

**Le Metier.** Has my relationship to riding changed? Am I enjoying it more? Less? Why? What's the next ride I want to do — not the next race, the next ride.

These aren't journal prompts to be precious about. They're the questions you'd ask a rider you coached. Now you're asking them of yourself, because you are the rider you coach.

## The 90-Day Forward Plan

The template in your downloads asks you to commit to five things for the next 90 days:

**1. The training phase.** Continue Build? Move into a Peak for a target event? Drop into a Base re-set? Pick one and own it.

**2. The mesocycle structure.** Three weeks loading, one week recovery, repeat. Three mesocycles in 90 days, with a four-day buffer for travel, illness, life. Lock the dates in the calendar.

**3. The non-negotiables.** Strength twice a week. Sleep target. Long ride. The polarised discipline. List the things that hold no matter what.

**4. The one variable to progress.** Pick one. Don't pick five. Volume, threshold work, climbing-specific sessions, race-pace fuelling tolerance — one thing to deliberately progress.

**5. The metric you watch.** One. Pick it. Watch it for 90 days. Don't change it because of a bad week.

Write it. Print it. Stick it on the wall by the bike. The plan you can see is the plan you keep.

## What the system actually changed

Most riders who come through this course don't tell us about the watts when they finish. They tell us about something else.

They tell us they've stopped second-guessing every session. The relief of that — the mental space recovered from not constantly wondering whether they're doing the right work — is what they didn't expect when they signed up.

They tell us they're sleeping better. They tell us they're not as wrecked between rides. They tell us their kids have noticed they're in a better mood on weekends.

They tell us they've started enjoying the ride again. The pressure of "I have to make this hour count" softens into "I trust the architecture." The training becomes a frame for the riding, not a substitute for it.

These are real outcomes from real riders, and they're the ones that compound into the next decade of cycling. The watts are downstream. The system change is upstream.

## The closing argument — Lachlan Morton, one more time

I keep coming back to Morton because he's the rider I'd want every Roadman Method graduate to think about when they're deciding what comes next:

> "I had this realisation — this experience that I've had, five days with some mates out in the hills, just doing 100ks a day and then stopping and having a beer at night. I was like, this is the actual experience that people would enjoy riding. That needs to be part of what I do in terms of promoting the sport. Because you can always go bigger and longer or faster — but ultimately it's like, what are we trying to get out of bikes? We want people to get out there, be healthy, but just enjoy it for what it is."

The science on maintaining and building performance into your 40s and 50s — from masters athletes who are getting faster, not slower — is in [Cycling After 40: The Science of Getting Faster](/blog/cycling-after-40-faster-science).

Performance serves enjoyment. Not the other way around. The system you've built over 12 weeks gives you the tools to get faster — but the goal of getting faster is to enjoy the riding more. Faster up the climb you're attempting next year. Stronger on the long ride you've been wanting to do. Fitter at 50 than you were at 40, and still in the saddle at 60. The watts are in service of the ride.

That's what *Not Done Yet* actually means. The course has finished. The riding hasn't. The next chapter is already starting.

## Common mistakes this week

**Skipping the Exit Assessment because you don't think the numbers will be impressive.** The numbers don't have to be impressive. The honesty has to be. Run the assessment. The data is for you.

**Treating Week 12 as the finish line.** It's not. It's a checkpoint. The next mesocycle starts Monday.

**Comparing your numbers to someone else's.** Your Week 1 to your Week 12 is the only comparison that matters. Not your buddy's Week 12. Yours.

**Letting the discipline lapse the day the course ends.** The system works because it's continuous. The Sunday review continues. The architecture continues. The non-negotiables continue.

**Forgetting why you started.** Go back and read the Week 1 audit. Read the goal you wrote down. Notice how far you've come from the rider who wrote that. Then plan the next 12 weeks for the rider you are now.

## What I want you to take from these 12 weeks

Three things, if you take only three.

The first: trust the easy work. The single most counter-intuitive idea in the entire system is that going slower most of the time is what builds the engine that lets you go faster when it matters. Every coach we've had on the podcast says it. The data backs it. Trust it for another 12 weeks and the next 12 watts come for free.

The second: the system is more important than any single session. You'll have bad weeks. Bad sessions. Weeks where life eats the schedule. The system survives. The discipline of the weekly review, the mesocycle structure, the recovery week — those are what hold across imperfect weeks. The session you nailed yesterday matters less than the architecture you ran for the past three months.

The third: you're not done yet. Whatever brought you to this course — a target event, a feeling that there was more in the legs, a frustration with stalled progress — you've answered some of it but not all of it. The work that produced these 12 weeks of progress is the same work that will produce the next 12 weeks, and the next, and the decade after that. The version of you that finished Week 1 wouldn't recognise the version that finished Week 12. The version that finishes Year 5 won't recognise this one either.

Welcome to the rest of the riding. The map is yours. The compass is yours. The system is yours.

You're not done yet — and now you know exactly what to do next.
