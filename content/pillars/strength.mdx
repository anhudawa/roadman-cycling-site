Start with [Strength Training for Cyclists: What Works and How Often](/blog/cycling-strength-training-guide). It is the canonical broad guide and explains the latest cyclist-only meta-analysis: significant pooled effects for performance, efficiency and anaerobic power, no significant pooled VO2max effect, and a low certainty rating that prevents one universal protocol.

This library then separates narrower intents—beginner progression, movement selection, masters considerations, in-season maintenance, mobility and safe pain guidance—so each page can answer one job without competing with the broad owner.

## What We Cover

This pillar covers the evidence for gym-to-bike transfer, ways to learn and progress useful movement patterns, decisions about dose and scheduling, masters-specific context and supporting mobility work. Pain pages use clear clinical boundaries rather than promising that one exercise prevents or diagnoses injury.

## The Roadman Position

Three principles hold across this library:

**Evidence and coaching are labelled separately.** The latest review supports heavy strength training, but rates the outcome evidence low certainty and does not prove one best frequency or exercise list.

**The combined programme matters.** Gym progress does not justify repeatedly losing the quality of priority rides. Dose, schedule and exercise choice must fit the rider's goal and recovery.

**Age adds context, not one compulsory template.** General health guidance supports muscle-strengthening work for older adults, while cycling-specific performance prescriptions still require individualisation.

## Where to Go From Here

If you've never set foot in a gym, start with the [12-Week Beginner Plan](/blog/cycling-strength-training-12-week-beginner-plan). It builds stability first, then strength, then power — in the right order.

If you already lift but your programme was designed by a personal trainer who doesn't ride, read [The Only 8 Gym Exercises That Actually Matter](/blog/cycling-gym-exercises-best). Strip out the fluff. Keep what transfers.
