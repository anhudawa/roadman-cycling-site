## What cycling coaching is

A coaching service should state who performs each part of the work and how often it happens. The core functions are:

1. **Assess.** Establish the goal, training history, current capacity, available hours, calendar, equipment, preferences and relevant constraints.
2. **Plan.** Translate those inputs into training phases, weekly priorities and sessions with a stated purpose.
3. **Review.** Examine completed work and athlete feedback at the cadence promised in the service.
4. **Adapt.** Change upcoming work when the athlete's response, health, recovery, schedule or goal changes.
5. **Explain.** Help the athlete understand decisions, uncertainty and what evidence would justify the next change.

The cadence is not universal. Some services review weekly, others more or less often; some include scheduled calls, while others communicate in writing. A static plan can still be useful, but it should not be sold as continuous individual coaching if nobody is responsible for reviewing and adapting it.

## When coaching may be useful

Coaching becomes easier to justify when the value lies in decisions you are struggling to make alone. Common examples include:

- a specific event or performance goal with a deadline;
- a plateau that has persisted despite consistent training;
- work, travel, family or recovery constraints that repeatedly break generic plans;
- several competing goals that need sequencing;
- difficulty interpreting training data and subjective feedback together;
- a need for accountability, education or an agreed decision process.

Coaching is not automatically the best first purchase. A rider learning basic consistency may get enough structure from a suitable plan, club or app. A rider who needs medical diagnosis, injury treatment, therapeutic nutrition or mental-health care needs an appropriately qualified professional, whether or not a coach is also involved.

The decision should be based on the problem, the service promised and the athlete's ability to use it—not a universal number of training hours or months of experience.

## Online vs in-person coaching

Online and in-person coaching are delivery formats, not quality grades.

| Question | Online coaching can provide | In-person work may add |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planning and review | Shared calendars, ride files, written feedback and video calls | The same planning loop with local contact |
| Technique | Video review where the movement is visible | Direct observation of handling, sprinting, group skills or gym technique |
| Access | A wider choice of coaches, subject to time zones and communication | Easier local scheduling and contextual knowledge |
| Data | Remote access to the platforms the athlete authorises | Direct testing or observation when facilities are available |
| Safeguarding and privacy | Written channels, access controls and retention rules still matter | Venue, supervision and communication rules still matter |

Neither format guarantees responsiveness, individualisation or competence. Ask what is actually included rather than inferring the service from the word “online” or “local.”

## How to compare cycling coaches

Give every candidate the same short rider brief, then compare written answers across nine areas:

1. relevant goal and athlete experience;
2. verifiable qualifications, insurance and safeguarding where relevant;
3. professional scope and referral boundaries;
4. onboarding and training method;
5. who reviews what data, and when the plan can change;
6. communication channels, cadence and response expectations;
7. privacy, platform access, retention, export and deletion;
8. evidence presented with context rather than guarantees;
9. complete price, minimum term, renewal, pause and cancellation terms.

No certificate, testimonial or race result answers all nine. A qualification can establish training and scope; it does not prove fit. A case study can show a process and an outcome; it does not forecast what another rider will achieve.

## How to compare cycling coaching costs

Do not compare a monthly headline until you have normalised the billing basis and service. Four-week billing creates 13 charges in a 52-week year, while calendar-month billing creates 12. Group coaching, an individually reviewed plan and named-coach one-to-one support are different products even when each uses the word “personalised.”

Record:

- currency, tax, setup fee and billing interval;
- who writes and reviews the plan;
- review and call cadence;
- included contact channels and response window;
- strength, nutrition, testing or event support;
- minimum term, renewal, pause and exit conditions.

## Cycling coach vs training app

Apps and coaches overlap: either may deliver structured workouts, progression rules, reminders and data views. The practical distinction is accountability for judgement.

With an app, check which decisions are automated, which inputs it uses and when the athlete must override it. With a coach, check which named person is responsible for reviewing context, explaining a change and acting when the available data is incomplete or contradictory.

An app may be enough when the goal is straightforward and the rider can manage exceptions. Coaching may add more value when the rider wants a human decision-maker, the context changes frequently or the cost of repeated training mistakes is high. Some athletes use both: software for delivery and analysis, with a coach responsible for interpretation.

## How to judge whether coaching is working

Agree on process and outcome measures before the first block. Useful process measures can include completed priority sessions, communication delivered, reasons for plan changes and whether fatigue or disruption was handled as agreed. Outcome measures depend on the goal and may include a repeatable field test, sustainable power, event execution, consistency or a relevant skills assessment.

Interpret every result with its starting point, timeframe, testing method, adherence, concurrent changes and setbacks. Improvement is not guaranteed, and one athlete's result is not an expected result for another. If the service itself is not being delivered as written, that is a contract and communication question before it is a physiology question.

## Scope, safety and data

A cycling coach can plan and explain training within their competence. That does not automatically qualify the same person to diagnose an injury, treat disease, prescribe a therapeutic diet or provide mental-health care. Ask how pain, illness, suspected low energy availability, disordered eating or other out-of-scope concerns are referred.

Training files can expose location, routines, physiological response and health information. Before connecting an account, identify the business responsible for the data, the platforms and people with access, the purpose, retention period, export process and how access can be revoked.

For a young rider or adult at risk, establish the safeguarding lead, approved communication channels, supervision arrangements and reporting process before coaching begins. Requirements vary by governing body and country; verify the rules that apply to the actual coach and athlete.
