The evidence supports a measured position. The latest cyclist-only meta-analysis found statistically significant pooled effects for cycling performance, efficiency and anaerobic power, but rated the certainty of evidence as low. It found no significant pooled change in VO2max, power at VO2max, maximal metabolic steady state or anaerobic capacity, and it did not establish one optimal frequency or exercise list ([Llanos-Lagos and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40632222/)).

## What is supported—and what is not

**Supported with low-certainty cyclist-specific evidence:** heavy strength training can improve group-level cycling performance, cycling efficiency and anaerobic power.

**Not established by that review:** exactly two sessions per week; one mandatory list of exercises; automatic FTP or VO2max gains; no change in body mass; injury prevention; or a special effect in a masters-only subgroup.

**Broader health guidance:** WHO recommends adults, including older adults, perform muscle-strengthening activity involving major muscle groups on two or more days per week. This is a public-health recommendation, not proof of the best cycling-performance protocol ([WHO guidelines](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128)).

**Bone health:** road cycling does not appear to provide a meaningful osteogenic stimulus on its own. Resistance and impact activity may be relevant, but individual bone-health risk belongs with appropriate clinical assessment ([cycling and bone-health review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23256921/)).

## A simple decision framework

1. **Name the goal.** General health, maximal strength, short-power support, rehab and pain management are different jobs.
2. **Protect the bike priority.** A gym plan that repeatedly ruins the event-specific sessions is overdosed or badly placed.
3. **Choose trainable movements.** Prefer versions you can perform through a controlled range and progress without pain or form failure.
4. **Start below your recovery ceiling.** Add load or volume only when both gym and bike quality remain stable.
5. **Measure transfer.** A stronger lift proves the lift improved. Use a repeatable cycling measure to test whether it helped the bike.

## Masters cyclists

Strength work becomes important in the broader training picture as riders age, but “older” does not prescribe one rep range. Training history, health, bone risk, recovery and current cycling load matter more than a birthday alone.
