## Why Train Indoors

Indoor training isn't a substitute for outdoor riding. It's a different tool that does certain things better.

**Precision.** A controllable smart trainer can adjust resistance to the workout target without traffic lights or descents breaking the interval. The power source, calibration guidance and FTP used by the app still need to be consistent.

**Time efficiency.** Indoor work removes the ride to a suitable road and makes it easier to complete uninterrupted intervals. It does not make every indoor minute physiologically superior; it makes a specific session easier to control.

**Consistency.** Rain, ice, dark mornings, short winter days. None of it affects your indoor setup. The riders who build fitness through winter aren't more motivated than you. They just have a trainer in their spare room.

**Safety.** No cars. No potholes. No black ice. If your local roads are dangerous in winter, the turbo removes every excuse.

Dan Lorang uses indoor training with his World Tour athletes specifically for precision interval work. When the session calls for exact power targets, the trainer delivers what the road cannot.

## Indoor Training Platforms Compared

This is the question we get asked most inside Not Done Yet. The short answer: it depends on what keeps you riding — and whether your indoor training is connected to your outdoor plan.

| Feature | TrainingPeaks Virtual | Zwift | TrainerRoad |
|---------|----------------------|-------|-------------|
| **Primary focus** | Virtual riding inside the TrainingPeaks planning and analysis account | Designed virtual worlds, social riding, events and workouts | Adaptive structured planning and workout execution |
| **Planned workout path** | TrainingPeaks calendar workouts sync natively | Supports planned-workout sync, including TrainingPeaks and TrainerRoad workflows | Builds its own plan and can send scheduled workouts to Zwift |
| **Social riding** | Group rides, races and team tools | Deepest social and event-led experience of the three | Training is primary; social world is not the product |
| **Best for** | Riders who want indoor and outdoor work in one TrainingPeaks workflow | Riders motivated by worlds, groups, events and racing | Riders who want adaptive workout progression |
| **Membership note** | Included with TrainingPeaks Premium | Separate Zwift membership | Separate TrainerRoad membership; using it with Zwift requires both |

**TrainingPeaks Virtual** is the lowest-friction option when TrainingPeaks is already the planning home. The official platform documentation says Virtual is included with Premium and automatically syncs planned workouts and completed data to the same account.

If you struggle to get on the trainer because it's boring, Zwift's virtual worlds and social features will get you pedalling more often. More rides completed beats a theoretically perfect plan you abandon by week three.

TrainerRoad centres the experience on adaptive planning and structured progression. It can also send scheduled workouts to Zwift, which means the real decision is not always one app or the other; some riders use TrainerRoad for the plan and Zwift for the virtual environment.

Many riders in our community combine platforms. TrainingPeaks Virtual or TrainerRoad for structured intervals during the week, Zwift for group rides or virtual races at the weekend. That's a perfectly valid approach.

## Smart Trainer Guide

The trainer has one job: deliver the intended session reliably with your bike and software. Start with compatibility, not the largest wattage or gradient number.

**Direct-drive vs wheel-on:**

| Type | Mechanical setup | Main advantage | Main checks |
|------|------------------|----------------|-------------|
| **Direct-drive** | Rear wheel removed; chain drives a cassette or single cog on the trainer | Removes tyre contact and supports controlled resistance on compatible models | Frame, axle, freehub, cassette or Cog, calibration and app features |
| **Wheel-on** | Rear wheel remains; tyre drives a resistance roller | Lower-friction entry and easier storage for some riders | Axle fit, tyre and roller setup, calibration, noise and controllability |

Direct drive is the practical default for frequent structured work because it removes rear-tyre wear and roller tension from the setup. It does not guarantee +/-1% accuracy, silence or calibration-free operation. The current direct-drive models in Roadman's guide carry manufacturer claims from <1% to +/-2%, and their drivetrain bundles differ materially.

Wheel-on trainers can still suit occasional riding, limited storage or a tighter budget. Their setup depends on tyre pressure and roller tension, so follow the exact manufacturer's installation and calibration guidance rather than applying a universal routine.

**What to look for:** frame and axle fit first; then cassette, freehub or virtual-shifting compatibility; then the app feature and connection method. Bluetooth or ANT+ alone does not prove that resistance control, virtual shifting or steering works in every platform.

## Pain Cave Setup

Your indoor setup needs to make you want to ride, not dread it. The riders who stay consistent indoors have optimised their environment.

**The essentials:**
- **Fan.** A powerful floor fan pointed at your upper body. Non-negotiable. Without airflow, your core temperature rises, power drops, and sessions become miserable. A large industrial fan is better than two small desk fans.
- **Mat.** Protects the floor from sweat and dampens vibration. A dedicated trainer mat or thick rubber gym mat works.
- **Screen.** Big enough to see from the saddle. A TV or monitor at eye level is ideal — looking down at a phone on the handlebars puts strain on your neck over a 90-minute session.
- **Towel.** Draped over the bars to catch sweat. Your stem and headset will thank you.
- **Hydration.** Two bottles minimum for any session over 45 minutes. You lose more fluid indoors because there's no wind cooling you.

**The upgrades that matter:**
- **Rocker plate.** Adds lateral movement so the bike doesn't feel locked in place. Reduces lower back fatigue on longer sessions.
- **Dedicated bike.** If you have the budget and space, a second bike that lives on the trainer means zero setup time. The fewer barriers between you and riding, the more you'll ride.

## Winter Training Structure

Winter is where next season's form is built. Most amateurs waste winter doing random Zwift races at full gas, accumulating fatigue without purpose. When spring arrives, they're tired and their base is no bigger than it was in October.

**A structured winter block (12-16 weeks) looks like this:**

**Weeks 1-4: Base building.** High volume of Zone 2 work. Indoor sessions of 60-90 minutes at conversation pace, supplemented with longer outdoor rides when weather and daylight allow. This is boring. It's also essential.

**Weeks 5-8: Base plus sweet spot.** Introduce 2 sessions per week of sweet spot work (88-93% FTP). These intervals build aerobic capacity with manageable fatigue. The base keeps expanding while the intensity starts sharpening your engine.

**Weeks 9-12: Build phase.** Shift to threshold and VO2max intervals. Two quality sessions per week: one threshold (2x20 at 95-100% FTP), one VO2max (4x4 at 106-120% FTP). Easy rides stay easy.

**Weeks 13-16: Specialisation.** Tailor sessions to your target events. Criterium racing? Add short, sharp efforts. Sportives? Extend threshold intervals to 30-minute blocks. Time trialling? Practise sustained power at your target watts.

Joe Friel's periodisation framework underpins this approach. As he emphasises, structuring your training year into distinct phases — rather than doing a bit of everything all the time — is the single most overlooked element of amateur cycling.

## Mental Strategies for Indoor Riding

The physical work is only half the battle. Staying mentally engaged on a stationary bike for 60-90 minutes requires strategy, not willpower.

**Break sessions into blocks.** A 90-minute ride feels endless. Nine 10-minute blocks feel manageable. Focus on completing the current block, not the whole session.

**Use structured workouts, not free rides.** When the screen tells you what to do for the next interval, your brain has a task. Free riding gives your brain permission to think about quitting.

**Entertainment is legitimate.** Podcasts, music, Netflix — whatever keeps you on the bike is valid. The riders who treat indoor training like a monastic experience burn out fastest. Put a show on. Nobody's judging you.

**Virtual racing for motivation.** Zwift races and group rides add external pressure that makes you push harder than you would alone. The power numbers riders produce in Zwift races consistently exceed their solo efforts. Schedule one race per week as your "fun" session.

**Reward completion.** Coffee after the ride. A good breakfast. Something you look forward to. Associate finishing the session with a positive outcome and the habit builds itself.

**Connect with others.** Inside Not Done Yet on [Skool](https://www.skool.com/roadmancycling), members share their indoor sessions, swap trainer tips, and keep each other accountable through the dark months. Having a community that understands why you're sweating in a spare room at 6am makes a difference.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is indoor training as effective as outdoor riding?**
For structured interval work, indoor training is often more effective because you control every variable. There's no coasting, no junctions, no wind — just sustained effort at the prescribed intensity. For endurance rides, outdoor riding has the edge: longer durations, varied terrain, and the bike-handling skills that only come from real roads. The best approach is both — indoor for precision, outdoor for volume and skill.

**Should I use Zwift or TrainerRoad?**
It depends on what keeps you consistent. Zwift suits riders who need social motivation, variety, and the thrill of virtual racing. TrainerRoad suits riders who want structured, AI-driven training plans with minimal distraction. Many cyclists use both — TrainerRoad for weekday intervals, Zwift for weekend group rides. The right platform is the one you'll actually use.

**What's the best smart trainer to buy?**
Roadman's current best-for-most pick is Wahoo KICKR CORE 2, but only after checking the exact frame, axle, drivetrain and application. Tacx NEO 3M, Elite Justo 2, Wahoo KICKR v6, Elite Avanti and JetBlack VICTORY solve different premium, movement, connectivity and virtual-shifting needs. **How do I stay motivated on the turbo trainer?**
Structure beats motivation. Use a training plan so each session has a purpose. Break long rides into short mental blocks. Use entertainment — music, podcasts, TV shows — without guilt. Schedule one Zwift race per week for the competitive stimulus. And connect with other indoor riders in a community like Not Done Yet, where accountability keeps you showing up on the days you'd rather not.
