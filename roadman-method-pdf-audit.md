# The Roadman Method — PDF Resource Editorial Audit

**Date:** 11 May 2026
**Auditor scope:** 46 PDF resources across 12 modules
**Benchmark:** 9.5/10 Rouleur magazine editorial standard
**Voice reference:** Anthony Walsh Voice Guide + Knowledge Base

---

## OVERALL SCORE: 8.8 / 10

## VERDICT: NEEDS REVISION

The resources are structurally excellent — well-organised, cycling-specific, grounded in named experts and real methodology. The system design is first-class. But the editorial voice falls short of the 9.5 Rouleur benchmark in several places. The core issues are: (1) flat boilerplate introductory copy on template PDFs, (2) one third-person reference to Anthony that breaks the first-person contract, (3) some PDFs with zero voice framing at all, (4) a duplicated quote within the same module, and (5) scattered productivity-bro language that doesn't match Anthony's spoken voice.

None of these are catastrophic. Most are fixable in a single editing pass. The best resources — Module 11's Decision Tree Cards, Module 9's Torque Cards, Module 5's Recovery Week guide — are genuine 10/10 and show what the standard should be across the entire set.

## MODULE-BY-MODULE SCORES

### Module 1: Where You Actually Are — Score: 8.5/10

**Files:** Entry Assessment Workbook, Training Audit Worksheet, Weekly Hours Mapping, One-Two-Twelve

**What works:**
The Training Audit Worksheet is strong — the Green/Yellow/Red zone system is simple, cycling-specific, and the diagnosis line ("If your yellow zone is bigger than your green zone, you do not need more training. You need different training.") is peak Anthony. Direct. No hedging. The One-Two-Twelve goal-setting framework is tight, and the Sanity Check questions ("Does it depend only on me, not race results?") feel like something Anthony would actually say across the table.

**Flags:**

**FLAG 1 — Duplicated quote across two PDFs in the same module**
The Dan Lorang quote ("What people try in general is they get a training plan and try to fit it in somehow and then it just ends up in pure stress") appears in BOTH the Entry Assessment Workbook AND the Weekly Hours Mapping Template. Using the same quote twice in the same week's materials looks automated, not curated. A Rouleur-quality publication would never repeat a pull quote within the same issue.

*Fix:* Keep the Lorang quote on the Entry Assessment (where it's most relevant as a scene-setter). Replace the Weekly Hours Mapping quote with something time-specific, e.g.:
> "I'm going to begin with frequency. All I care about is you get out the door a certain number of times a week."
> — Professor Stephen Seiler — Roadman Cycling Podcast

This quote is already used in Module 2, but it's more relevant here where the user is literally mapping their available hours.

**FLAG 2 — Entry Assessment intro text is slightly textbook**
Current: "This workbook is the foundation of your next 12 weeks. Every coaching decision depends on a real picture of where you are right now. Complete each section honestly. You will return to these numbers in Week 12."

This is functional and clear, but it reads like course documentation rather than Anthony talking to you. It's missing the warmth.

*Suggested rewrite:* "Before we change anything, we need to know where you actually are. Not where you think you are — where the numbers say you are. Be honest with yourself here. You'll come back to these exact numbers in Week 12, and the gap between then and now is going to tell the real story."

### Module 2: Training Architecture — Score: 8.5/10

**Files:** Architecture 6hr, 8hr, 10hr, 12hr, Session Library

**What works:**
The Session Library is excellent. Expert attribution done right ("Sessions drawn from Dan Lorang, Professor Seiler, John Wakefield, and Derek Teel"). The torque session quote at the bottom is perfectly placed. The architecture rules are punchy and practical — "Rule 4: Protect the total. If life compresses the week, cut the short Z2 rides, never the long ride" sounds exactly like something Anthony would say on the podcast.

**Flags:**

**FLAG 3 — Identical boilerplate intro across all four architecture templates**
Current (repeated verbatim 4 times): "This template is built for X available hours per week, following the polarised model from Professor Seiler's research."

This is functional but flat. It reads like a generated description, not Anthony introducing a tool he's giving you. The Seiler quote is also identical across all four, which compounds the templated feel.

*Suggested rewrite (vary slightly per template):*

6hr: "Six hours a week. That's what you've got. Here's the thing — that's enough. This is the architecture that makes those six hours count, built on Professor Seiler's polarised model."

8hr: "Eight hours gives you room to breathe. You've got a proper long ride, a quality session, and enough easy volume to actually build an aerobic base. Here's how to lay it out."

10hr: "Ten hours a week is where things start to look like a proper training programme. You've got frequency, you've got a long ride with teeth, and you've got space for two quality days when we get there."

12hr: "Twelve hours. You're serious. This is the template that lets you use all of it without burning out by Week 6."

**FLAG 4 — Blank page 2 on all four architecture PDFs**
Every architecture template has a completely blank second page (just header/footer). This is a generation bug, not a design choice. It looks unfinished.

*Fix:* Either remove page 2 entirely, or use it for "My Weekly Notes" or a session log table.

### Module 3: Fuelling the Engine — Score: 8.5/10

**Files:** Daily Fuelling Framework, In-Ride Nutrition Calculator, Race Week Nutrition, Performance Grocery Guide

**What works:**
The Daily Fuelling Framework opens with the right Anthony quote about calories in vs. calories out. The Grocery Guide's intro line ("Stock these and you will never have to think about what to eat on a training day") is direct, practical, no waste. The In-Ride Calculator is genuinely useful — carbs/hour by session type with specific example sources.

**Flags:**

**FLAG 5 — Third-person reference to Anthony (CRITICAL)**
Current (Daily Fuelling Framework): "Built around the principle Anthony followed when he lost 7kg in 12 weeks while eating more food."

This is a voice-breaking error. Anthony doesn't refer to himself in the third person in his own course materials. The entire Roadman voice contract is first-person or direct address. This reads like someone else wrote it about him.

*Suggested rewrite:* "I lost 7kg in 12 weeks. I went from 86 to 79kg. And I did it while eating more food than I've ever eaten in my life. This framework is built on the exact approach I followed."

**FLAG 6 — "protocols discussed with World Tour nutritionists" is too formal**
Current (In-Ride Nutrition Calculator): "In-ride fuelling targets by session type, based on protocols discussed with World Tour nutritionists on the podcast."

"Protocols discussed with" is corporate language. Anthony wouldn't say this.

*Suggested rewrite:* "In-ride fuelling targets by session type — the exact numbers the World Tour nutritionists gave us on the podcast."

**FLAG 7 — Race Week Nutrition has almost no voice**
Current intro: "Seven-day race week nutrition plan."

That's it. Five words. No context, no framing, no Anthony. Every other module has at least a sentence of scene-setting.

*Suggested rewrite:* "Race week is where most people panic and change everything. Don't. This is a seven-day countdown that keeps it simple — normal nutrition, then a controlled carb load from Thursday."

### Module 4: Strength & Conditioning — Score: 9.5/10

**Files:** S&C Manual, Foundation Circuit Card, Exercise Demos, S&C Integration Calendar

**What works:**
This module is strong throughout. The Derek Teel quote is perfectly chosen and attributed casually, not formally. The S&C Manual intro is punchy: "Three-phase cycling-specific S&C programme. Dumbbell-led, bodyweight-supported, two 30-minute sessions per week. No heavy barbell movements." That last sentence addresses the audience's fear directly. The Exercise Demo cues are clear and practical — "How / Avoid" format is exactly what you'd want pinned on a gym wall. The Integration Calendar's closing Teel quote about cutting 60 minutes of riding for two strength sessions is a great sell.

**No flags.** This module meets the 9.5 standard.

### Module 5: Recovery — Score: 9.0/10

**Files:** Sleep Optimisation Checklist, Daily Readiness Score, Recovery Week Guide, HRV Quick Guide

**What works:**
The Recovery Week Guide is exceptional. "Volume drops 40-50%. No intensity. Sleep more. Eat normally. Enjoy it." Five sentences. Each one lands. This is Anthony at his best — permission to rest, delivered without apology. The Daily Readiness Score's traffic-light decision system (Green/Amber/Red) is clean and actionable. The HRV Guide correctly positions HRV as "a readiness metric, not a fitness metric" — this is a distinction Anthony makes on the podcast.

**Flags:**

**FLAG 8 — "highest-leverage recovery tool" is productivity-bro language**
Current (Sleep Optimisation): "Sleep is the single highest-leverage recovery tool you have."

"Highest-leverage" is Silicon Valley speak. You'd hear it from Andrew Huberman, not from Anthony Walsh at a coffee stop.

*Suggested rewrite:* "Sleep is the single biggest thing you can do for recovery. Nothing else comes close."

The Pacing Cards are excellent reference tools — the Wiggins quote is well-placed, and the Sportive/Gran Fondo pacing advice ("First third: Ride 10% easier than you think you should") is classic Anthony practical wisdom. The Goal Recalibration template's question structure ("What has worked? What hasn't? Do I need to recalibrate?") is honest and non-judgemental.

### Module 7: Periodisation — Score: 9.0/10

**Files:** Season Planning Template, Mesocycle Builder, Phase Cheat Sheet

**What works:**
The Season Planning Template's Anthony quote ("they periodise everything. Nothing is random.") is well-chosen. The three-step structure (Target Events → Work Backward → Map the Year) is clear. The Mesocycle Builder's Dan Lorang quote and 3:1 loading pattern are correctly presented.

**Flags:**

**FLAG 9 — Phase Cheat Sheet has zero voice framing**
The Phase Cheat Sheet is just a bare comparison table (Base/Build/Peak) with no introductory text at all. No quote, no context, no single sentence explaining what the reader is looking at or why it matters. It's a reference card, yes, but even reference cards need a one-line frame.

*Suggested addition:* "Three phases. Each one has a different job. Know which phase you're in and train accordingly — don't mix them up." Or use a quote from Joe Friel or Dan Lorang on periodisation.

### Module 8: Body Composition — Score: 8.5/10

**Files:** Body Composition Tracking, Performance Plate Guide, Race Weight Timeline

**What works:**
Body Comp Tracking is strong — the Anthony quote about losing 7kg while eating more is perfectly placed, and the rules at the bottom are specific and responsible: "Deficit on easy days only (200-300 kcal). Protein floor 1.8-2.2g/kg/day. Track weekly average, not daily." This is the anti-crash-diet positioning that defines Anthony's nutrition stance. The Race Weight Timeline's three-phase approach (Active Deficit → Maintenance → Pre-Event) with the hard rule "Body comp work stops 3 weeks before your A event" is excellent coaching.

**Flags:**

**FLAG 10 — Performance Plate Guide has ZERO voice text**
The Performance Plate Guide contains two tables (Quality Training Day Plate and Easy/Rest Day Plate) with zero introductory text, zero quotes, zero framing. It's the only resource across all 46 PDFs that has literally no written voice at all. For a paid coaching product at Rouleur standard, this is a gap.

*Suggested addition:* "Two plates. One for hard days, one for easy days. The protein stays the same — the carbs move. That's the entire system." Or use a relevant quote from Dr. David Dunne.

### Module 11: Self-Coaching — Score: 10/10

**Files:** Weekly Self-Coaching Review, Decision Tree Cards, Next-Phase Mesocycle Planner, Framework Summary

**What works:**
The Decision Tree Cards are the single best-written resource in the entire 46-PDF set. Every scenario is real. Every answer is specific. "Quality session? Do not double up. Move on." "Easy ride? Let it go." "One bad day? 30 min, no data, just ride." "Bad week? Review your primary goal." The Illness protocol uses the neck rule correctly. The Travel Week adaptations are practical ("3-5 days: hotel gym for S&C; One bodyweight session replaces bike"). The Work Stress section: "Whole week? 3 sessions, all Z2. Protect sleep." This is what self-coaching material should feel like — calm, specific, no panic.

The Framework Summary is the capstone and it delivers. "The System in 10 Sentences" is clean, memorable, and closes with: "You're not done yet." Perfect.

**No flags.** This is the gold standard for the entire course.

### Module 12: Not Done Yet (Graduation) — Score: 9.0/10

**Files:** Exit Assessment Workbook, 12-Week Progress Report, 90-Day Forward Plan, Resource Library

**What works:**
The 90-Day Forward Plan opens with "The Roadman Method is a start line, not a finish line." This is the right framing — it sets up ongoing engagement with the community. The Exit Assessment mirrors Week 1 perfectly ("Same six domains. Same conditions."), which is structurally satisfying. The Progress Report's five-pillar reflection format is clean. The Resource Library is a useful index with expert sources.

**Flags:**

**FLAG 11 — Resource Library lists "Alistair Brownlee" as an expert source**
The Resource Library's expert table includes "Alistair Brownlee — Endurance training, cross-discipline." Brownlee does not appear in the Roadman Cycling Knowledge Base and is not referenced as a podcast guest in any of the source materials I've reviewed. If he hasn't been on the podcast, this is a factual accuracy issue that undermines the "I asked the people who actually coach the best riders" credibility model. Every expert listed should be someone Anthony has actually interviewed.

*Fix:* Verify whether Alistair Brownlee has been a podcast guest. If yes, keep. If no, remove and replace with someone from the actual expert network (e.g., Tim Kerrison, Rosa Kloser, or Joe Friel — all of whom appear in the knowledge base).

1. **Fix the two High-severity flags first** — the third-person Anthony reference in 03-daily-fuelling and the voiceless 08-performance-plate. These are the only two that would genuinely make a paying member pause.

2. **Add one-line voice framing** to 03-race-week-nutrition and 07-phase-cheat-sheet. These don't need much — a single sentence that sounds like Anthony setting context.

3. **De-duplicate the Lorang quote** in Module 1. Use a different, time-relevant quote on the Weekly Hours Mapping.

4. **Vary the architecture template intros** across the four hour-volume versions. Even a sentence of variation removes the templated feel.

5. **Fix the blank page 2** on all four architecture PDFs — either remove or repurpose.

6. **Swap "highest-leverage"** for natural language in 05-sleep-optimisation.

7. **Verify Alistair Brownlee** as a podcast guest before the resource library ships.

8. **Use Module 11 as the editorial benchmark** when revising everything else. If a line wouldn't sound right in the Decision Tree Cards, it doesn't belong in a Roadman resource.
